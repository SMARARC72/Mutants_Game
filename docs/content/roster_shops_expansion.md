## How to read this file

Three bodies of net-new content, written to *extend* wave 1 (`economy_items_rivals.md` + `factions_npcs.md` + `regional_cast.md`), never to redo it.

- **Part A — More Named Rivals (12).** Authored nemeses beyond the original twelve. Each: **name, personality, goal, grid-alignment** (the 9-god grid), **faction**, **signature team** (creatures by real Codex name + force/role), a **3-act escalation**, and a **Succession angle** binding to a **UNIQUE DG-###** not used by the existing twelve.
- **Part B — More Named Characters (24).** Legends & historical figures (dead champions, founders, infamous tamers), wandering notables, and recurring minor characters — each a line of who-they-are + voice + why they matter. No name reuses the wave-1 roster.
- **Part C — More Shops & Vendors (12).** Per-region counters, traveling carts, and black-market specialists, each with a themed inventory drawn from the existing item catalog + a few new themed items, priced in ₯/✶/◈ respecting the economy's sinks/sources.

> **Currency key.** **Drachma** (₯) = mortal coin, generous, for consumables & common gear. **Essence** (✶) = distilled souls, scarce & load-bearing (Lab/breeding/rituals/ascension). **Ichor** (◈) = rare divine, off gods/legendaries, never sold for Drachma. *Renown* + *Corruption* gate items but aren't priced. **Black market** charges an ✶/◈ premium + **heat/notoriety**.

> **Force ↔ stat key.** Gaia→Bulk · Ouranos→Celerity · Cosmos→Ward · Chaos→Spike · Eros→Vitality · Thanatos→Bane (+ universals Luck, Focus). Opposed pairs (taboo to fuse): Cosmos⇄Chaos · Eros⇄Thanatos · Gaia⇄Ouranos.

> **DG-### uniqueness (per `_CANON_RATIFICATIONS.md` #5).** The existing twelve already bind DG-001, 003, 005, 007, 010, 011, 012, 015, 017, 019, 022, 023, 024 (and *command*, via the Refused, 002/004/018). **This file's twelve bind only free Book05 slots (DG-006, 008, 009, 013, 014, 016, 020, 021) and newly-minted slots (DG-025…DG-028)** — no overlap with the existing twelve, no overlap with each other.

# PART A — MORE NAMED RIVALS / NEMESES (12)

Twelve more authored god-aspirants (`Mutants_Game_Bosses_Rivals.md` nemesis system), filling the corners the first twelve left thin: a second foil per grid cell where the world needs density, plus the faction-specific recurring nemeses (the Stoneblooded exile, the Deep Choir drowned-rival, the Bloomwarden-gone-wrong). Outcomes stay **sticky** — beat one and it returns obsessed; spare one and it may turn ally; humiliate one and it defects and hunts you.

> Team notation: `Creature (Force · role)`, all drawn from `Creature_Codex_*`. Tiers escalate across acts; Act-3 teams reach legendary/god support where noted. Each rival's Succession form binds a **unique DG-###**.

### B13. Veris Calx — "The Codifier"
*Faction: The Concord · Grid: **Order / Pure** (the Lawgiver road — Cassia Vane's harder, younger shadow)*

**Personality.** Where Cassia Vane is exhausted mercy, Veris is *fresh* zeal — a prodigy who read every covenant by twelve and decided the gods failed because the *language* was sloppy. He doesn't audit you; he *redrafts* you, correcting your grammar of ambition mid-sentence. Insufferably precise, secretly thrilled by a worthy clause.
**Goal.** Author the single perfect Law that, once enacted, makes tyranny *literally unspeakable* — a covenant so airtight no future god can wriggle. He means to ascend only to *sign* it.
**Signature team.** Platewarden (Cosmos/Gaia · controller), Nullscute (Cosmos/Thanatos · support), Cathedral-Chiton (Cosmos/Gaia · control-tank), Sigilframe (Cosmos/Gaia · sentry, construct). Act-3 anchor: **Sanctwall Colossus** (Cosmos/Gaia · siege-colossus, construct) sealed under a god-organ Ward.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** challenges you to a "clause-duel" at the Astral Tier — a lawful ladder-match where he narrates the *rule* you're breaking as he wins by it. **Act 2:** if you've run any unsealed taboo op, he serves you an *amended* writ — the offense plus your *future* offenses, pre-drafted. **Act 3:** a Ward-lock gauntlet where you must break his interlocking seals before the Law finishes ratifying you into stillness.
**Succession angle.** **High.** Order/Pure. If Veris ascends he snapshots as **The Cathedral That Walked Away a Winner (DG-021)** — a fortress-boss that "fights back by simply existing correctly," serene and faintly disappointed in your siege. Spare him at low corruption and he certifies your lineage with a fussy, genuine pride; humiliate his draft and he hardens toward the Iron Throne and re-files you as a *case*.

### B14. The Choir-Widow Nerin Saltvow — "The Drowned Bride"
*Faction: The Deep Choir · Grid: **Balanced / Corrupt** (the Plaguelord shade — Vael Mourncoil's warmer, sadder echo)*

**Personality.** Nerin went under to bring her drowned husband *up* and came back married to the *whole deep* instead. She speaks in the plural-of-grief, gentle and dripping, and she wants you to come down not to harm you but because *everyone she loves is already there* and she's lonely in the shape of an ocean. The kindest rival who is also a tide.
**Goal.** Reunion-by-drowning — sink enough of the world's grief into the Tideless that the deep surfaces as one great remembered god, and no one she loves is ever lost again (because nothing down there is ever *gone*, only *kept*).
**Signature team.** Reefdirge (Ouranos/Thanatos · striker), Brineherald (Ouranos/Thanatos · nuker), Fathompsalm (Ouranos/Thanatos · controller), Geyserfin (Cosmos/Ouranos · control-tank). Act-3 anchor: **Choirstone Galechorus** (Gaia/Ouranos · nuker, abomination) — a leviathan-chord she *called*, not caught.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a half-sung challenge in the Shallows — control-heavy, drowning your tempo, weeping the whole time. **Act 2:** she sends *bottles* — battles framed as letters, each one asking you to "stay for dinner" (the Choir's dinner-where-nobody-eats); winning only convinces her you're "almost ready to come down." **Act 3:** the Kelp-Cathedral set-piece, a hymn-prison where the sea decides initiative and every turn you lose a little surface.
**Succession angle.** **Medium.** Balanced/Corrupt. If Nerin ascends she binds **The Tender of the Ordered Garden (DG-009)** *re-read as a drowned cloister* — the Book05 durability ceiling, a support-bastion that "out-lives" rather than attacks, humming behind its sigils while you chip at a wall healing faster than you're scared. Befriend-route players can talk her *back* one verse at a time; she becomes the Choir's gentlest deep-recipe keeper, still half-ocean, finally less lonely.

### B15. Harrow Sceln — "The Clanbreaker"
*Faction: started Stoneblooded, exiled toward the Iron Guild · Grid: drifts **Balanced/Pure → Order/Tainted** (Warden curdling to Architect)*

**Personality.** Cast out of the Stoneblooded for the same sin as Driftblood (using the Lab), but where Driftblood stayed bitter-clanless, Harrow got *ambitious*. He took the clans' patience and the Guild's heresy and welded them into something colder than either: a man who breeds a true bloodline *and then renders it for parts* the moment it stops improving. Quiet, courteous, unforgivable to the clans precisely because he was *good* at the slow way and chose the fast one anyway.
**Goal.** Prove endurance and engineering aren't opposites — build a *self-improving lineage-engine*, organic stock fed into a construct chassis, a god that breeds *and* manufactures itself. The clans called it blasphemy; Harrow calls it the obvious synthesis they were too sentimental to reach.
**Signature team.** Tundramound (Gaia/Eros · fortress), Hoarwool (Gaia/Eros · bruiser), Wardengine Mk.II (Gaia/Cosmos · war-frame, construct), Cogframe Mk.I (Gaia/Cosmos · sentry, construct). Act-3 anchor: **Quickening Colossus** (Eros/Gaia · siege-colossus, construct) grown from his own rendered bloodline — husbandry poured into a chassis.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a Breeding-Show rivalry where his entry is *flawless* and then, win or lose, he walks it straight to the rendering pits, smiling, to fund the next one. **Act 2:** if you favor the slow honest road, he counters every true lineage you raise with a *better* one he'll happily melt; Ostrega's clans turn cold toward *you* if you're seen trading with him. **Act 3:** the lineage-engine reveal at the Forgefell/Titanfall seam — an organic-construct hybrid that breeds reinforcements mid-fight.
**Succession angle.** **High.** His drift lands Order/Tainted (the Architect's neighbor) by way of the Warden he abandoned. If Harrow ascends he mints **DG-025 — The Husbanded Engine, Self-Sown** (newly-minted; Construct-apex flag, Gaia/Eros): a foundry-class Dead God that *breeds its own reinforcements* between runs, returning each NG+ one true-bred generation stronger. The hardest rival to *out-grind*; the Stoneblooded will fund your fight against him purely out of spite, and Driftblood will, grudgingly, *cheer*.

### B16. Mother Cinv-the-Glut — "The Overgrowth"
*Faction: the Bloomwardens, fallen toward the Revel/Devourer · Grid: drifts **Chaos/Pure → Chaos/Corrupt** (Free Wild rotting to Devourer)*

**Personality.** Once a Bloomwarden hospice-sister who loved too many dying things and broke the wrong way: instead of grieving the doomed, she decided *nothing should ever stop growing*, and now she loves the world the way a cancer loves a body. Warm, maternal, monstrous — she'll cradle your fallen creature and *make it bloom again*, wronger, fonder, *more*. The Bloomwardens' nightmare of what their mercy becomes without its grief.
**Goal.** Let the Glut eat everything — not for ruin (she's not the Unbound), but out of *love*: a world entirely alive, entirely fed, entirely *hers*, where death is the one indignity she's abolished by simply never letting anything finish dying.
**Signature team.** Greenwatcher (Eros/Thanatos · regenerator), Canopywake (Eros/Thanatos · healer), Crone-of-the-Bog (Eros/Chaos · regenerator), Quagsovereign (Chaos/Eros · nuker). Act-3 anchor: **Verdant Gravechoir** (Eros/Thanatos · bruiser, abomination) — a chorus of everything she "saved" from death, blooming and grateful and wrong.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a gentle Breeding-Show meeting where her entries are *unsettlingly* lush — too alive, faintly grateful, slightly more than were planted. **Act 2:** if you've ever let a creature die clean (or burned one at Cinda's pyre), she takes it as an *insult to life* and fields a regeneration-war meant to "show you death was never necessary." **Act 3:** the deep-Glut set-piece — an opponent you cannot out-damage *or* out-patience, because every fallen thing on the field she re-blooms onto her side.
**Succession angle.** **Medium-high.** Free Wild souring to Devourer. If Cinva ascends she binds **The Worldmother, Still Blooming (DG-019)**'s *Chaos-Corrupt twin* by way of a newly-minted **DG-026 — The Glut That Forgave Death** (Eros/Gaia, Devourer-shade): a healing-monster Succession boss that "heals faster than invaders can kill" *and* turns your dead against you. The single hardest rival to permanently end; Saoirse will weep to fight her former sister, and *spare-and-redeem* is nearly impossible — Cinva counts your mercy as proof she was right.

### B17. The Tallyman Ors Veck — "The Repossessor"
*Faction: The Pale Court · Grid: **Order / Corrupt** (the Iron Throne — Brother Quill's colder, ledger-first cousin)*

**Personality.** Where Brother Quill apologizes to every creature he harvests, Ors Veck has never apologized for anything and considers the impulse a *clerical inefficiency*. He is the Court's finest debt-enforcer, promoted past mourning entirely: to him a soul is a line item, a grief is an unpaid invoice, and *you* are a maturing asset he's already pencilled a withdrawal date against. Soft-spoken, itemized, terrifying because he's *fair*.
**Goal.** Foreclose on the whole Succession — buy up enough soul-debt that when the thrones refill, the Court *holds the paper* on every new god, and Ors Veck is the registrar who can call the loan. Not to rule. To *own the lien on* rule.
**Signature team.** Veldtmourn (Thanatos/Gaia · assassin), Sungallows (Thanatos/Gaia · drainer), Knellworks Engine (Thanatos/Cosmos · war-frame, construct), Tollframe Cur (Thanatos/Cosmos · sentry, construct). Act-3 anchor: **Reliquary Colossus** (Thanatos/Cosmos · siege-colossus, construct) — a walking vault of repossessed souls, your *own* lost creatures audible inside if you've defaulted near him.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** doesn't fight — *serves* you. A polite notice that a creature you lost is "Court collateral pending settlement," then a duel to "establish standing." **Act 2:** every reanimation you take from anyone accrues to *his* ledger; he arrives, cart and condolences, to collect creatures you thought were yours. **Act 3:** the foreclosure set-piece — an attrition war where every default you've ever signed comes due at once, his Reliquary Colossus disgorging your repossessed dead.
**Succession angle.** **High.** Order/Corrupt. If Ors Veck ascends he binds **The Sexton-King of the Deep Loam (DG-006)** *re-read as an accountant* — Thanatos/Gaia, an attrition-tank that "drains Bane each turn and banks it in its own buried mass; the longer it stands, the heavier it gets." Funerals charged by the hour. Cannot be befriended (sentiment is a line item he wrote off centuries ago); *can* be out-paid — clear every debt you owe the Court and he closes your file with a single, chilling *"settled"* and never troubles you again.

### B18. Quint Galefellow — "The Squall"
*Faction: The High Table · Grid: **Balanced**, leaning **Ouranos/Chaos** (a Broker's golden boy with a gambler's heart)*

**Personality.** The Table's favorite action: a charming, reckless sky-duelist who treats the whole Succession as the longest, best bet ever opened and intends to *be* the marquee event, not book it. Where Dolos brokers from the shadows, Quint *plays the table loud* — fast, generous, already three contracts and one near-death ahead of you. Everyone's friend; no one's safe wager.
**Goal.** Win the biggest line in High Table history — *on himself*. He doesn't want a throne so much as he wants the *whole house* betting against his ascension and then to *collect*, personally, gloriously, on the longest odds ever set.
**Signature team.** Khamsoon (Ouranos/Chaos · nuker), Siroccoub (Ouranos/Chaos · striker), Sandskip (Ouranos/Chaos · skirmisher), Constellute (Ouranos/Eros · nuker). Act-3 anchor: **The Squall That Kept the Crown** as a *borrowed* mercenary — a Dead God he rented on a marker, fielded before it repossesses *him*.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** challenges you to a friendly sky-duel "for the line" at the Storm Vault, fights gracious, loses with a grin or wins with a bow, splits the purse either way. **Act 2:** the Table keeps *pairing* you — every bounty board has both your names; humiliate him and he doubles down, borrowing wilder mercenaries to chase the rematch. **Act 3:** the altitude set-piece where the sky decides initiative and he opens every exchange first, betting the fight on tempo.
**Succession angle.** **Medium.** Balanced-leaning-Ouranos/Chaos. If Quint ascends (by *winning his own bet*, which the Table finds delicious) he binds **The Squall That Kept the Crown (DG-008)** — Ouranos/Chaos, a fast striker whose **First-Strike Sovereign** "opens every exchange with a blinding pre-emptive blow… it ruled by going first; it still does," gracious in a victory it would dearly love a real fight to interrupt. The most *fun* invasion boss after Pyx; spare-and-befriend turns Quint into the rival who *bankrolls* your wildest runs, purely because a friend who ascends is the best long-shot he's ever held.

### B19. The Thunderfool Errant, Bram Ozzle — "The Accident"
*Faction: the Unbound / unaligned · Grid: **Chaos / Tainted** (the Reveler shade — Pyx's dumber, luckier little brother)*

**Personality.** Bram has no business being a rival and everyone, including Bram, knows it. He's a grinning hazard with a knack he doesn't understand, a creature he caught by *falling on it*, and a winning streak that statisticians at the High Table have stopped trying to explain. He's not malicious, ambitious, or even particularly *awake* to the stakes — he just keeps *accidentally* doing the impossible and beaming about it. The funny-grim sting: he might ascend purely by *luck*, and the cosmos doesn't seem to mind.
**Goal.** None, really. Bram wants to "see what happens," buy everyone a round, and not die — in roughly that order. The horror is that *no goal at all* is no obstacle whatsoever to the Succession, which has always rewarded survival over merit.
**Signature team.** Bograttle (Chaos/Eros · nuker), Floeling (Chaos/Eros · striker), Slubcoil (Chaos/Eros · striker), Avalanche-Heir (Chaos/Eros · nuker). Act-3 anchor: a creature he *fused by accident* and cannot replicate, one-rolls-it-all-or-fumbles, exactly like its owner.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** stumbles into a Lab-Craft Contest he didn't sign up for, fields something illegal he made wrong, wins on a Luck roll that makes Sober Tem put down his pen. **Act 2:** keeps *finding* you, region to region, never on purpose, always with a worse haircut and a better team; every win baffles him more. **Act 3:** a fight that scrambles harder the longer it goes, his gambits one-rolling the formation in directions *he* can't predict either.
**Succession angle.** **Medium.** Chaos/Tainted. If Bram ascends — and the game *lets* him, because luck is a force and Luck is a stat — he binds **The Thunderfool Who Won Anyway (DG-022)**'s sibling slot via newly-minted **DG-027 — The Stumblecrown, Beloved by Dice** (Chaos/Ouranos, Gambit-class): a Succession boss who "had no business winning… and won the whole run anyway," a bolt with a personality striking wherever it's looking. The *only* rival you can lose to and feel insulted rather than outmatched. Befriend him and he becomes the ally who fails upward into saving your run at the worst possible moment.

### B20. Archpriest Solmen Vexx — "The Dawnsung"
*Faction: The Bloomwardens, hardening toward the Concord · Grid: **Chaos/Pure → Order/Pure** (Free Wild calcifying toward Lawgiver)*

**Personality.** A beloved Bloomwarden healer so adored, so *swift* with mercy, that the love went to his head: he decided the soft creed fails because it's *disorganized*, and began *regimenting* kindness — mercy on a schedule, healing by writ, compassion enforced. He's still warm, still beloved, still genuinely good at the saving; he's just started to believe gentleness needs a *law*, and that he should be the one to write it. Saoirse's grief is that her sweetest student is becoming Velleth.
**Goal.** Codify mercy into the next pantheon — a *lawful* gentleness, gardens governed, healing mandated, no creature ever again left to the Glut's appetite because the *rules* won't allow it. He'd ascend to *legislate* kindness.
**Signature team.** Canyonsoar (Eros/Ouranos · regenerator), Thermalwing (Eros/Ouranos · healer), Updraftling (Eros/Ouranos · support), Constellfox (Eros/Gaia · healer). Act-3 anchor: **Dawnlit-Frame "Quickening" (DG-017)**-styled support that out-speeds your turns to mend its own — except his binds elsewhere (below).
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a Breeding-Show / Mend-rivalry where he out-heals and out-*organizes* you, sweetly correcting your "inefficient compassion." **Act 2:** if you favor wild mercy (befriend-heavy, ungoverned), he frowns and fields a regimented heal-war designed to *outlast* your sentiment with system. **Act 3:** the heart-grove set-piece — a fast support-bastion that mends faster than you can act, humming a hymn with a *rulebook*.
**Succession angle.** **Medium-high.** Drift lands him Order/Pure-adjacent. If Solmen ascends he binds **The Dawnrunner, Beloved and Gone (DG-014)** — Ouranos/Eros, a fast support whose **Reveille of the Risen** "grants its side initiative and evasion and mends its most wounded… being beaten by something this nice should be illegal; it is merely fatal." The pantheon's *favourite*, which is the cruelty. *Preventable* (keep him wild — befriend beside him, never regiment) or *redeemable* early (remind him mercy that needs a law isn't mercy); left to harden, he becomes the kindest, most *fatal* wall in the book.

### B21. Drubb Gravelorn — "The Rockslide"
*Faction: The Stoneblooded (orthodox, but spurned) · Grid: **Chaos / Tainted** (Reveler-by-temperament inside a Warden faction — a contradiction that *works*)*

**Personality.** The clan's embarrassment and secret champion: a Stoneblood who has all the patience of his people and *none* of the restraint, who breeds true bloodlines and then charges them headlong into everything because *winning is fun and waiting is boring*. The elders disapprove; the bloodlines he raises are undeniable; and Drubb just keeps *running at problems until they're gravel*, fondly, loudly, victoriously. Proof the slow way can produce a very fast idiot.
**Goal.** Win every contest by *momentum* — not the clans' six-generation patience, but six-generation *stock* hurled downhill at speed. He wants to be the Stoneblooded champion who proves you can be patient in the *breeding* and reckless in the *fighting*, and he is annoyingly close to right.
**Signature team.** Stormgizzard (Gaia/Chaos · bruiser), Gravelchick (Gaia/Chaos · tank), Cumulobrute (Gaia/Chaos · fortress), Basaltbeast (Chaos/Thanatos · nuker). Act-3 anchor: a true-bred apex he raised for *six generations* and then trained to do exactly one thing: *charge*.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a Stoneblooded gauntlet you expect to be a stall-war — and isn't; Drubb comes *downhill*, fast and heavy, and the clans wince. **Act 2:** mentors you in *breeding* and then humiliates you in the *arena* with the results; spurn him and he adopts the feud like a festival. **Act 3:** the Titanfall slope set-piece — a charger that hits harder the more ground it crosses and the more hurt it already is, no brakes installed at any generation.
**Succession angle.** **Medium.** Chaos/Tainted by temperament. If Drubb ascends he binds **The Rockslide That Remembers Winning (DG-016)** — Chaos/Gaia, a charger whose **Avalanche, Repeated** "hits harder the more ground it crossed and the more hurt it already is… it does not remember strategy; it remembers *winning*, fondly and loudly." Spare-and-befriend gets you the rival who teaches you the *breeding* half he's good at; the elders quietly hope you'll *beat* him so they can pretend they were never proud.

### B22. The Fault-King, Maddox Thern — "The Grudge-Heavy"
*Faction: the Unbound (Devourer-leaning) · Grid: **Chaos / Corrupt** (the Devourer — Ruskin Hale's heavier, older, angrier parallel)*

**Personality.** Ruskin Hale curdles *fast and panicked*; Maddox curdled *slow and certain*, decades ago, and has had a very long time to get *heavy* with it. He's a former champion who lost a throne by a hair and has been settling the score with the entire world ever since — a grievance so old he can no longer name it, carried in a body he's spliced into something the size of a small disaster. Quiet, geological, and *furious* in the way a landslide is furious: not at you specifically, just *generally*, and you're standing there.
**Goal.** Bring the whole rigged ladder *down* — not the Unbound's clean Unmaking, but a Devourer's *spite*: if he can't have the throne, no one gets the mountain it sits on. He climbs only to *collapse* what's at the top.
**Signature team.** Magmaul (Chaos/Thanatos · nuker), Cindercub (Chaos/Thanatos · striker), Riftgrowth Slagmutant (Chaos/Gaia · nuker, abomination), Cinderlich Boneheap (Chaos/Gaia · nuker, abomination). Act-3 anchor: a *self-spliced* Maddox — he's grafted so much god-organ onto his own frame that he's more grievance than man, the way the Lab lets *you* become.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a heavy, slow ladder-duel where he barely speaks and hits like terrain; you out-tempo him or you're buried. **Act 2:** the Devourer fork — if *you* go dark, he recognizes a kindred and *escalates* to prove he's further gone; if you stay clean, he resents your restraint as cowardice and hunts it. **Act 3:** the collapse set-piece — a bruiser so heavy his blows crack the ground and delay your turns, settling a score he can't name with a body the size of a tantrum.
**Succession angle.** **High.** Chaos/Corrupt. If Maddox ascends he binds **The Fault-Line That Was a King (DG-013)** — Gaia/Chaos, a bruiser whose **Tectonic Grudge** "scales off both Bulk and Spike and cracks the ground under the struck… diplomacy was attempted by the last pantheon; the last pantheon is now a valley." The cautionary Devourer-boss for players who took the *slow* corruption road (where Ruskin is the fast one). Near-impossible to redeem — the grudge is load-bearing now — but *preventable*: beat him decisively early and he stays a mortal cautionary tale instead of a mountain range shaped like rage.

### B23. Sister Halloran Vex — "The Stormwake"
*Faction: The Deep Choir, defecting toward the Pale Court · Grid: **Balanced/Corrupt → Order/Corrupt** (Plaguelord drifting to Iron Throne)*

**Personality.** A Deep Choir assassin who learned the deep's patience and then *lost* it — too quick, too cold, gone before the grief catches up. She found the Choir's communion too *slow* and the Pale Court's ledger too *warm*, and carved a third way: hit, wither, vanish, and let the rot do the bookkeeping. She arrives like weather and leaves like one, and the cold she leaves behind does more damage than the blow. Vael's faction, Quill's destination, her own lonely tempo.
**Goal.** Become the *settlement itself* — not the Choir's remembered god, not the Court's registrar, but the swift cold *fact* of an ending, the storm-wake that closes accounts before anyone agrees the fight started. She'd ascend as the *period at the end of the sentence*.
**Signature team.** Polypip (Ouranos/Thanatos · skirmisher), Reefdirge (Ouranos/Thanatos · striker), Palllantern (Ouranos/Cosmos · striker), Tombwing (Ouranos/Cosmos · nuker). Act-3 anchor: **Hollowdrift Endmutant** (Thanatos/Eros · assassin, abomination) — a thing she made to be *faster than grief*.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** a sudden Tideless ambush — she's past you, you're withering, she's elsewhere before you understand you've lost. **Act 2:** Choir secrets *and* Court debts both bartered against you; she defects mid-arc and the two factions' grudges braid into a colder pursuit. **Act 3:** the storm-wake set-piece — a fast assassin that acts first and trails Bane over everything behind the target, lightning that leaves rot in its footprints.
**Succession angle.** **High.** Drift lands her Order/Corrupt. If Halloran ascends she binds **The Stormwake, Crowned in Absence (DG-020)** — Ouranos/Thanatos, a fast assassin whose **Wake of the Departed** "is past you before the cold catches up… you will lose the fight to something that isn't standing where you're swinging." Cannot be befriended once she's defected (she's traded warmth for speed deliberately); *can* be caught at the fork — keep her in the Choir's slow communion and she never sharpens into the storm.

### B24. The Foundry-Heir, Castle Greel — "The Iterator"
*Faction: The Iron Guild · Grid: **Order / Tainted** (the Architect — Halix Dray's rival-within-the-Guild, the next generation)*

**Personality.** Halix Dray builds *better gods than were born*; Castle Greel builds *better gods than Halix*, and says so to Halix's face with the cheerful brutality of an apprentice who's surpassed the master and hasn't the tact to hide it. Where Halix is delighted by the craft, Castle is delighted by the *competition* — every build is a shot at someone else's build, every contest a chance to obsolete a colleague. The Guild's future, and its anxiety.
**Goal.** Make every other maker's work *obsolete on arrival* — an iterating construct-line that returns from each defeat with a counter pre-installed, so that to beat it once is merely to teach it. He'd ascend as the *patch that never stops shipping*.
**Signature team.** Galeworks Engine (Ouranos/Cosmos · war-frame, construct), Swiftspire Colossus (Ouranos/Cosmos · siege-colossus, construct), Stormstack Colossus (Ouranos/Cosmos · siege-colossus, construct), Pistonworks Engine (Ouranos/Cosmos · war-frame, construct). Act-3 anchor: a **living-core iteration prototype** grafted with a god-organ — the Guild's bid, version *N+1*.
**Escalation.** **Act 1:** beats you at a Forgefell contest with a tin team, then *patches it overnight* into a worse matchup for you specifically. **Act 2:** if you favor husbandry or repetition, he iterates a hard counter to your roster each meeting, treating you as a *changelog*. **Act 3:** the iteration set-piece — a high-tempo construct that out-speeds your turns and returns each phase with the counter to whatever just worked.
**Succession angle.** **High.** Order/Tainted. If Castle ascends he mints **DG-028 — The Patch That Shipped Itself, Frame-Eternal** (newly-minted; Construct-apex flag, Ouranos/Cosmos): a foundry-class Dead God that *re-instances with a counter to whatever beat it last run*, the iterating machine-boss that treats every player who defeats it as free QA. The strongest second candidate (after Halix) to *recruit* for a God-Maker run — but where Halix would build your pantheon out of love, Castle would build it to *prove he could*, and never quite let you forget whose changelog you are.

## B.1 — Legends & historical figures (the dead, the founding, the infamous)

**L-1. Aurelia Twice-Drowned, the First Aspirant** *(dead champion / founder-myth)*
*Who:* The nobody the Threshold betting-pool was *invented* for — the first mortal to climb the god-ladder after the gods fell, who reached a throne, *refused* it, and walked back down into legend. Whether she became the first God-Maker or simply *vanished* is the oldest argument in the world.
*Voice (as quoted by others):* spare, dry, allergic to ceremony — *"The throne's just a taller grave with a better view. I've seen the view. I came down."*
*Why she matters:* the *origin* of the God-Maker temptation, name-checked by Old Maddox Quillan, the Refused, and Pilgrim Sool; her "came down" is the thesis the whole Balanced-grid argument inherits. Gates a Threshold lore-quest and the *First-Among-Nobodies* echo (`factions_npcs.md` #56) is rumored to be hers.

**L-2. Hephaestion the Abandoned, the Patron Who Fell Mid-Project** *(dead god / Forgefell's founder-corpse)*
*Who:* The god of making whose death *mid-build* created Forgefell — the industrial ruin the Iron Guild colonized. Already canon as "the Patron"; named here for the legends-roster (the mortal *Forge-hands* Bel Tindersoot mourns died building *his* works).
*Voice (as the automatons loop it):* *"—SERVE THE MAKER— —MAKER NOT FOUND—"*
*Why he matters:* the dead god whose *unfinished* directives still march Forgefell's loops; his collapsed primary forge is the site of the Guild's "Patron's Cold Heart" quest and the source of every Coal-Heart god-organ. The Memorial of Hands exists *because* of how he used his mortal workers.

**L-3. Vorrigan Saltbroke the Elder, the First Heretic** *(infamous founder / Unbound progenitor)*
*Who:* The Saltbroke who *first* welded two opposed forces on purpose and survived the world's response — progenitor of the whole abomination-crafting line (Vorr, the cousin Quill, and the field-vendor all carry his trade and his name). The man who decided shame was optional.
*Voice (carved over the Open Heresy market):* *"They named it taboo so you'd never test whether it was true. I tested it. Hold still."*
*Why he matters:* founding figure of the Unbound's taboo-tech; the "First Heresy" quest re-enacts his original sin, and the Saltbroke surname's recurrence across the cast is *his lineage*. Gates the opposed-force fusion ceiling's lore.

**L-4. The Sun-Notary Who Came Before, Archon Pell Greyvow** *(dead founder / the Concord's first ledger)*
*Who:* Velleth Sun-Notary's predecessor — the Archon who *first* concluded the gods failed for lack of *process* and began the Empty Bench. Died, it's said, of finishing a single perfect covenant no one could enact. (Surname-coincidence with the common "Pell"/"Greyvow" stems; flagged.)
*Voice (in the Concord's founding minutes):* *"Move that we govern the next ones. Seconded by no one. Carried regardless. The motion was always going to carry."*
*Why he matters:* the Concord's origin and the reason the Empty Bench has a *shape*; Veris Calx (B13) idolizes him, Velleth is *tired* of him, and his unfinished covenant is a Concord lore-relic.

**L-5. Ostrega's Mother, the Matriarch Stoneholt** *(dead founder / the Stoneblooded's first bloodline)*
*Who:* The clan-mother who raised the *first* Worldback from a Sproutling and established the six-generation patience the Stoneblooded still keep. Matriarch Ostrega Deephold is *her* heir; the oldest bloodlines in the breeding-vaults trace to her hand.
*Voice (sung by Granny Loam):* *"Catch a strong one, you've got a season. Raise a true one, you've got an age. I chose the age. I'm still in it."*
*Why she matters:* the *source* of the Stoneblooded husbandry creed and the bloodlines gated behind the "Six Generations Deep" quest; her name opens the deep breeding-vaults in clan lore.

**L-6. The Drowned King Maren Fathomlost** *(dead god-adjacent / the Tideless's first communion)*
*Who:* The first mortal to *go under on purpose* and come back able to hear the deep — founder of the Deep Choir's communion, the one who learned the low note before there was a Choir to teach it. Half-myth, possibly still down there, possibly the thing the Choir actually sings *to*.
*Voice (as the Patient Drowned relay it):* *"...I went looking for what we lost. I found what kept it. I am still... considering... coming up..."*
*Why he matters:* founding figure of the Deep Choir and a candidate for "what sank instead of fell"; Nerin Saltvow (B14) and the Drowned Cantor both reach *toward* him. Gates the deepest Tideless communion lore.

**L-7. Caustreia the Vintner-Saint, First to Change** *(infamous legend / the Revel's progenitor)*
*Who:* The Revel's founding ecstatic — the first to drink the Sweet Rot and *love* the becoming, who mutated so many times she forgot her original shape and called it freedom. Brother Cask considers himself her heir; whether she's dead, transformed past recognition, or *all the wine* is unclear and, to the Revel, irrelevant.
*Voice (toasted at every Revel):* *"To the body you were born in — may you outgrow it gloriously, and not one moment too soon!"*
*Why she matters:* the origin of the Revel's "change for its own sake" creed; her recipe is the root of every Wildchange catalyst, and the Sweet Rot's oldest, fondest mutations are said to *remember her*.

**L-8. Greel Wyrmhand, the Tamer Who Befriended a God** *(infamous dead tamer / the impossible bond)*
*Who:* The legendary tamer who, the stories insist, *befriended* an Olympian-tier creature with patience alone — no trap, no Lab, just an impossible bond held across a decade. Died old, beloved, and unascended, with the god-beast still at his side. The Bloomwardens canonize him; the Iron Guild thinks he's a fairy tale.
*Voice (as Granny Wickerlight tells it):* *"He never caught a thing in his life. Everything he had, *chose* him. That's the whole trick, littles. It's also the hardest one."*
*Why he matters:* the *proof-of-concept* for the befriend path's highest ceiling (a legendary/god won by bond, not capture); Tame-Mother Yula, Cuff Stillblood, and Seed-Keeper Wll all invoke him. Gates the befriend-a-Legendary lore and validates the Charm-slot's whole philosophy.

**L-9. The Burnt Court, the Pantheon That Lost Last Run** *(collective legend / the Devourer's ledger)*
*Who:* The previous age's *failed* champions — the gods who climbed, ruled briefly, and were *unmade* rather than enthroned, now the "Burnt Court" the Grudgekeeper (DG-024) and Maddox Thern (B22) draw from. Not individuals so much as a *warning roster*: this is who the throne eats.
*Voice (the Grudgekeeper's, for all of them):* *"We were nobodies once too. We climbed. We *won*. Ask us how that ended. ...we'll show you."*
*Why they matter:* the lore-bank of cautionary Dead Gods; every Devourer-arc rival recruits from them, and the Burnt Court is the *texture* behind the async-invasion loop — proof the wall was always someone who stood where you're standing.

**L-10. Sable Ninefold the First, the Croupier Who Booked the Gods** *(founder-legend / the High Table's origin)*
*Who:* The original Croupier (the current Sable Ninefold, `factions_npcs.md`, carries the *title*) — the gambler who looked at the fallen pantheon and *opened a line* on who'd refill it, founding the High Table on the insight that an apocalypse is just the biggest table ever set. The first to profit from the Succession instead of climbing it.
*Voice (the founding wager, still framed at the Storm Vault):* *"Nine empty thrones. Infinite nobodies. *Someone's* going to sit down. I'll take the action on all of it."*
*Why she matters:* origin of the High Table and the bounty/rival network itself; "Ninefold" is a *title* passed down (disambiguates the current holder), and her founding wager is the lore behind why the Table stays neutral — *the house doesn't pick, the house books.*

*Why he matters:* a wandering budget-vendor (Part C) that gives Drachma-poor players a cheaper-but-riskier path to rare goods; comic cross-region texture and a sink that *occasionally* burns you.

Stock is drawn from the **existing item catalog** in `economy_items_rivals.md` (re-sold, sometimes standing-gated or marked-up), plus a **few new themed items** per shop. Prices respect the locked sinks/sources: **₯ generous** (consumables/common gear), **✶ scarce & load-bearing** (souls/Lab/breeding), **◈ a divine trickle** (never sold for ₯), **black market** = ✶/◈ premium + **heat**.

> New items introduced here carry the same discipline: none exceeds `loot_engine.py`'s validated capture/breed/lab/tame ceiling, none converts ✶→₯, and divine-tier rows stay ◈-gated. Where a row re-sells a wave-1 item, the wave-1 price is the baseline (a traveling markup is called out).

- **12 net-new vendors** beside wave 1's ten markets + mobile black market: **8 regional sub-counters** (Glut hospice, Mournmarch pyre, Forgefell sundries, Storm Vault actuarial, Sunder open-heresy, Titanfall birthing-hold, Astral permitted, Tideless toll-house), **2 traveling caravans** (Ironcart, Mother Wend), **2 black-market specialists** (Memory-Fence, Gristle's Abyssal Salvage).

- **Sink/source discipline held:** ₯ for consumables/maintenance/common gear; ✶ for souls/Lab/breeding/anti-repossession; ◈ only for divine salvage, never sold for ₯; black-market rows all add **heat/notoriety**; the Deep Choir toll-house and the Memory-Fence introduce a deliberate **non-coin "memory" barter** (flagged for ingest as a Choir-exclusive token, not a currency). No new item exceeds the `loot_engine.py` capture/breed/lab/tame ceiling, and none converts ✶→₯.
- **New items total: 25** themed additions across the 12 shops (≈2 per vendor), each re-stating a locked mechanic (containment seals, environmental gene-expression, anti-repossession, befriend-aids, death-insurance, convenience-Build) rather than inventing a new system — extending the catalog, not contradicting it.
