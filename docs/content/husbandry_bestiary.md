# PART ONE — ACQUISITION FLAVOR

Three ways to take a creature into your soul-ledger, each one a small confession about who you are. The lines below are diegetic — they're what the *moment* says, not what a tooltip says. Group them by method; fire one at the relevant roll outcome.

## A. Befriend — *the pure, mercy path (high-bond start, slow)*

You don't catch it. You convince it the world is, briefly, survivable in your company. The grid reads this as mercy; the creature reads it as a gamble it might regret.

**Befriend — success (it chooses you):**
- "It stops bracing. That's the whole thing — the moment a wild thing stops getting ready to die near you. It steps in. It has decided you are the lesser of the night's evils."
- "It eats from your hand, watching your eyes the whole time, and something in it that had been clenched since birth lets go. You have just become responsible for that. Good luck."
- "No cage. No blow. It simply follows you out, the way a stray follows the one person at the funeral who looked like they'd cry too. The bond starts warm. It will test that."
- "It bows its head into your palm and the bond takes like a held note. The wild ones say you don't tame a creature — you just agree to grieve it first. It agrees back."
- "You spared it when you didn't have to, and it noticed, which is rarer in this world than gods. It comes with you. It will spend a while not believing its luck, and so should you."

**Befriend — fail (you misread it):**
- "You offered your hand. It offered its opinion. The hand is fine. The trust is not — it remembers kindness that asked for something, and now it counts you among them."
- "Too fast. You reached when it wanted distance, and the small open thing in it closed like a door in winter. It won't flee. It'll just never look at you the same, and neither will the next one."
- "It takes the food. It does not take *you*. There is a difference, and it has just taught you the difference, and the lesson cost you the food."
- "It almost stayed. You can see the exact moment it decided not to — a flinch, a recalculation, the arithmetic of a creature that has been promised things before. It leaves. You let it; that's the only mercy left."
- "Mercy isn't a key, and this lock was never going to turn for a stranger. It melts back into the green. Somewhere a Bloomwarden would tell you that's a kind of success. You are not a Bloomwarden yet."

## B. Trap — *the neutral/dominator path (reliable, impersonal, low-bond start)*

A device. A snare, a binding-jar, a circle drawn fast. It works because it doesn't ask. The creature arrives hating you, which is at least honest, and the grid notes you took the efficient road.

**Trap — success (it's caught, and furious):**
- "The binding takes. It thrashes once, twice, and then goes still with the specific stillness of a thing saving its strength for later. The bond starts at *grudge*. Everything from here is parole."
- "Snared. Clean. It glares out at you with the whole-body contempt of something that knows exactly what just happened and exactly whose fault it is. Welcome to the roster. It will not forget the jar."
- "The circle closes. No ceremony, no consent — that was the point. You have a new creature and a new enemy, and for now they're the same animal. Feed it. Slowly. From a distance."
- "It's yours, in the way a debt is yours: legally, resentfully, and with interest. The trap was reliable precisely because it didn't care how it felt. It feels how you'd expect."
- "Caught. It tests the binding with the patience of something that has all the time in the world and a very long memory. The bond is a thread. The thread is the only thing between you and the memory."

**Trap — fail (it breaks the device, or slips it):**
- "The binding holds for exactly as long as it takes the creature to be insulted by it. Then it doesn't. The snare's a ruin, your pride's a ruin, and it's gone — slower than it could've left, to make the point."
- "Close. The circle flickered, the jar trembled — and then it simply declined, the way the wild declines most things that ask it to be smaller. You're out a device and up one wary legend."
- "It hits the edge of the trap, finds the flaw you didn't, and is through it before the lid finishes shutting. The deep ones are like that. The trap is for the ones who haven't learned the world yet."
- "The device does its job. The creature does *better*. You watch your reliable, impersonal method get treated as a suggestion, and you understand, briefly, why some tamers kneel instead."
- "It breaks free and does not flee — it *looks* at you, long enough that you feel cataloged, then leaves at a walk. You have made an impression. You did not want to make this one."

## C. Summon — *the Creator path (grown, not caught — a Lab op)*

Not a hunt. You take harvested essence and you *grow* one, in the dark, on purpose. The grid calls this the Creator's road; the world calls it a few worse things. (Mechanically a Lab operation — see `Mutants_Game_Lab.md` — but the flavor of the *arrival* lives here.)

**Summon — the rite takes:**
- "Essence pools, holds its breath, and decides to be alive. It opens eyes that have never not belonged to you — no wild to miss, no mother to grieve, no parole. Just you, and the cold fact of having been *made*. The bond starts total. So does the question of what you owe a thing you summoned."
- "It coalesces in the circle, wet and new and yours from the first heartbeat, and looks up at the only face it will ever have known like that. There is no fear in it. You'll have to decide whether that's mercy or theft."
- "You didn't find it. You *meant* it. It steps out of the essence fully fond of you and entirely without a past, which the Bloomwardens find obscene and the Iron Guild finds efficient. Both are watching what you do next."
- "The rite seals. The thing inside it inhales for the first time and is already loyal — loyalty you didn't earn so much as *author*, which is the whole uneasy point of the Creator's path. It will love you because you wrote it that way."

**Summon — the rite curdles (a botch):**
- "The essence sets wrong. What climbs out of the circle is yours, yes, and alive, technically, and looking at you with the bewildered hurt of something that knows, even new, that it came out incomplete. You made this. The ledger remembers who."
- "It coalesces, shudders, and resolves into something the recipe did not promise. Still bonded. Still yours. Just *more* than you asked for, and watching you with eyes that already suspect the answer to 'why am I like this' is standing right there."

## D. The Creature That Refuses to Fight — *the conscientious objector*

Some creatures will bond, will follow, will love you — and will not raise a claw, ever, for anyone. Healers, settlers, vigil-keepers: the radiant deer, the Mercy-Garden tenders, the small glowing things that mend and will not maim. The world has a whole quiet category for the beast that came to *care*, not to win. Fire these when one is fielded into violence, or when a tamer tries to push a pacifist into the fray.

- "It plants itself between your wounded and the worst of it, glowing, patient — and does not strike back. It will mend the whole line bleeding under it before it lifts a hoof in anger, which it will not do. Some creatures don't fight. They *attend*."
- "You order the attack. It looks at you, then at the dying thing across the field, then at you again — and goes to the dying thing. It came into this world to close wounds, not open them. You can field it or you can leave it home; you cannot make it a blade."
- "It will follow you into the Maw itself. It will not bite. It glows brighter the worse things get, healing what it can reach, refusing on some old gentle principle to add to the count. The Bloomwardens weep at its funerals. Everyone does."
- "Push it toward the front and it digs in *deeper*, not forward — a settler, not a soldier. Disputes lose their heat near it; dice land flatter; tempers cool. It wins fights by making them stop being fights. It cannot do anything else, and would not if it could."
- "Strange thing about the ones that won't fight: the wild predators bow to them anyway. A pacifist that survives long enough in this world is making a point the rest of the bestiary can't argue with, and somewhere it knows it."

# PART TWO — BOND & RELATIONSHIP LORE

## What the bond *is*, in the world

The bond is not friendship and it is not ownership, though tamers argue for both at the tavern. In-world, the bond is a **soul-tether** — a thin, load-bearing line of shared essence between you and a creature, recorded permanently in its creature-soul alongside its lineage. The Pale Court, who treat everything as accounting, call it an *open account*: a thing you and the creature are both, slowly, paying into. The Bloomwardens call it a *vow you keep accidentally*. Both are describing the same three jobs the tether quietly does.

**The bond does three jobs** (this is the locked system — `Acquisition_Husbandry.md` "bond-does-three-jobs"):
1. **It gates evolution.** A creature will not change its shape for a stranger. Evolution is the soul agreeing to become *more*, and it only agrees for someone it has decided to become more *for*. A low-bond beast plateaus, stubborn, half-finished — the way a thing refuses to grow up out of spite, or grief, or simple distrust.
2. **It gates Lab-consent.** The tether is also a permission. A bonded creature will *let* you take it to the altar — let you splice, mutate, improve it — in a way an unbonded one fights. (The dark joke the Iron Guild never says out loud: a high bond makes the rendering pits *easier*, because the creature holds still for the one it trusts. Slag-Master Bohm hums to them first for a reason.)
3. **It gates autonomy.** High enough, the tether runs both ways, and the creature acts on its own read of a fight — covers your blind side, refuses a bad order, finishes a thing you started. Low, it does exactly and only what it's told, like a tool with a grudge. The most powerful creatures in the world are the ones trusted enough to *disobey* you correctly.

## How the bond *feels* — the husbandry voice

The husbandry voice is the warm, grief-aware register of someone who has raised a great many beasts and buried most of them and does it anyway. It is the Bloomwarden register at its core — tender, blunt, funny in the way grief is funny — but every faction has a dialect of it. Cobb the beast-handler speaks it in single words. Mother Kestrel speaks it like a warning she's given ten thousand times: *"It'll love you or it'll eat you. Sometimes both, in that order. Hold out your hand anyway."* The voice never coos. It respects the creature too much to coo. It says hard things gently and gentle things plainly, and it always, always assumes the animal can tell when you're lying.

**Bond-state flavor (fire on relationship-tier shifts):**
- *Wary (fresh trap-catch):* "It eats when you're not looking and sleeps facing the door. Give it that. Everything in this world that ever fed it wanted something. You'll have to be the boring exception for a long, dull while before it believes you're the exception at all."
- *Warming:* "It let you touch the scar today. Didn't flinch. You'd think nothing of it if you didn't know what the flinch used to cost it. Small thing. Whole world in it."
- *Bonded:* "It comes when you're hurt now, not when you call. There's a difference, and you've earned the difference, and you should be a little frightened of how much you'd do to keep it."
- *Devoted:* "It would walk into the Maw for you, and the terrible part is it *knows* it would, and so do you, and neither of you has found a way to make that not be true. This is the part the songs leave out. Mind it."

## How a creature reads its tamer — pure vs. corrupt

The tether carries more than affection; it carries *flavor*. Raise a creature with mercy and it grows up expecting the world to be survivable. Raise it in the rendering pits and the altar-smoke and it grows up expecting to be spent. The creature can feel which one you are long before the grid finishes deciding, and its body reports the verdict — gene expression, temperament, the look in its eye.

**A pure tamer's creatures:**
- "They sleep soundly near you. That's the tell. A thing that sleeps with its soft parts exposed beside its tamer has made a judgment about that tamer, and the judgment is *kind*. The radiant lines will only bond at all to someone who reads this way — try to trap a Goldhart with a corrupt soul and it simply leaves, glowing, disappointed in you specifically."
- "They evolve *toward* the light in them. Care nudges expression. A Sunnix raised gentle wakes brighter every dawn; one raised hard learns to hide the glow, which a Bloomwarden will tell you, weeping, is the saddest thing a Sunnix can learn."

**A corrupt tamer's creatures:**
- "They obey beautifully and trust nothing. A corrupt-raised beast is a flawless instrument and a hollow one — it does exactly what it's told because it has correctly concluded that affection here is a transaction, and it is not interested in transactions it will lose. The Pale Court considers this ideal. The creature considers it Tuesday."
- "They flinch at the altar, then go anyway. That's the corrupt bond — not love, exactly, but a learned, resigned *yes*, the way you stop arguing with weather. The dark lines like it. The Gloamveil host, the Cinderwyrms, the Riotgrief brood — they were never going to sleep soft beside anyone. With a corrupt tamer they don't have to pretend."
- "They start to look like you. Not literally — but the temperament migrates. People who have met both will say your creatures carry your weather. A corrupt tamer travels with a small storm of beasts that have all, quietly, given up on being surprised by cruelty. It is the most honest portrait of you the world will ever paint."

**The hinge (a 4th-wall-adjacent beat, used rarely):**
- "Here is the thing no faction will tell you, because each needs you to pick a side: the creature is keeping a record of you, in its soul, that outlives you. On a throne ending it becomes a boss someone else will fight — and it will fight them *the way you raised it*. Every act of care or cruelty is a brushstroke on the portrait your champion becomes. The bond was always a mirror. You just thought you were the one holding it."

# PART THREE — BREEDING & GENETICS FLAVOR

The slow path's slowest, deepest game: two creatures, an egg, an incubation, and a roll across a wide **±35% genome** that decides what climbs out. Dominant and recessive genes, inherited IVs, a rare-gene chase, and the region itself tinting the offspring — the locked system in `Acquisition_Husbandry.md`, given a voice. This is the husbandry endgame: lineage tracking and selective breeding toward the "perfect specimen," the entry fee for the Breeding Shows.

## How lineages combine — the in-world picture

The world does not say *genome*. The world says **the long thread**. Every creature-soul carries its full lineage like a string of knots — each ancestor a knot, each knot still *humming* faintly with what it was. Put two creatures to breed and you are, in the diegetic frame, **braiding two threads and seeing which knots speak loudest in the child.** Some traits shout (dominant); some have been quiet for generations and only surface when two carriers happen to braid (recessive). The Iron Guild, who'd rather build than breed, calls breeding "the gambler's foundry — same outputs, none of the control." They mean it as an insult. Breeders take it as the appeal.

**Lineage-combination flavor (egg laid / pairing made):**
- "Two threads, braided. The egg holds the knot of everything they both were, arguing quietly with itself about which parts to be. You won't know who won the argument until it hatches. Nobody ever does. That's the whole sport."
- "She carries her grandmother's frost in a knot that hasn't spoken in four generations. He carries the same silence on the other side. Braid them and the old cold might wake — or might sleep one more lifetime. You're not breeding a creature. You're *interviewing the dead* and hoping one answers."
- "The egg takes. Inside, two lineages negotiate. Dominant traits sit at the head of the table and talk over everyone; the recessives wait in the back, patient as the Pale Court, for the one pairing in a dozen where they finally get the floor."

## Genetic archaeology — the dormant-gene flavor

The richest flavor in breeding is that recessives are *archaeology*. A dormant gene is an ancestor still trying to be born — a trait that ran in the line once, got buried under louder blood, and waits in the thread for a tamer patient enough to dig it back up. Breeders speak of it exactly like grave-robbing, which in a world that is *the graveyard of gods you came to rob* is the entire thesis in miniature.

- "Dig long enough down a bloodline and you find the gods in it. Every dormant gene is an ancestor that didn't get to finish being itself — and breeding is just the shovel. The deep recessives are the oldest dead in the thread, the ones nobody alive remembers carrying, surfacing in some great-great-grandchild like a name read off a stone."
- "The hedge-witch Old Garran calls it 'waking the cellar.' Every creature is a house, and the cellar is full of what its ancestors were, and most tamers never go down there. You go down there. You breed for the thing on the bottom shelf that hasn't seen light since before the gods fell. Sometimes it's a treasure. Sometimes it's a reason the family stopped breeding that way."
- "A recessive isn't *new*. That's the eerie part. When it finally surfaces, the creature doesn't gain a trait — it *remembers* one. The body knows the shape already, the way you know a song you've never heard. It was always in the thread. You just kept braiding until it had nowhere left to hide."

## The rare-recessive chase — what it *feels* like

This is the min-max heart of the slow path, and it should feel like obsession with a body count of eggs. The chase: pair, lay, incubate, hatch, check the thread, and — almost always — find the old gene stayed quiet one more generation. Gear shifts the odds (a Glyph slot, per the ratified gear map, pushes 10%→30% — the breeder's relic), but the genome is wide and the dead are stubborn.

- "Forty eggs. Forty hatchlings, each one *almost*. The recessive is in there — you've traced the thread, you *know* it's in there — and forty times it has chosen to keep sleeping. This is the chase: not difficulty, exactly. Patience, weaponized against you by your own ancestors."
- "You'll hold the forty-first to the light and check the thread before it's even dry, the way a gambler checks a card he already turned. Pure recessive hunting is the closest the gentle path comes to the altar's hunger — you just feed it eggs instead of essence, and tell yourself that's different. (It is. A little.)"
- "The Glyph helps. The Glyph always helps — thirty percent instead of ten, the breeder's whole religion in one carved stone. But thirty percent means seven in ten times the cellar stays locked, and you will run the math, and you will breed again, because the perfect specimen is always exactly one more egg away. It is never one more egg away."
- "The faithful keep a name for the breeder who finally surfaces a god-deep recessive: *thread-reader*. It is half admiration and half the way you'd talk about someone who spent thirty years on a single grave. Both halves are correct. The specimen is beautiful. Ask what it cost."

## Breeding-outcome quips (fire on hatch)

- **Common outcome (no rare gene):** "It hatches healthy, ordinary, and entirely loved — which is its own kind of failure, if you were chasing the cellar. The thread stayed quiet. Forty-two, then. The egg doesn't know it disappointed you. Don't tell it."
- **Good IV roll:** "It comes out *built* — ceilings raised, the good knots loud, the makings of a specimen. Not the recessive you wanted, but the kind of hatchling that makes you forgive the genome for a day. Write it in the thread. You'll breed it back in."
- **Rare recessive surfaces:** "There. *There.* The old gene, awake at last after a dozen lifetimes asleep — the thing the line forgot it carried, remembered in the body of a creature still wet from the egg. Every disappointed clutch was the price of this. You'd pay it again. You know you would. You will."
- **Region-tint hits (environmental expression):** "It hatched in the cold and the cold got an opinion. The offspring carries the region's hand on it — a tint of where it was raised, braided in alongside the blood. Raise the next one somewhere else and watch the same thread come out a different color. The land breeds too; you just thought it was only you."
- **Botch / mismatch:** "Something in the braid pulled wrong. It's alive, it's yours, it's *off* — a knot that spoke when it shouldn't have, an ancestor that surfaced sideways. Lineage tracking calls it a fault. The creature calls it being born. Same event, kinder word."
- **Apex-lineage milestone:** "Generations of patient braiding, and the thread finally holds nothing but the best of itself — every loud knot good, every quiet one chosen. This is the specimen the Breeding Shows were built to crown. It doesn't know it's a masterpiece. It just knows you, which it has decided is enough, and is somehow right."

# PART FOUR — BESTIARY CONNECTIVE TISSUE

Fifteen short ecology notes that braid the roster into a living web — who preys on whom, who fears whom, who herds and who parodies, and how the six forces draw the lines. Each references the canonical creatures (`creatures_wild_1.md`, `creatures_legendary_construct.md`, `Creature_Codex_Families.md`) and the canonical regions (`content/regions.md`). Funny-grim register throughout: the food chain is real, and it has a sense of humor about itself.

**1. The radiant block and the dark interlude — the bill comes due (Eros vs. Thanatos · the Verdant Glut fringe & edges).**
The Verdant Glut's sunlit fringe runs thick with radiant lines — the **Gilt Hart / Sunfleece / Aureole** clade, the **Aurelet** kits and **Lumbkin** lambs, all glow and mercy and Vitality. The grim joke the Bloomwardens live inside: every block of sunrise *summons its own predator*. The **Sablewidow**, **Falseangel**, and **Scarletwidow** — Thanatos shadows that mimic the light to get near the trusting — evolved *specifically* to hunt the radiant ones, the **Falseangel** wearing a counterfeit halo to walk right up to a **Halofleece** that's never learned to distrust a glow. Eros and Thanatos are an opposed pair; here that opposition is a literal pursuit. Where the daisies are brightest, the widows are closest. The Wardens know. They glow anyway.

**2. The Drown-Choir keeps a hunting ground, not a herd (Thanatos · the Tideless & the Mournmarch's fens).**
The lich-and-spirit **Drown-Choir** — **Sepulchre Cantor**, **Choir-Shade Soprano**, **Threnody Warden**, up to the **Sovereign of the Silent Pews** — does not prey in the ordinary way. It *conducts*. The Cantor sets a tempo that slows everything in earshot to a funeral's pace, and the rest of the choir simply collects what can no longer run. In the Tideless and the cold Mournmarch fens, smaller Thanatos and Ouranos beasts have learned the Choir's key the way prey learns a hawk's shadow: when the field goes quiet and slow, the small things have already lost, and the only survival trait left is *having left earlier*. Nothing herds with the Choir. Things flee *ahead* of it, which from a distance looks like the same animals migrate every season, and is really the same animals running from the same song.

**3. The frost court runs in a pecking order of speed (Ouranos/Chaos · the Storm Vault & high Tideless).**
The **Hoarfrost Court** is a single ecology stacked by velocity. At the bottom, the **Sleetkit** foxes and **Snowdown** harelings — fast, friendly, used "mostly for mischief, occasionally for murder if you didn't share." Above them the storm-wolves: **Galewolf**, **Stormveil**, and the apex **Tempestmane**, who "starts a fight quick and accelerates." The court's law is Celerity — rank is whoever arrives first, and the kits survive by being too quick and too cute to bother eating until the wolves are genuinely hungry. The **Frostpaw** ices every surface it crosses "as a public service," which mostly means the slower predators behind it eat pavement. A frost court at full strength is less a pack than a foot-race with teeth, and the prize is the prey.

**4. The crystal wyrms parody the dragon, and resent the comparison (Cosmos/Chaos · the Astral Tier & Forgefell crystal reaches).**
The crystal clade — **Prism Wyrm**, **Glasswing Lattice-Drake**, **Geodecrane**, up to **Galaxis Prime-Wyrm** — are dragons in shape and *proofs* in temperament. They don't breathe fire; they breathe **refraction**, geometry, angles, "the cold precision of a proof." The molten **Cinderwyrm Flight** two regions over considers them bloodless show-offs; the crystal wyrms consider the Cinderwyrms loud and structurally unsound. It is the oldest rivalry in the bestiary that never actually fights, because Cosmos and Chaos are opposed and a meeting would be a taboo abomination — so they sneer across the force-line instead, two halves of "dragon" that each think the other got it wrong. The **Iridescent Faewyrm**, "vanity, armed," is the clade's self-portrait: beautiful, brittle, and absolutely certain it's the real article.

**5. The Forbidden Choir herds with nothing and frightens its own makers (taboo abominations · the Sunder & the Maw Beneath).**
The eldritch cluster — **Branchflayer**, **Vespermoth**, **Gallowsthread**, **Orantmantis**, **Palewretch**, **Maulbrood** — are "siblings in sin," each one a taboo-fusion the world hunts you for making. They do not form an ecology because nothing will *join* one. Wild beasts give them a wide berth that reads, on a map, like a dead zone moving slowly through the Sunder. The unsettling part, per the codex, is the **Orantmantis** "prays without pause to no god, and the prayer *works*" — and the smaller creatures can tell. A clearing the Forbidden Choir has passed through stays empty for a season; the local Chaos lines, who fear almost nothing, relocate. When the things born of the deepest corruption are avoided by the wild *children* of corruption, that is the bestiary drawing a line even it won't cross.

**6. The foundry constructs don't eat, so the wild doesn't know what to do with them (construct · Forgefell & the Iron Guild's holdings).**
The **Cogwright** and **Bastion** lines — riveted, wound-spring, "built, not born" — break every instinct the organic roster runs on. They give off no fear-scent, no hunger, no prey-signal; a **Cogwright Mk.VIII "Owlwork"** tallying you against an unseen standard registers to a wild beast as *terrain that is, disconcertingly, paying attention.* Organic predators learn to ignore them (you can't eat a closed door) right up until they wander into a held line and discover the door pushes back. The constructs, for their part, herd only with each other and only by **standing still in the same place**, which is the foundry's idea of company. A **Bastion-Mk.I "Aegistread"** and a **Worldspine** kaiju can hold the same ridge for a decade and never once acknowledge one another — the only two things in the region too solid to bother.

**7. The orchard and mangrove herons are the Glut's gentle backbone, and the Revel's larder (Eros/Gaia · the Verdant Glut).**
The heron lines — **Saplingstilt → Verdwade → Rootsalve** (mangrove, tidal mercy) and the **Budwarden → Boughtender → Orchardmother** wardens — are the Bloomwardens' favorite wild allies: patient, healing, region-tinted toward Eros, the warm spine the fringe is built on. They herd loosely with the radiant deer and tolerate tamers easily (high befriend odds). Deeper in, the **Revel**'s carnival beasts — **Tumblebaby → Riotmummer → Bigtopper** — treat the same herons as ingredients for a party, "life is sacred *and* should be enjoyed until it screams." The Glut's whole tragedy in one food web: the gentlest creatures in the world are also, to the things one clearing over, the most delicious. Matron Sevvy prunes the fringe to keep that clearing far away.

**8. The Gloamveil host is a single nightmare cut into body-parts (Thanatos/Chaos · the Mournmarch & the Maw Beneath).**
The **Gloamveil clade** — wild "lesser" stalkers (**Mantissable**, **Direhusk**, **Pallpanther**) feeding the Legendary "greater" (**Vesperdrake**, **Mourningmare**) — is, per the codex, "a sprawling abomination-host split into body-cuts": *Sablewing, Hornblack, Coilgloam, Husklimb, Pall-stalker.* Ecologically they behave like one organism wearing many bodies. The **Pallpanther** marks; the **Mourningmare** ferries the marked toward the back of the field; the greater forms collect. They don't compete — they *coordinate*, the way a hand's fingers don't fight over the cup. To a tamer this is the most dangerous thing in the Mournmarch after the Pale Court itself: a host that hunts you in parts, each one a piece of the same old crime, none of them the whole of it until it's too late.

**9. The Cinderwyrm Flight rules by altitude and grudge, and taxes everything below (Chaos/Thanatos · the Sunder & Forgefell's lava reaches).**
The molten apex dragons — **Cinderwyrm Patriarch**, **Slagmaw Tyrant**, **Emberscourge**, the vain **Furnace-Crowned Wyrm** — sit at the literal top of their food web and the literal top of the air. The **Slagmaw Tyrant** "taxes every trespasser in the currency of being on fire"; the **Furnace-Crowned Wyrm** mistook its burning horns for a crown "and you for a subject who hasn't yet bowed." Below them, the Sunder's ground-bound Chaos lines live in a protection racket: stay low, stay ashen, don't be worth the dive. The carrion-birds have learned the Flight's schedule so precisely that "the carrion birds know dusk is dinner" is less observation than appointment. An ecology organized entirely around *not being noticed by the things on fire above you*.

**10. The Stoneblooded giants don't hunt, they accumulate (Gaia · Titanfall).**
In Titanfall — the region around dead **Mordathun, the Old Weight** — the apex Gaia beasts barely register as predators. **Worldspine** "does not attack or hurry, it is simply there, a horizon standing in your way." **Cairnsaur** lets the things that die near it stay, "becoming the cairn it carries without comment." The food web here runs *downhill*: small things live in the shadow and shelter of the big things, which do not eat them so much as fail to notice them, and when the big things finally lie down they become geography the small things live *on*. Mordathun is the proof of the pattern at god-scale — a Titan who fell and became the hill. Everything in Titanfall is either a mountain-in-waiting or a tenant of one. The Stoneblooded clans farm the difference.

**11. Force-rivalry without contact — the opposed pairs sneer across a line they can't cross.**
The three opposed pairs (Cosmos⇄Chaos, Eros⇄Thanatos, Gaia⇄Ouranos) structure the whole bestiary's rivalries — but because fusing across them is taboo, the rivalries are *cold*. The crystal wyrms and Cinderwyrms (Cosmos/Chaos) sneer and never meet (note 4). The radiant block and the dark widows (Eros/Thanatos) *do* meet, in a one-sided hunt, because predation isn't fusion (note 1). And Gaia⇄Ouranos plays out as pure contempt-of-pace: the **Worldspine** giants of Titanfall regard the frost-court racers and storm-wolves as undignified twitching, while the **Tempestmane** and its kin treat the standing mountains as scenery to lap. Nobody bridges the line. The line is the taboo. The bestiary's deepest rivalries are all, by design, arguments nobody is allowed to finish — which is exactly why they never stop.

**12. The pacifist healers sit *outside* the food web, and the food web lets them (cross-force · everywhere, concentrated in the Verdant Glut).**
The radiant settlers and Mercy-Garden tenders — the **Goldhart**, **Hallowdeer**, **Sanctback**, the small mending things — won't fight, and the strangest fact in the bestiary is that this *works*. Predators that would eat anything else give a glowing pacifist a berth. The codex line on the **Sigilram**: "disputes lose their heat near it, dice land flatter, tempers cool." Ecologists among the Bloomwardens think the healers became, over generations, a kind of neutral ground — a creature so committed to non-violence that fighting near it feels, to the wild, like fighting in a temple. The dark mirror is real too: the **Glorivore** and **Goldcalf** weaponize exactly this trust, "false-holy radiance that eats anything that kneels." The bestiary's one safe space is also its best bait. Everything in this world that looks like mercy is worth a second, careful look.

**13. The herd-parodies — creatures that ape a stronger neighbor to survive (mixed · the Storm Vault & the Verdant Glut fringe).**
A whole stratum of small beasts survives by *impersonation*. The **Frostling**, "a thumb-sized thing convinced it's a dragon," nips far larger creatures and zips away — a parody of the **Glacewyrm** that works because predators can't be bothered to disprove it. The **Skydown Chicklet** and **Sproutling Drakelet** ape apex flights they'll never grow into, "failing upward" on borrowed menace. In the Glut, **Violet Wyrmkit** appoints itself emotional support to a party that didn't ask. These are the bestiary's bluffers — creatures whose entire survival strategy is *resembling something the food web has already decided to respect.* Mostly it works. The funny-grim catch: the day a predator finally calls the bluff is also the only day the bluff ever mattered, and the little ones never see that day coming, because if they could, they wouldn't bluff.

**14. The Maw Beneath inverts every food web above it (deep corruption · the Maw Beneath, below the Mournmarch).**
Down in the Maw — where dead force pools and rots into living entropy, anchoring the **Devourer** — the ecology runs backwards. There is no prey and no predator; there is only *what is dissolving and what is dissolving it*, and the roles swap constantly. Creatures that surface from the Maw, like the deepest **Riotgrief** brood, don't hunt to eat — they hunt because the Maw taught them that the only stable state is *less*. The **Maelkraken** "mistook ambush predator for being everywhere at once." Up in the lit regions, an ecology is a wheel — things eat, breed, die, feed the next things. In the Maw the wheel has no rim; everything pours toward the center and the center is never full. It is the bestiary's one true bottom, and the things that live there are the proof that "alive" and "hungry" can be the same word.

**15. Domestication runs against the grain of all of it — and that's the point (the husbandry thesis · Threshold & the Verdant Glut).**
Step back and the whole web is a machine for *not* trusting. Prey flees, predators stalk, the opposed forces sneer across uncrossable lines, the Maw dissolves, and every glowing mercy might be bait. Into this, the tamer walks holding out a hand. The **Foundling Pens** in Threshold gather the canonical baby lines — **Gnawpup → Ruinmaw**, **Sproutling → Worldback**, **Dapplefawn → Palehart**, **Duskling → Gloamcat** — in their gentlest forms, precisely *before* the food web has finished teaching them what they are. To bond a creature is to interrupt the ecology — to be the one thing in its life that wanted nothing it would lose by giving. The bestiary is a list of reasons that shouldn't work. The bond is the slow, stubborn, generational argument that it can anyway. That argument is the game. Hold out your hand anyway.
