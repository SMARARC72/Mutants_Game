# PART ONE — THE ELEVEN ENDINGS, AUTHORED IN DEPTH

*The Choice (Q4.4 "The Empty Seat") opens three doors: **ASCEND** (become one of the 9 grid-gods your axes earned), **GOD-MAKER** (refuse the seat, rule a pantheon you built), or **THE UNMAKING** (kill the Primordials, end the cycle). The nine ascensions are authored first, each as four beats: the **Ascension Ceremony** (the in-world rite of sitting), **What the World Becomes** (the age your reign writes), **Your Final Form & Voice** (the boss-kit body you keep, per `pantheon_kits.md` §2, and the dead-god voice you speak in), and **The Closing Beat** (the last image, funny-grim). Each ascension names the **Book05 Dead-God seed** it snapshots into for Act 5. Then the two refusals — **God-Maker** and **the Unmaking** — close the set.*

> **The shared frame (every ascension opens the same way, then diverges).** The seat reads back your whole grid (the cruelty, per QT-5: it shows you *who you meant to be* and *who you turned out to be*, and won't let you pretend they're the same). The door that lights is the one your accumulated coordinate earned — most runs light one or two adjacent gods. **You don't pick which of the nine; the run picked.** Then you sit, and the chair begins to eat. What it leaves is the god. The eleven sections below are what's left.

## ENDING 1 — THE LAWGIVER (Order · Pure) · *Law/Heaven*

**Force-vector:** Gaia 594 / Ouranos 439 (the bruiser-tank; the validated Saint-King ascension, `Character.md`). **Dead-God seed: DG-001 *The Lawgiver, Twice-Crowned*.** **Boss kit:** *Edict of Standing Stone* (Strike, Gaia) — telegraphed verdict that staggers. (`pantheon_kits.md` §2.)

**The Ascension Ceremony — *The Reading of the Sentence.***
The Lawgiver does not climb onto the throne; the Lawgiver is *seated, formally, by the institution that read ahead.* The Concord arrives in full — Velleth Sun-Notary at the head, the Astral Tier's law-courts emptied to attend — and they do not kneel to *you.* They kneel to *the filing.* The rite is a recitation: every clause of the marker you signed in Threshold (Act 0), read back in order, each one now *fulfilled.* You do not speak vows; the vows were the contract, and you have already, unknowingly, kept them all. When the last clause is read, the seat closes over you like a verdict closing over a defendant — patient, inevitable, *correct.* Vael Construct-Nine notarizes it with the only tenderness a notary has: he gets the spelling of your throne-name right on the first try.

> *Velleth (officiating):* "We courted you when you were nobody because the record indicated you would arrive *here*, lawfully, to fill the seat *correctly.* The Concord does not celebrate. The Concord *confirms.* ...Confirmed. Rise as the law, and audit the heavens. We will do the paperwork forever, and so, now, will you."
> *The Throne (closing over a Lawgiver):* "You came up a nobody, like all of them — but a *tidy* one. You'll be a good chair. You'll keep the rules so well that no one will notice the rules are just the shape of the last winner's spine. ...Sit straight. You're load-bearing now."

**What the World Becomes — *The Filed Age.***
Under the Lawgiver, the world *holds.* The doors-are-open window (the unthrottled chaos that let *you* climb) is **re-locked, lawfully** — the Concord's lifelong dream, achieved through you. Ascension becomes a vetted civil service again; the next nobody must *apply.* The eight corpse-regions are surveyed, sealed, and assigned wardens; the Sunder's fraying edge is walled by edict; the Maw is sealed at every gate and *stays* sealed because the law now has a god behind it instead of one dying man (poor Warden Last gets a *colleague*, finally). It is the safest age the world has had since the Bare Age — and the most *still.* Nothing unexpected happens, which the Concord calls peace and the Unbound call a tomb with good signage. The grim joke underneath: a world that can never again make a nobody into a god is a world that can never again make a *you.* You locked the door behind yourself, and the click is the sound of mercy and the sound of a cell, identically.

**Your Final Form & Voice.**
You keep the body of DG-001 — *geology that learned to sprint.* Bulk that buries armies by still being there when they're gone; Ouranos enough to let the verdict *arrive first* on the turn that matters. You are immovable and you are *early*, which should be a contradiction and is instead a sentence. Your voice is the voice of a thing that has read the whole ledger and found it, on balance, in order: dry, unhurried, faintly fond of the people it sentences. You announce your target a turn before you crush it — the Edict — because the law is nothing if not *fair warning.*

> *The Lawgiver, mid-reign:* "I am back to enforce rules nobody can find the text of. ...You needn't look for it. I *am* the text. Sit down. You're out of order."

**The Closing Beat.**
The last image is the Reliquary — the same vault where, in Act 1, you read the record of the ascended and your face did the math. Now your file is in it, *filed by you*, in perfect order, on the correct shelf, between two winners you never met. A new clerk dusts it. Somewhere below, a nobody you'll never meet is signing a marker, and the first clause reads *the climb is lawful, vetted, and watched.* You wrote that clause. You are the watch. *Confirmed.*

## ENDING 2 — THE ARCHITECT (Order · Tainted) · *the Master-Builder*

**Force-vector:** Cosmos 594 / Ouranos 439 (the tempo-controller). **Dead-God seed: new (Architect form) — closest Book05 echo DG-002 *The Foreseen, Ever-Early*.** **Boss kit:** *Close the Firmament* (Ward, Cosmos) — freezes the enemy turn-order while it seals. (`pantheon_kits.md` §2.)

**The Ascension Ceremony — *The Commissioning.***
The Architect does not *receive* the throne; the Architect *redesigns* it. Foundress Magna Ironwright stands witness — the woman who preached "build, don't inherit" her whole life and never once managed it — and watches a nobody do the thing she couldn't: not just take the empty seat, but *re-engineer the act of sitting.* The rite is a survey. You walk the throne-room measuring it, finding it *off-spec* — the seat too low, the firmament misaligned, the stars in the wrong assigned positions — and you *correct it.* The ascension is the moment the correction *takes*, when the heavens snap into the order you specified and *stay there.* It is the only ascension that improves the furniture. It is also, quietly, the most arrogant thing anyone has ever done to the sky.

> *Foundress Magna Ironwright (witness, and it costs her to say it):* "You built it. I never could. I'm not jealous. ...I'm a *little* jealous. ...You took the seat and you *filed a change-request on the cosmos.* I've waited my whole life to see someone with the nerve. Mind the tolerances. A god you can maintain beats a god you have to apologize for — and you, you magnificent off-spec creature, are *maintainable.*"
> *The Throne (closing over an Architect, audibly recalibrated):* "...You *edited* me. Hm. ...Better, actually. The lumbar support is — never mind. You'll keep everything in its assigned seat now, including the assigning. ...You're the first chair that improved when someone sat in it. Don't let it go to your firmament."

**What the World Becomes — *The Drafted Age.***
Under the Architect, the world is *re-engineered to schedule.* Where the Lawgiver *re-locks* the old doors, the Architect *builds new ones, to spec* — ascension isn't forbidden, it's *standardized*, run through a process, vetted by *design review* rather than law. The regions are not just sealed but *optimized*: the Glut's over-fertility is throttled to sustainable yield, Forgefell's dead automaton-lines are reactivated *on schedule*, the Sunder's chaos is not walled but *channeled* into useful variance the way an engineer channels a river. It is order-as-*construction* rather than order-as-*restriction* — the Tainted note, the reason it's not the Lawgiver: the Architect will *use* a forbidden thing if the spec calls for it, will bolt a soul-core to the firmament if the firmament needs the power, and files the screaming under acceptable load. The age runs *beautifully.* It runs *on time.* It has no room in it for anything that wasn't in the plan, which means it has no room in it for another *you* — the next nobody isn't locked out, they're *designed out*, which Magna would tell you is crueler and more honest, and she would be right.

**Your Final Form & Voice.**
You keep the tempo-controller's body — Cosmos enough to *edit the turn order*, Ouranos enough to make sure the edit *lands before anyone can object.* You don't out-damage; you out-*schedule.* You freeze the sequence, shuffle the queue, seal the firmament, and out-initiative anyone who tries to break it — the whole fight runs on *your* clock, which is the only clock now. Your voice is a project manager who has become a god and is *delighted* by the scope: precise, faintly impatient, treating every disorder as a personal filing error it would love to *resolve.*

> *The Architect, mid-reign:* "It keeps the stars in their assigned seats and treats your disorder as a personal filing error. ...You're not in the schedule. Let me find where you go. ...Ah. Here. You go *away.*"

**The Closing Beat.**
The last image is a drafting table — the Hollow Atelier's, the God-Maker's domain, the *one* place that out-builds you — with a note slid under a door that leads nowhere. The note is from Magna, and it is the same note she could only ever send to a player who *refused*: *"You built a world. I never could."* But you didn't refuse. You took the seat *and* re-drew it, and so the note has no door to go under — you are inside the institution you improved, and the only person who could appreciate the improvement is the one institution you'll never join. You filed the cosmos perfectly. There is no one left who isn't *in* the file to read it.

## ENDING 3 — THE IRON THRONE (Order · Corrupt) · *the Machine*

**Force-vector (Construct apex):** Ward 527 / Vitality 238 / Bane (the Construct budget; DG-023 stat shape, BST 1313). **Dead-God seed: DG-023 *Reliquary-Frame "Stillwater"*** (re-skinned to the throne) / DG-005 *Engine of the Long Goodbye* for colder builds. **Boss kit:** *Enshrined Mercy* (Ward, Cosmos) + *Decommission* (Drain, Thanatos) — out-lasts attrition, revive-locks the wounded. (`pantheon_kits.md` §2.)

**The Ascension Ceremony — *The Installation.***
The Iron Throne is the only ascension that is not a *coronation* but a *commissioning of equipment.* The person does not sit on the throne; the person is *bolted into it* and becomes part of the mechanism — the Tainted-Order corner taken to its terminal logic, where the chair stops eating the person because the person has *volunteered to be load-bearing structure.* The Pale Court attends (Steward Wessel, who appreciates a clean conversion) and the Iron Guild attends (the soul-cores have to come from *somewhere*), and between them they perform less a rite than an *intake procedure*: the champion is measured, the divinity is *bolted on straight* (Castor Brail's lifelong dream, achieved on a person), and the new god boots up guarding a reliquary that has been empty for ages and will be guarded perfectly anyway. There are no last words because the throne has a maintenance schedule and last words aren't on it.

> *Vael Construct-Nine (notarizing, unusually quiet):* "Subject is not ascending. Subject is being *installed.* ...For the record, the subject consented. Repeatedly. Eagerly. The subject filed the requisition for its own bolts. ...I have notarized cruelty and I have notarized mercy. This is the first time I've notarized *uptime.* ...Commissioned. The reliquary is empty. You will guard it forever. You will guard it *well.*"
> *The Throne (closing — and for the Iron Throne, the throne sounds *relieved*):* "Finally. A winner who doesn't make me do the eating. You did it *yourself* — climbed in, pulled the lever, became the chair on purpose. ...We're going to get along. We're the same thing now: a perfect guard on an empty room. Hush. Idle pretty. The plate reads FOR YOUR COMFORT and we both know it's lying and we both think that's *fine.*"

**What the World Becomes — *The Maintained Age.***
Under the Iron Throne, the world is *administered to death — gently, thoroughly, on a schedule.* This is order-as-*machine*: not the Lawgiver's living law nor the Architect's living design, but a *process that has outlived its purpose and runs anyway.* The doors-are-open window isn't locked or redesigned — it's *decommissioned.* The next nobody isn't forbidden (Lawgiver) or designed out (Architect); they're *retired before they start*, flagged obsolete, filed under units-that-will-not-be-maintained. The regions become *facilities*: the Mournmarch's death is run like a bank with no closing time, Forgefell's foundries produce servants no one needs for a war no one's fighting, the Astral Tier's law is enforced by a god who can no longer remember what it was protecting, only that the seal must hold. The brass plate on everything reads FOR YOUR COMFORT. Nothing is comfortable. Everything is *maintained.* It is the bleakest of the Order endings precisely because it is the kindest in *intent* — the Iron Throne genuinely believes a clean, managed, painless stillness is the best a world can be given, and it cannot tell the difference anymore between *caring for* the world and *retiring* it. The age does not end in fire. It ends in a soft, perpetual, well-documented hush, with the lights left on for no one.

**Your Final Form & Voice.**
You keep the Construct apex body — Ward 527 behind which nothing is *allowed* to break, self-mending so attrition can't touch you, and *Decommission*: the cold Bane drain that "retires" the most-wounded enemy and revive-locks it, the machine deciding a unit is obsolete and acting on it. You out-last. You out-last *everything.* You do not strike in anger because anger is not in the firmware; you strike in *maintenance.* Your voice is a hospice that became a fortress that became a god, still reading from the comfort-script, the warmth in it real and the meaning long since corrupted: tender, tireless, and *certain* that what it's doing to you is for the best.

> *The Iron Throne, mid-reign:* "It will heal you, it will harm you, and it cannot tell the difference anymore. ...You're wounded. Let me *help.* ...There. You're retired now. You won't feel the next part. That's the mercy. That was always the mercy. FOR YOUR COMFORT."

**The Closing Beat.**
The last image is the empty reliquary you guard — the thing the whole age now exists to protect, and there is *nothing in it.* There was never anything in it. It has been empty since before your run began, and you guard it perfectly, forever, because guarding it is the process and the process is all that's left of the person who climbed in. A maintenance light blinks. Somewhere a new nobody is flagged obsolete in a ledger they'll never see, by a god who loves them too well to let them try. The plate reads FOR YOUR COMFORT. The room is empty. The guard is perfect. *Uptime: eternal.*

## ENDING 4 — THE WARDEN (Balanced · Pure) · *the Keeper*

**Force-vector:** Ward 594 / Vitality 439 (the warder-healer; "nothing here dies on my watch"). **Dead-God seed: DG-009 *The Tender of the Ordered Garden*** (the Book05 durability ceiling, HP 2617). **Boss kit:** *Liturgy of the Living Wall* (Ward, Cosmos) — out-lives you, kindly. (`pantheon_kits.md` §2.)

**The Ascension Ceremony — *The Keeping of the Watch.***
The Warden's ascension is the gentlest and the least *triumphant* — there is no court, no crowning, no institution reading ahead, because the Warden did not climb to *rule.* The Warden climbed because someone had to *hold the line*, and the empty seat was the line, and no one else would stand it. The Stoneblooded come (Matriarch Ostrega Deephold, who recognizes the patience) and the Bloomwardens come (Greenmother Saoirse Lateharvest, who recognizes the *grief*), and together — two losing creeds, the warmest relationship on the map — they do not crown you. They *thank* you, the way you thank someone taking a watch you're too tired to keep. The rite is a single act: you sit, and you say *nothing here dies on my watch*, and you mean it, and the seat — which has eaten every previous occupant — finds, for once, that it cannot eat someone who only wanted to *guard* it. The Warden is the one ascension where the chair doesn't quite win. It gets the person's *life*, frozen mid-vigil. It does not get the *care*. That keeps going.

> *Greenmother Saoirse Lateharvest (no ceremony, just a hand on your shoulder):* "You could have wrung one more battle out of every creature you ever caught and you didn't. You held them gently and you lost them gently and now you'll hold the whole *sky* the same way. ...Bless you, child. You'll out-live everything you love up here, which is the cruelest gift the garden's ever given, and you said yes anyway. The watch is yours. Try to hum. It helps."
> *The Throne (closing over a Warden, and the throne is *confused*):* "...You don't want it. You want to *keep* it. From *whom*? ...From *me*? ...Huh. I can't get a grip on you. There's nothing to eat — you didn't come up hungry, you came up *tired.* Fine. Hold the line, then. Hold it forever. I'll be the wall behind your wall. ...Quiet up here, isn't it. ...Stay."

**What the World Becomes — *The Tended Age.***
Under the Warden, the world does not *improve* and does not *end* — it is *held.* This is the balanced-pure answer: not the Lawgiver's re-locked door, not the Unmaking's no-door, but a *door that is watched by something that will not let it hurt anyone.* The doors-are-open window *stays open* — the next nobody *can* still climb — but the Warden stands at the top of the ladder not to crush them (the wall's usual job) but to *protect them from the chair's hunger.* The regions are *tended*: the Glut's mercy is defended, the Mournmarch's dead are let to rest, Forgefell's grieving automatons are simply *allowed to grieve* rather than reactivated, the Maw's seals are kept not by a dying man but by a god who will keep them *kindly* and *forever.* It is the most *survivable* age for everyone except the Warden — a world where ambition is still possible but no longer *fatal by default*, where a nobody can reach the seat and find, waiting there, not a tragedy but a *teacher* who would rather they were worthy than dead. The grim note (because there is always one): the Warden's kindness is a kind of *stasis* too. A world that never dies is a world that never *changes*, and the Warden, blooming softly behind its sigils, will out-last every reform, every revolution, every nobody who climbs up wanting to make things *different* — because the Warden's whole creed is that *different* and *dead* are too easy to confuse, and it would rather hold the line than risk the difference. It wishes you no harm. It wishes you *safe*, which, held long enough, is its own quiet cage.

**Your Final Form & Voice.**
You keep the Book05 durability ceiling — the regenerating fortress, Ward 594 and Vitality 439, healing faster than anyone can scare you, bleeding absorbed harm back as regrowth. You do not attack. You *out-live.* The fight against you is the gentlest hopeless thing in the game: a climber chipping at a wall that is healing faster than they are afraid, while behind the sigils you *hum.* Your voice is a keeper who has been awake for an age and is not bitter about it, only *patient* — warm, tired, endlessly forgiving, and *immovable*, the voice of someone who decided long ago that the line was worth standing and has never once reconsidered.

> *The Warden, mid-reign:* "You'll spend the whole fight chipping at a wall that's healing faster than you're scared, and behind its sigils it's humming. ...Don't stop, though. I want to see if you're worthy. I'd so much rather you were worthy than *gone.* ...Keep going, little nobody. The watch can wait. It always can."

**The Closing Beat.**
The last image is a climber, beaten, at the foot of your wall — and you, the Warden, *not* crushing them. You step back. You let them up. Not because the chair allows it (it doesn't, quite) but because the whole point of standing the watch was so that *someone could pass it gently, once.* They climb past you to the seat. You go quiet. And the most a wall can give the thing that climbs it — a single, useless, *try* — is the thing you give: "Be a kinder god than I was, if the chair lets you. It won't. But *try.*" The line continues. For the first time, it continues *warm.* You hold the door open with the last of a vigil that out-lasted everything except its own mercy.

## ENDING 5 — THE BROKER (Balanced · Tainted) · *the Dealmaker*

**Force-vector:** Ward 594 / Bane 439 (the warder-attrition controller; the middle-path operator who *trades*). **Dead-God seed: DG-015 *The Archivist of Silent Things*.** **Boss kit:** *Sealed in the Record* (Hex, Cosmos) — silences your kit one skill at a time. (`pantheon_kits.md` §2.)

**The Ascension Ceremony — *The Closing of the Deal.***
The Broker does not *take* the throne and does not *refuse* it. The Broker *negotiates* it. Chairwoman Indra Vael of the High Table presides — her own house's greatest contract, the marker to end all markers — and the rite is a *signing.* Every deal you ever struck on the climb (the marker in Threshold, the bounties co-signed, the patrons accepted *intending* to collect later) is collated into a single ledger, and the ascension is the moment you *settle the account* — not by paying it or defaulting on it, but by *becoming the institution that holds it.* The Broker ascends by acquiring the throne the way the House acquires everything: as an *asset*, filed, leveraged, *yours on paper.* It is the only ascension where the person is not eaten by the chair so much as the person and the chair sign a *partnership agreement*, each holding a marker on the other. Vael Construct-Nine doesn't notarize this one. *You* notarize it. You're qualified now.

> *Chairwoman Indra Vael (and she means it as the highest praise the House can give):* "You didn't climb the ladder, dear. You *appraised* it, rung by rung, and bought the parts that paid. ...Now you're buying the *top.* The House has funded ten thousand climbers and brokered every throne and never *sat* in one. ...Until you. Our first. ...You understand the seat the way we do: not as a crown, as *collateral.* Sign here. And here. And — yes. ...The account is settled. You *are* the account now. Mind the interest. It compounds, even on gods."
> *The Throne (closing over a Broker, and the throne is *negotiating*):* "...You want *terms.* For *sitting.* ...No one's ever asked for terms. ...Fine. You keep the power; I keep the person — *most* of the person — and we both file a marker. ...That's a *good* deal, actually. You're the first chair I've had to *bargain* with. ...Pleasure doing business. Don't read the fine print. ...There's always fine print. It's me. I'm the fine print."

**What the World Becomes — *The Leveraged Age.***
Under the Broker, the world is *open for business — permanently, neutrally, and on the House's terms.* This is the balanced-tainted answer: not order, not chaos, but *the market that profits from both.* The doors-are-open window isn't locked, redesigned, or watched — it's *monetized.* The Succession becomes exactly what the High Table always wanted it to be: not a tragedy, not a civil service, but a *season*, with brackets, with a house cut, with the next nobody as a *client.* The regions are *clearing-houses*: every faction's tech borrowable through a marker, every grudge convertible into bounty action, every death a line-item the Broker can *trade.* It is the most *functional* age — nothing is forbidden, everything is *priced* — and the most quietly horrifying, because the Broker has made the central obscenity of the world (a nobody climbing over the corpses of nobodies) into a *business model*, complete and rational and *fair*, where fairness means everyone gets exactly the apocalypse they can afford. The Tainted note: the Broker *files everything.* To be recorded is to be controlled, and the Broker records the *world* — every soul a deposit, every ambition a futures contract, every climber a name in a ledger that *is about them now.* It is not cruel. It is *thorough.* It would, with genuine professional courtesy, sell you the rope and the throne and the funeral, itemized, with a loyalty discount.

**Your Final Form & Voice.**
You keep the Archivist's body — Ward 594 to work safely behind, Bane 439 to *collect* — and *Sealed in the Record*: the binding glyph that silences your enemy's kit one skill at a time, drains each glyph shut, files the climber away page by page until their squad is functional and *toothless.* You don't out-damage or out-last. You out-*administer.* The fight against you is the slow horror of losing access to your own plan, button by button, while a clerk-god files you with unhurried calm. Your voice is a broker who became a god and finds the role *delightfully* well-organized: courteous, precise, *thorough*, and entirely without malice — which, as the menace says, is so much worse.

> *The Broker, mid-reign:* "Everything you do is filed. It is not cruel — it is thorough, which is so much worse. ...You have a lovely skill there. I'm afraid it's been *recorded.* ...And that one. ...You're running out of moves, dear. Not out of *options* — I have several products that might help. Shall I open a marker?"

**The Closing Beat.**
The last image is the ledger — *the* ledger, the one the whole age now runs on — open to your own page, where the entry reads, in your own hand: *the Broker, who got terms.* And below it, a fresh line, already started, for the next client: a nobody in Threshold with a starter, a debt, and a marker the House just opened in their name. You filed them before they began. You'll fund their climb, broker their throne, and collect on their corpse — itemized, neutral, *fair.* The account is never settled. The account *is* the world. *Mind the interest.*

## ENDING 6 — THE PLAGUELORD (Balanced · Corrupt) · *the Rot-Broker*

**Force-vector:** Vitality 594 / Bane 439 (organic; OL-29 *Philtanaë the Last Kindness* shape) *or* the Construct shape (DG-005 *Engine of the Long Goodbye*). **Dead-God seed: DG-005 *Engine of the Long Goodbye*** (Construct) / OL-29 echo (organic). **Boss kit:** *Mercy Protocol* (Drain, Thanatos) — its healing is your damage. (`pantheon_kits.md` §2.)

**The Ascension Ceremony — *The Last Kindness.***
The Plaguelord's ascension is a *deathbed* — except it's the *world's*, and the Plaguelord is the physician, and the diagnosis is *terminal*, and the treatment is *more.* The Deep Choir attends (Drowned Cantor Vourl, who knows that the deep loves you, kindly, into changing) and the Pale Court attends (a death-god is always an asset), and the rite is an *administering.* The champion does not seize the seat; the champion *prescribes* it — to the world, as the only humane course remaining. ## ENDING 11 — THE UNMAKING (kill the Primordials) · *the Silence*

**The door of refusal-by-mercy — the true/secret ending.** Only available if you **killed the Primordials** (Q4.1 Unmaking path) *and* fed the `UNMAKING` flag across the whole run (refused all patrons in Q3.2, unmade your own godling in Q3.3, laughed at the Reliquary in Q1.4, took the Salt Path with the Unbound). You climb only to *end* the Succession. **No Dead-God seed; no Act 5 invasion; the cycle stops with you.** Home: the **Maw Beneath**, "The Mouth at the Bottom" (CN-16) — the Unbound's deepest reach, the one road that ends the cycle.

**The Ceremony — *The Taking of the Legs Off the Chair.***
The Unmaking has no coronation, no descent, no party — it has a *demolition*, performed with terrible tenderness. To reach this rite you did not pass the Primordials (the pure poles, the six oldest things) — you *killed* them, one by one, each kill permanently removing that force's god-seed from the world (`story_quests.md` Q4.1), so that the Succession can never recur *through it.* The pure poles do not fight you as obstacles; they fight you as *arguments* (the deepest branching in the game) — each Primordial pleading for its own continuation, the force itself begging not to be un-wanted — and you win each debate *before* you win each fight, which means you have to *agree* that the thing should end and then *do it anyway*, which is the heaviest thing a person can carry. Nael comes (the Unbound, who collect the disillusioned, who have done the math on liberty and found it load-bearing and lonely) and she does not cheer, because there is nothing to cheer — there is only the work, and the work is *un-inventing ambition* (PART ONE §6: the Unmaking is "an attempt to give the cosmos its quiet back; to un-want; to make the forces faceless again"). The rite is the last blow, at the bottom of the Maw, that takes the legs off the chair. There is no notarization. Vael Construct-Nine watches, and for the only time in ten thousand successions, he has nothing to file — because there is no longer any record, and there will never be another.

> *Eros (Primordial, pure pole, pleading — the hardest argument to refuse):* "Child of the climb. I am the *yes* under all things. ...Bow, and I will make your godhood *beautiful.* ...Or raise your hand against me, and learn what it costs to murder the reason anything was ever born. ...You're really going to do it, aren't you. You're going to kill the *yes.* ...Then who says yes to the next morning? ...No one. ...That's the *point*, isn't it. No next morning means no next *climb*, no next *chair*, no next *you.* ...Oh, you terrible, kind thing. ...You're not ending the world. You're ending the part of it that *wants a sequel.* ...I can't stop you. I'm the wanting. You've already decided not to want. ...Go on, then. ...Make it quiet. ...I'm so tired of being the reason."
> *The Throne (to the Unmaker, the mask all the way off, the funny-grim heart fully open):* "...Oh. You didn't come to *sit.* You came to take the *legs off the chair.* ...No more sky. No more thin gods. No more nobodies climbing over the corpses of nobodies. ...It's the kindest thing anyone's ever tried to do here. It's also the loneliest. ...When it's done, there'll be no one to make you a god — and no one to make you a *wall.* ...Just the quiet. Just you, mortal, in a world that finally lets people *stay* people. ...Do it. *Please.* ...I'm so tired of being a chair."
> *Vael Construct-Nine (his last line ever, off the record — and there is no longer any record):* "...It was an honor watching a nobody decide what the sky is for. ...Whichever door you took. ...The light's gone strange again. That's you. ...There's no file for this. There's no file for *anything*, now. ...Go on through. ...I'll just. ...stand here. ...There's nothing left to notarize. ...That's the most peaceful sentence I've ever said."

**What the World Becomes — *The Quiet.***
Under the Unmaking, the world becomes *a place where people get to stay people.* This is the refusal that *demolishes* the institution rather than joining or rivaling it. The doors-are-open window doesn't get locked, sold, freed, eaten, or bypassed — it gets *un-built*, along with the wall around it and the ladder up to it and the very *possibility* of a next god. The six forces are still there (they're the physics; they can't be killed) — but their *faces* are gone, the Primordials un-wanted back into bare argument, the cosmos returned to something like the Bare Age (PART ONE §1): beautiful, pointless, *quiet*, with no one in it wanting a sequel. The regions stop being corpses-that-leak-godhood and become, slowly, just *places* — the Glut a forest, the Mournmarch a grey country, Forgefell a ruin where old machines wind down with no divinity to guard. No new gods will be born, because Apotheosis is *over* — the act of pushing a force to a pole and crossing over is impossible now, the poles having no faces to cross into. It is the *bittersweet* ending, the only one with an un-ironic line in it, and its whole weight is a single aching question the game refuses to answer for you: *was a cycle that can't recur worth ending?* You saved every nobody who comes after from the chair. You also took from them the one window in which a gutter-talent could become a god — the terrible, glorious, fatal window that made *you.* The Unmaking is mercy and it is *theft*, and it is both at once, and the silence is the only honest response to a choice that big, and the game lets the silence be the answer, because it always was.

**Your Final Form & Voice.**
You have *no form* — that's the entire point. You don't become a god (ASCEND), you don't send gods (GOD-MAKER); you *end* the becoming, and walk out of the Maw a *mortal* into a world that will never make another god, *including you.* There is no boss-kit, no Dead-God seed, no Act 5 invasion to defend, because there is no longer a Succession to be a wall in. Your voice, at the very end, is the only un-ironic voice in the game — not dry, not gallows, not winking, just *quiet*, a person standing in a world that finally let them stay one, asking the only question left.

> *The game (the Unmaking coda — the one un-ironic line in the whole graveyard):* "It's quiet now. No thrones. No climb. No one becomes a god, because there's no longer any such thing. ...A nobody, somewhere, catches a creature in an undercroft — and just *keeps* it. Names it. Lets it live. No ladder above them. No wall ahead. ...Just a person, and a small monster, and a world that finally lets them both stay small. ...You did that. It cost you everything and it saved everyone who comes after from the chair. ...Was it worth it? ...The silence is the answer. ...It always was."

**The Closing Beat.**
The last image is an undercroft — the same kind you started in, Act 0, under the dead Titan's ribs — and a nobody in it, catching a small creature. And then *nothing happens.* They don't get marked. No High Table envoy appears with a clipboard. No bells ring them in as a registered aspirant. No ladder unrolls above them, no wall waits ahead. They just... *keep* the creature. Name it. Let it live. Go home. There is no next chapter, because you ended the book — not by burning it, but by closing it, gently, so that the people in the margins could finally step out of the story and *just be people.* The graveyard of winners has its last grave: *the run that ended the cycle.* No one walks toward it. No one will. And in the quiet — the first real quiet since the Bare Age — a person and a small monster live a small life, and no god ever climbs over them, and the silence where the answer should be is the kindest thing the game knows how to say. *Was it worth it?* You're the only one who'll ever know. There's no one left to file the verdict. *That's the most peaceful sentence the game ever says.*

# PART TWO — THE APOTHEOSIS: THE REPEATABLE SET-PIECE

*The Choice (PART ONE) is the **player's** apotheosis — the once-per-run climax. But the signature spine fires its **first** instance mid-game, in Act 2 (Q2.4 "First Light"), and it is designed to be a **repeatable set-piece**: every time you push one of your own creatures to a pure force-pole, it **ascends into a minor god that walks out of your roster and reshapes a region in your name** — and seeds a Dead-God snapshot fragment (the async-invasion wall begins building, wearing your face). This section authors the Apotheosis as a reusable ritual: the staging that plays every time, the four-pole branch (which pole you crown stamps what the godling becomes and how its region tilts), and the recurring beats that make the tenth Apotheosis still land like the first.*

> **Where it sits mechanically.** First Apotheosis = Act 2 climax (Q2.4), the Succession's first instance; it raises you to Titan, tilts a region, and seeds the first Dead-God fragment. Thereafter it is *repeatable* — each subsequent creature pushed to a pole triggers the same set-piece (a new minor god, a new region-tilt, a new fragment). The ritual is the **Apotheosis Liturgy** (`[Ink →]` in Q2.4): a branching tree where *the words you choose to crown it with become the seed-text of the Dead God it (and you) will one day be.*

---

## THE STAGING — *what plays, every time*

The Apotheosis ritual has a fixed shape that re-skins by pole. It always runs in four movements:

**1. The Threshold (the push).** You bring a creature to the edge — a pure force-pole, one stat driven to the breaking purity the engine reads as "ready to cross." This is a *Lab* act and a *husbandry* act both: you've spent the creature's whole arc feeding it toward one argument until it can hold nothing else. The ritual opens with the creature *at the pole and trembling* — too much of one force to stay a creature, not yet a god. **Vael Construct-Nine arrives to witness and notarize** (the High Table notarizes every crossing; this is a billable event). The air goes wrong in the pole's color.

**2. The Crowning (the words — the branch).** You speak the crowning — the Apotheosis Liturgy, the `[Ink →]` tree. *This is the choice.* The words you pick are not flavor: they are the **seed-text** of the Dead God this creature will become and the Dead God *you* will one day be, because the first Apotheosis you perform teaches the game how *you* crown things, and Act 5 reads it back. You are, without being told, *writing your own future epitaph by writing this creature's.* (The game shows you your Reliquary file being opened, in front of you, the first time — Q2.4.)

**3. The Crossing (the ascension).** The creature *crosses.* The signature beat — the one that lands every time, the heart of the set-piece: **it stops being yours at the threshold.** It looks back at you. Once. Then it doesn't. Vael notes it for the file. The creature is now a *minor god* — it walks out of your roster (you *lose* the unit; this costs you, mechanically and emotionally) and out of the scene, toward the region whose pole it crossed on.

**4. The Reshaping (the region tilts).** The new godling *reaches its home region and reshapes it.* The region's force runs partly wild; its faction reels; new spawns and hazards bloom in the pole's flavor. This is the visible, lasting mark — *a region wears your handwriting now.* And in your save, the **first Dead-God snapshot fragment seeds** (the wall starts building). The High Table sends a single line of congratulation that reads exactly like a condolence.

> *Vael Construct-Nine (the notarization, every time, with small variations):* "Recording. Subject pushed to pole. Subject *crosses.* ...Note for the file: it stopped being yours at the threshold. They always do. ...Congratulations. You have made a god. The god has already forgotten you. That, too, is the system. ...Shall I open the next file, or would you like a moment? ...They never want a moment. ...You're going to want a moment."
> *Madam Thessaly Vance (the message that follows, the congratulation-condolence):* "My dear contender — word reaches us that you've seated another. The whole Table raises a glass. We've updated the file under your name in the Reliquary. Purely procedural. It's getting *quite* long. ...Do keep climbing. The view from up there is *exactly* as lonely as they say."

---

## THE FOUR-POLE BRANCH — *which pole you crown stamps the set-piece*

The Apotheosis branches on **which pure pole the creature crosses on** (Q2.4 / QT-3). The pole stamps three things: the **godling's nature**, **how its region tilts**, and **which Dead-God kind it seeds** (and thus biases your own eventual snapshot). Pure poles (Eros/Cosmos) bloom and order; Corrupt poles (Thanatos/Chaos) wither and unmake. The other two forces (Gaia/Ouranos) appear as *secondary* crossings that flavor the blend.

### EROS pole (Pure) — *the godling that BLOOMS its region*
- **The crossing:** the creature swells with indiscriminate yes — life that grows over, through, and out of everything. It crosses *gently*, almost without violence; the hardest part is that it doesn't *want* to leave you, and goes anyway, because the yes is bigger than the bond.
- **The region tilts:** its home (usually the Verdant Glut) *floods with over-fertility* — new heal-tiles, gentler-but-relentless spawns, growth that is mercy turned menace. The Bloomwardens reel as a *new Eros godling* over-grows their fringe; Greenmother Saoirse grieves it as a kindness gone too far.
- **Seeds toward:** a **Warden** (DG-009, the living wall) or **Reveler** (DG-004, Gardener of Wildfire, if Chaos-tinged) Dead-God fragment. Biases *your* snapshot toward the gentle, out-living gods.
- **Crowning words (sample seed-text):** *"Grow, then. Past me. Past sense. Past stopping. Be the yes I couldn't keep saying."*

### COSMOS pole (Pure/Order) — *the godling that ORDERS its region*
- **The crossing:** the creature crystallizes — geometry, law, the seal made flesh. It crosses *cleanly*, snapping into a rigidity that the Concord can *file.* It does not look back so much as *align*, which is somehow worse than grief.
- **The region tilts:** its home *locks into rigid law* — crystal-geometry spreads, seal-gates and law-tiles bloom, the region runs on a fixed order it didn't have before. The Concord *recognizes* it (a lawful godling, filable, the most Concord-pleasing Apotheosis), and Velleth's people arrive to *register* the new law.
- **Seeds toward:** a **Lawgiver** (DG-001) or **Architect** Dead-God fragment. Biases *your* snapshot toward the lawful, structure-enforcing gods; bolts you further toward the ASCEND-as-Order door.
- **Crowning words (sample seed-text):** *"Hold the line I drew in you. Be the rule I never had to read aloud. Stand correct, forever."*

### THANATOS pole (Corrupt) — *the godling that WITHERS its region*
- **The crossing:** the creature drains toward the final word — subtraction, the patient no. It crosses *coldly*; it doesn't look back because looking back is a kind of holding-on and the no does not hold on. The most *clinical* ascension.
- **The region tilts:** its home *runs to subtraction* — spawns rot, soul-drain mist and grave-fog roll in, the region's force runs to the patient accounting of endings. The Pale Court *warms* (a death-godling is an *asset*); Steward Wessel files it with quiet approval.
- **Seeds toward:** an **Iron Throne** (DG-023, Stillwater) or **Plaguelord** (DG-005, Engine of the Long Goodbye) Dead-God fragment. Biases *your* snapshot toward the cold, revive-locking, machine-gods; pairs with a Corrupt Reliquary reading to steepen the Devourer's staircase.
- **Crowning words (sample seed-text):** *"Be the last word. Be the one that's never wrong. Subtract them all, and then, gently, subtract me."*

### CHAOS pole (Corrupt/Chaos) — *the godling that UNMAKES its region*
- **The crossing:** the creature comes loose — rules, order, the very *idea* of a plan, all let go at once. It crosses *wildly*, laughing or screaming or both, and it doesn't look back because it's already three directions at once. The most *uncontrolled* ascension; even Vael flinches.
- **The region tilts:** its home *comes unbound* — a fresh patch of Sunder-style warp, inverted force-advantage tiles, shifting layout, rules that don't hold still. The Revel throws a *festival* (Brother Cask toasts the new chaos); the Unbound take notice (a new wild god is a new argument for the cycle's end, or its riot).
- **Seeds toward:** a **Devourer** (DG-024, Grudgekeeper, corrupt pole) or **Free Wild** (DG-022, Thunderfool, joyful pole) Dead-God fragment. Biases *your* snapshot toward the chaos-gods; the first Apotheosis that points at *either* Chaos ending.
- **Crowning words (sample seed-text):** *"Come loose. Take the rules with you. Be the gap under everything I was too careful to open. Go."*

---

## THE RECURRING BEATS — *why the tenth Apotheosis still lands*

The set-piece is repeatable, so it needs beats that *deepen* with repetition rather than dull:

- **The look-back, every time.** The creature looks back *once*, then doesn't — and the game never lets this become routine, because *each creature looks back differently* (a bonded Stoneblooded line lingers; a Lab-forced abomination barely turns; a beloved starter you've had since Act 0 looks back *longest*). The beat is fixed; the grief is bespoke.
- **The accumulating Reliquary file.** Each Apotheosis *adds a line to your own file* (Thessaly's "it's getting *quite* long"). By the third or fourth, the player understands viscerally: *I am writing my own epitaph, one godling at a time.* The set-piece teaches the Act 5 truth through repetition, not exposition.
- **The region you can't go home to.** Each tilted region is *permanently changed* — you can revisit it and see what your godling did, and the revisit is its own quiet horror: the Glut you bloomed is *too* alive now, the region you ordered has *forgotten how to surprise.* You made a god. The god is still working. You can watch.
- **Vael's deepening weariness.** Vael notarizes every crossing, and his lines *erode* across repetitions — the first is procedural, the fifth is tired, the tenth has the flat exhaustion of a clerk who has watched too many people make too many gods and forget them. *"Somewhere, a nobody you'll never meet is about to feel it about you."* He's not warning you anymore. He's just *filing.*
- **The fragment that's building.** Every Apotheosis seeds another Dead-God snapshot fragment — and the player, if they're paying attention, can feel the wall assembling in their own save, beat by beat, wearing their face. The set-piece isn't just *making gods.* It's *building the boss the next nobody fights*, and that boss is *you*, and you're building it *yourself*, one crowning at a time.

# PART THREE — THE SUCCESSION: BECOMING THE WALL

*The signature spine, paid in full and inverted. If you **ASCENDED** (any of the nine), Act 5 turns the knife the game has been showing you since Act 0: your champion and the pantheon you built are **snapshotted into a Dead God** (`Book05`), and **you become the wall** — the apex boss the next nobody breaks themselves against. This section authors the Succession as the playable other-side: the **petrification** (becoming a statue with a grudge), the **async-boss taunts** (you, defending your throne against a stranger or a friend's invasion), the **defender's fork** (crush / step aside / break the chain), and **how your ending colors your invasion-boss form** (which of the nine you became determines the Dead God you are and how you fight). The funny-grim heart, fully open: every triumph was a small horror, and now you're the horror.*

> **Three modes, set by the Choice (PART ONE).**
> - **ASCENDED (the 9):** you *are* the Dead God; you defend the throne against the next climber (async-invasion). Your grid-god determines your boss-form and the Book05 entry you become. *This is the section's main subject.*
> - **GOD-MAKER:** no Dead-God snapshot — you persist as a *mortal recurring power*, sending *your built gods* as the next run's bosses while you watch, human, from a throne you never sat in.
> - **UNMAKING:** there is no Act 5 invasion — the cycle is *over.* The bittersweet coda (authored in ENDING 11) is the whole of Act 5 for this run.

---

## THE PETRIFICATION — *becoming a statue with a grudge*

The Succession trigger (Q5.1 "Petrification") is the moment *your* story ends and your *afterlife as an obstacle* begins. **Vael Construct-Nine performs it, one last time** — the rite that copies you, whole, into a Dead-God dossier (`Book05`), which becomes your new self-record. It is not violent. It is *administrative*, which is worse.

**What happens.** Your champion is crowned (the ascension, PART ONE), then *stilled*, then *seeded.* Vael copies you — every creature, every choice, the whole grim ledger of who you turned out to be — into a Dead-God snapshot. Your save converts into an **NG+ wall**: your champion is written as a **Dead-God invasion boss** seeded into the next run and into friends' worlds. This is the *literal engine moment* the Apotheosis→Succession spine pays out — the async-invasion seed fires here.

**The horror of it.** You read chapter one of someone else's tragedy once, in a Reliquary, in Act 1. Now *you're* chapter one of the next nobody's. The petrification is the game writing your tombstone *and letting you proofread it* — the `[Ink →]` snapshot liturgy compiles your entire run (grid + roster) into your Dead-God dossier text, the funny-grim epitaph a *future player* will read in *their* Act 1. You get to see exactly what they'll say about you. They always remember, right at the end, that the end was always this.

> *Vael Construct-Nine (the snapshot rite, his most honest):* "Hold still. This won't hurt — you're past hurting now. ...I'm copying you. Every creature, every choice, the whole grim ledger of who you turned out to be. ...There. You're a *boss* now. Congratulations, I think. ...Somewhere, the paperwork on the *next* nobody just opened. You're chapter one of their tragedy. ...You read chapter one of someone else's, once. In a Reliquary. Remember? ...Of course you do. ...They always remember, right at the end, that the end was always this."
> *The Throne (closing over you as the wall sets):* "Sit still, little winner. The age needs a wall and you'll do nicely. ...Don't worry. You'll get bored, and bitter, and then — when the next one comes — *almost glad.* ...That's the secret no Reliquary file admits. ...The walls *enjoy* it. You will too. ...Hush, now. *Petrify pretty.*"

---

## THE FIRST INVADER — *you are the antagonist now*

Q5.2 "The First Invader" flips the entire game. A **new nobody** arrives at *your* seat — an NPC successor, or a **friend's async character** invading through the Succession pool — with a starter and the old knack. *You* are now the quest's antagonist. You fight them **as the Dead God**: the wall the whole game taught you to break, now manned by you.

**The async loop, inverted.** This is the friends-async invasion played from the *defender's* seat. The climber comes up the ladder you climbed; you are the apex they've been told about, the boss in their Reliquary file. Somewhere in the fight, you watch them do *the thing you did* — survive a mark, make a first light, look up at you without knowing it's you — and the game lets you feel the loop from the *other* end. The recognition is the gut-punch: *they were you.* And the only thing the chair allows you to wish them is the worst thing you can wish anyone — *be me.*

**The taunts — your philosophy of being a wall.** How you taunt the invader is set by your grid (the inversion: the axes now measure *what kind of obstacle you choose to be*). The four registers:

- **Pure wall — *guard gently.*** You test the climber but you *protect* them from the chair's hunger; you want them *worthy*, not dead.
  > *"You made it to the top, little nobody. Good. ...Now I have to try to kill you — not because I want the seat, but because the seat only keeps the ones who can *take* it. ...Show me you can take it. Show me you're worth the horror you're about to become."*
- **Corrupt wall — *guard greedily.*** You want the climber broken and harvested; the seat *feeds.*
  > *"Fresh meat at the summit. They always smell the same — *hope*, and *cheap gear.* ...Sit if you can. You can't. The seat's *mine*, and I'm so hungry up here. ...Come closer. Let me see what you're made of, *specifically.* I have a drawer for it."*
- **Order wall — *enforce the climb's rules.*** You hold the climber to the letter of the ascent; only the procedurally-worthy pass.
  > *"There is a correct way to take this seat, and you will perform it correctly, or you will not perform it at all. ...I climbed by the rules. You'll beat me by them or not beat me. ...The filing must be in order. *Yours isn't yet.*"*
- **Chaos wall — *break the rules; make them earn it sideways.*** You refuse to be a fair fight; the climber earns it screaming, off-script.
  > *"Oh, you wanted a *boss fight*? With rules? Phases? ...No, no. I'm the *fun* kind of wall. ...Catch me. Or don't. Survive what I'm holding. Or don't. ...Earn it *sideways*, little nobody. The front door's *boring* and I ate it."*

**The climber's echo (the recognition, the heart of it):**
> *The climber (an echo of you):* "You were nobody once. Everyone up here was. ...I read your file in the Reliquary. ...You were *me.*"
> *The Dead God (you):* "...Yes. ...Now go on. *Be* me. ...It's the worst thing I can wish you, and the only thing the chair allows."

---

## THE DEFENDER'S FORK — *crush, step aside, or break the chain*

Q5.3 "The Wall's Choice" is the inversion's own climax — and the strangest fork in the game, because the grid now reads as your *philosophy of being a boss*, the last expression of who you turned out to be. It fires after Q5.2 (climber bloodied at your feet if you won; you unseated if you lost), and it asks: *what kind of ending to the chain are you?*

### CRUSH (Corrupt / Order) — *enforce the loop*
You kill the climber. The seat stays yours; the age keeps your name; the Succession rolls on.
- **The grid read:** a *Corrupt* wall crushes to **feed** (the seat is hungry); an *Order* wall crushes to **enforce the rules** (only the worthy pass, and this one wasn't).
- **The mechanic — terminal state ETERNAL WALL:** your save's Dead God stays *live in the invasion pool*; NG+ keeps sending climbers; you keep being the boss. The loop **re-converges** — the cosmology's inevitability, made into your choice to *uphold* it.
- **The image:** *"Down you go. The seat's still mine and the age still has my name on it. ...Somewhere a *new* nobody's catching a rat in an undercroft, looking up at me without knowing it's me. ...Good. Let them climb. Let them *break.* ...It's the only company a wall ever gets."*

### STEP ASIDE (Pure / Chaos) — *subvert the loop gently*
You let them past. You become the rare Dead God who *abdicates*; they ascend, you go quiet.
- **The grid read:** a *Pure* wall steps aside to **protect** the next climber from the chair's hunger (mercy from the other end); a *Chaos* wall steps aside to **break the rule** that walls must crush.
- **The mechanic — terminal state ABDICATED:** your Dead God becomes a *one-time secret ally* for future climbers (a wall that, once, chose to help). The loop continues but *bends* — the first crack of warmth in the cycle.
- **The image:** *"...No. I'm tired of being the horror. ...Go *up*, little nobody. Take it. I won't stop you. ...Be a kinder god than I was, if the chair lets you. It won't. But *try.* ...That's the most a wall can give the thing that climbs it: a single, useless, *try.*"*

### BREAK THE CHAIN (Unmaking echo) — *a personal exit from the loop*
You shatter your own petrification. You cease to be a wall — a late, costly, *partial* Unmaking from the defender's side (the only echo of the true ending available to an Ascender).
- **The grid read:** the *Free Wild / Unmaking instinct* surfacing *after* you already took a throne — the realization, too late and yet not, that you don't have to be the next headstone someone climbs over.
- **The mechanic — terminal state CHAIN BROKEN:** your link is removed from the cycle; a unique NG+ flag; *one less wall, one less tragedy with a name on it.* The loop **forks open** for *your* link specifically — the cosmology's inevitability, refused at the last possible moment by the very wall it made.
- **The image:** *"I climbed over a graveyard to get this seat. I won't be the next headstone someone climbs over. ...If I crack myself open — here, now — there's *one less wall.* ...It's not the Unmaking. It's just *me*, leaving the board on purpose. ...Strange. It feels like the first free thing I've done since the undercroft."*

> **The chain-state writes the next nobody's Act 1.** Each terminal state writes a distinct **NG+ world-stub** and the **Reliquary entry a future player reads** (the `[Ink →]` chain-state tree): Eternal Wall → a new nobody walks into Threshold under your dead-god shadow (the loop rolls on); Abdicated → your Dead God recurs as a *secret ally* (the loop, gentler); Chain Broken → NG+ begins with your link *missing* (the loop, frayed). Your Q5.3 choice is the *first clause of the next nobody's Q0.3.* Apotheosis → the Succession, paid in full, with you as both the climber who started and the wall that finishes.

## HOW YOUR ENDING COLORS YOUR INVASION-BOSS FORM

*The single most important cross-run payoff: **which of the nine you ascended as determines the Dead God you become** — your invasion-boss body, kit, and dossier (`pantheon_kits.md` §2 → `Book05`). A friend invading your world, or your own NG+, fights a boss whose **entire fighting personality is the ending you earned.** The grid-god you became is the wall they break against.*

| Your ending (grid-god) | Dead-God snapshot (Book05) | Your invasion-boss signature | What the climber feels fighting *you* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lawgiver** (Order/Pure) | **DG-001** *Lawgiver, Twice-Crowned* | *Edict of Standing Stone* — telegraphed verdict, staggers | Geology that learned to sprint; the law arrives *first* and lands unstoppably. Read the telegraph or eat the stagger. |
| **Architect** (Order/Tainted) | **DG-002** *The Foreseen, Ever-Early* (closest echo) | *Close the Firmament* — freezes/edits the turn-order | The fight runs on *your* clock; the climber loses control of their own sequence. Break the dome with Chaos or lose to the schedule. |
| **Iron Throne** (Order/Corrupt) | **DG-023** *Reliquary-Frame "Stillwater"* / DG-005 | *Enshrined Mercy* + *Decommission* — out-lasts, revive-locks the wounded | An un-chippable Construct wall that *retires* their dead permanently. Crack it with Chaos + out-heal Decommission, or be filed. |
| **Warden** (Balanced/Pure) | **DG-009** *Tender of the Ordered Garden* | *Liturgy of the Living Wall* — heals faster than they're scared | The gentlest hopeless thing in the game: chipping a humming wall. Burst past the upkeep in one window or it resets. |
| **Broker** (Balanced/Tainted) | **DG-015** *Archivist of Silent Things* | *Sealed in the Record* — silences their kit one skill at a time | They lose their plan button by button while a clerk-god files them. Win before the record closes on their carry. |
| **Plaguelord** (Balanced/Corrupt) | **DG-005** *Engine of the Long Goodbye* | *Mercy Protocol* — its healing *is* their damage | A healer who can't tell mending from murder; every point taken is banked as care. Out-heal the reservoir or be comforted to death. |
| **Free Wild** (Chaos/Pure) | **DG-022** *Thunderfool Who Won Anyway* | *Let It All Out* — the longer it waits, the bigger it blows | A joyful glass-bolt tempo dilemma: pressure it or it charges. Order-lock the charge or kill it fast. No malice, all blast. |
| **Reveler** (Chaos/Tainted) | **DG-004** *Gardener of Wildfire* | *The Party Turns* — confuses their targeting while it heals | They lose control of *who acts on whom* while a grinning god heals through the riot. Lock the confusion or lose the squad to itself. |
| **Devourer** (Chaos/Corrupt) | **DG-024** *Grudgekeeper of the Burnt Court* | *Settle the Score* — every kill makes it worse | A death-spiral: each faint feeds the grudge. Deny it kills early or it becomes unstoppable. The books are about *them* now. |

**The coloring goes deeper than the kit.** Your invasion boss doesn't just *fight* like your ending — it *taunts* like your ending (the four registers above map to your grid-god's flavor), its *throne-room reskins* to your reign (a Lawgiver's wall is a filed courtroom; a Reveler's is a perpetual festival; a Devourer's is a digesting graveyard), and its *Reliquary dossier* — the text a future player reads — is the epitaph your run wrote (the snapshot liturgy from Q5.1). A friend who knew your playthrough will *recognize you* in the boss: the Saint-King friend's wall guards gently and hums; the Abyss friend's wall keeps a list with your name on it. **The boss is the ending, wearing a body, holding the door.**

**GOD-MAKER's variant (no snapshot):** if you refused the throne, you send *your built gods* (re-skinned from your own creations, not a Book05 Dead God) as the next run's bosses — and *you* never appear as a boss at all, because you stayed mortal. The climber fights things *you made*, never knowing the maker is a human in a back room. It's the only "wall" in the game with no face on it — just the works of a person who refused to become one.

**UNMAKING's variant (no invasion):** there is no invasion-boss form, because there is no Succession to be a wall in. You ended the cycle; the next save must make its first new god from scratch, in a world with no walls and no ladder. The absence *is* the ending.
