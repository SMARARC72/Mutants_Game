# PART ONE — DEEPER COSMOLOGY: HOW THE GODS DIED AND WHY MORTALS CAN ASCEND

*Six rich passages. The premise that makes the Succession possible — written so the whole game's central obscenity (a nobody can become a god because gods were only ever nobodies) has a mythology under it, not just a rule.*

---

## 1. The Age Before — when the forces had no faces

There was a first age, and it had no gods in it, because it had no *faces* in it. Before there were thrones there were only the **six forces** — Gaia the weight, Ouranos the speed, Cosmos the order, Chaos the unmaking, Eros the yes, Thanatos the no — and they were not *beings*, they were the **physics**: the bare substrate of a reality that had not yet bothered to mean anything. They did not want. They did not war. They simply *were*, in their three eternal arguments — weight against speed, order against ruin, life against ending — and the arguing was the world, and the world was beautiful and pointless and very, very quiet.

The Stoneblooded, who are the oldest culture that still keeps a memory, call this the **Bare Age**, and their one creation-hymn is mostly a list of things that did not exist yet (it takes nine verses to get to "and there was no one to sing this"). The Concord calls it the **Unfiled Age** and finds it faintly distressing — a cosmos with no records, no covenants, no accountability, just forces doing as they pleased with no form to ask permission of. The Unbound call it the only honest age there ever was, and Nael, when she's feeling unkind, calls it *home*.

What ended the Bare Age was the worst thing that can happen to a force: it learned to **want a shape**. And once one of them did, they all had to.

---

## 2. The First Faces — the Primordials, and the original sin of wanting

The **six Primordials** are what the forces became when they grew faces. They are not separate from the forces — **Gaia the Primordial *is* Gaia the force, at full and terrible purity** (this is why the codex gives them a single towering stat at 1400 and paper everywhere else: a Primordial is one argument, won absolutely, and nothing else). They are the oldest beings that ever were, near-unkillable, and they are the *ceiling* of everything that climbs — the pure poles at the top of Apotheosis.

The myth all six factions agree on (which is itself unsettling, because they agree on nothing): the Primordials' original act was to **want to continue**. A force does not care whether it persists — but a *face* does. The moment the forces grew faces, they grew the one thing the Bare Age never had: a stake in tomorrow. Eros wanted there to *keep being* life to say yes to. Thanatos wanted there to keep being endings to be the final word of. Cosmos wanted the rules to *hold*, which means it wanted there to keep being a next moment for the rules to hold *in*. Every Primordial, the instant it had a face, looked at the quiet beautiful pointless cosmos and thought, for the first time, *more.*

This is the world's actual fall — older than the Titans, older than the Olympians, the **root the whole Succession grows from**. The Primordials invented *ambition*, which is just a force wanting to continue past its turn. And ambition, once it exists, cannot be uninvented — it can only be **inherited, downward, forever**, from the Primordials to the Titans to the Olympians to the gutter-talent squatting under a dead god's ribcage with a starter and a plan. When the priests call your ambition blasphemy, they are technically correct: you are repeating the original sin of the universe. When the Unbound say the only mercy is to *end the cycle*, what they mean — the thing Nael won't quite say in front of strangers — is that the Unmaking is an attempt to give the cosmos its quiet back. To un-want. To make the forces faceless again.

> *Madam Cessil (Threshold astrologer, 4th-wall-adjacent), reading this to a customer:* "The gods didn't sin by being cruel, dear. They sinned by wanting a *sequel*. ...So do you. So does whoever's watching you want one. We're all just the universe, refusing to stop. Two drachma."

---

## 3. The Titans and the First Fall — why the regions are corpses, and why they fell *first*

The **Titans** came next: the children of the Primordials, the first beings to be *blends* rather than pure poles (Cronus is Chaos+Thanatos; Rhea is Gaia+Eros; Oceanus is Ouranos+Eros; Hyperion is Ouranos+Chaos — and the buried ones under Titanfall, Tartaron and Krathonar, and **Mordathun the Cairn-King**, the Old Weight, who is canon-ratified as Gaia/Thanatos). A blend is a *compromise* — two forces holding hands instead of one force winning — and the Titans were therefore the first beings who could be **negotiated with**, **bargained against**, and, crucially, **killed**, because a thing made of two forces has a *seam* where they meet, and a seam can be broken.

The Titans **fell first** — before the Olympians, in a war the world has mostly forgotten — and this is the single most load-bearing fact in the geography, because **their corpses are the regions.** Titanfall is the most literal: a range of corpse-peaks where the buried sternum of Mordathun forms a plateau and the deeper strata stop being rock and start, unmistakably, being *flesh* that still dreams. But it is true of all of them, refracted: the dead Titans are the *bedrock* the dead Olympians later fell *onto*, the older layer of corpse under the newer one. (The Tideless grinds against the "Old Weight of the dead sea-Titan" below its trench; the Maw Beneath is the sump where the spilled force of *both* dead generations finally collects and rots.) The world is not one graveyard. It is a graveyard built on an **older** graveyard, and the Stoneblooded are the only ones who still tend the lower graves, because they are the only ones old enough to remember they're there.

**How the Titans died** is the Bare Age's argument made lethal. The Primordials, having invented wanting, wanted *successors who would not replace them* — and the Titans, being blends, being *negotiable*, being able to die, were the first generation to be expendable. The myth (kept clearest by the Last Titan-Priest, Mund Graveweight, who worships a corpse whose name fell off) is that the Primordials did not kill the Titans so much as **let them be killable** — built the seam into them on purpose, so that when the Titans inevitably reached for *more* (the inherited sin), there would be a way to put them down. The first deicide in history was a *Primordial permitting a Titan to be murdered by the next thing in line.* The ladder was load-bearing from the very first rung. Every Titan that became a mountain was a winner who got climbed.

---

## 4. The Olympians and the Second Fall — gods who were *almost* mortal, and the death of the Pantheon

The **Olympians** — the named force-vectors of the current Pantheon, your **33 god-rank creatures**, Zeus through Persephone — are the generation *after* the Titans, and the generation *closest to you*. This is the part the Reliquary truth hinges on. The Olympians are not pure poles (Primordials) or vast elder blends (Titans). They are **tight, specific, two-force vectors with a god-budget stat shape** (BST 1549 — one stat 594, a secondary 439, the rest minimal): Zeus is Ouranos+Cosmos, Demeter is Eros+Gaia, Hades is Thanatos+Gaia, Hephaestus is Cosmos+Gaia (the Construct patron), Persephone is Eros+Thanatos — *the moral axis incarnate.* They are, in other words, **shaped exactly like a strong ascended mortal would be shaped** — because that is precisely what they are.

Here is the obscenity the priests never say aloud, the thing the Reliquary of Winners exists to make you read: **the Olympians were mortals who climbed.** Every god in the empty sky has a *before* — a gutter, a debt, a first catch in some undercroft. They climbed the same ladder you are on, reached a pure-enough pole, ascended into a god that reshaped its region, and **sat down on a throne — and the chair ate the person and kept the power.** The Pantheon is not a different kind of being from you. The Pantheon is a graveyard of *previous you's*. (The codex files them as "Successors." The honest word, as Archivist Pellos Vane puts it, is "meals.")

So **how did the Pantheon die?** Three ways, all of them the same way:
- **Some were killed** — by the next nobody up the ladder, exactly as the Titans were killed by them. Deicide is not a crime in this cosmos; it is the *promotion mechanism.*
- **Some ascended and walked out** — climbed *past* their own throne toward the Primordials or some further door, and simply left, abandoning the seat. (These are the most dangerous to think about: gods who found the throne and decided it wasn't the top.)
- **Some are still technically sitting** — but the chair has eaten so much of the person that what remains is "a thing that used to be people," running the office on muscle-memory and grudge. The "standing Pantheon" that turns its head toward you in Act 3 is mostly *this*: not living gods, but occupied thrones, husks with power, the divine equivalent of the Forgefell automaton still guarding a door whose wall is long gone.

The Pantheon died of **the same ambition that made it** — each god the meal of the next, the whole sky a slow cannibalism dressed up as a succession of dynasties. When you learn the Succession truth and your face does the math, the math is this: *the gods are not above you. The gods are a warning about what's above you, written in the corpses of people exactly like you, and the warning is "don't," and you are absolutely going to anyway.*

## 5. What the Forces *Are*, Mythically — the six arguments the world is still having

Mechanically the six forces are stats (Gaia→Bulk, Ouranos→Celerity, Cosmos→Ward, Chaos→Spike, Eros→Vitality, Thanatos→Bane). Mythically they are the **six arguments the cosmos started in the Bare Age and has never finished**, and every creature, region, god, and aspirant is a *position* in those arguments. The funny-grim heart of the cosmology is that the forces are not good or evil — they are *load-bearing*, and the horror is in what each one costs:

- **GAIA (the weight) — "Stay."** The argument that things should *endure*, refuse to move, outlast. Gaia is the patience of stone and the stubbornness of the grave. Its cost: it cannot let go. A Gaia-soul buries armies by simply still being there when they're gone. (The Stoneblooded creed; the Old Weight that remembers everything because it has nothing to do but remember.)
- **OURANOS (the speed) — "Go first."** The argument that to be high is to be free and to stand still is to fall. Ouranos is altitude, motion, the law of *before*. Its cost: nothing it loves stays still long enough to keep. (The Storm Vault; the High Table's whole business is access, and access is just being *first* through a door.)
- **COSMOS (the order) — "Hold the line."** The argument that there should be *rules*, that a thing should be *this far and no further*, that variance is the enemy. Cosmos is the seal, the ward, the binding, the law that keeps the world's edges from fraying into the Sunder. Its cost: it forbids the wild, and the wild is where life keeps its surprises. (The Concord; the Astral Tier where nothing unexpected has happened in four hundred years, which is the most frightening sentence in the game.)
- **CHAOS (the unmaking) — "Let go."** The argument that rules are a prison, that the only honest state is *loose*, that order is a lie the universe tells to feel safe. Chaos is the one force that *spreads* rather than settles. Its cost: it cannot build, only free, and freedom with nothing under it is a fall. (The Sunder; the Unbound, who have done the math on liberty and found it *load-bearing* and *lonely*.)
- **EROS (the yes) — "Continue."** The argument that there should *keep being more* — more growth, more life, more bond, the terrible indiscriminate *yes* of fertility that grows over the dead, through the dead, out of the dead, and calls it healing. Its cost: it doesn't know when to *stop*, and life that won't stop is the Verdant Glut's digestive deep — mercy turned menace. (The Bloomwardens, the warmest and most doomed.)
- **THANATOS (the no) — "Enough."** The argument that things should *end*, that the final word should be *spoken and never wrong*, that the patient subtraction is the only honest accounting. Its cost: it cannot tell the difference between an ending that's earned and one that's merely *due*. (The Pale Court, who run death like a bank; the Mournmarch, the quiet after.)

The **three opposed pairs** (Gaia⇄Ouranos, Cosmos⇄Chaos, Eros⇄Thanatos) are the three arguments themselves, and fusing an opposed pair is *taboo / abomination* because it forces two truths the cosmos swore would never touch to hold hands inside one chassis — the instability is the universe's immune response to a contradiction wearing skin. (This is why the world posts a bounty: an opposed-fusion isn't just illegal, it's *ontologically rude.*) The **two universals** — Luck and Focus — are not arguments; they're the *thumb on the scale*, the cosmos admitting that even its physics has a margin of "and sometimes, for no reason, it goes the other way."

## 6. Why Mortals Can Ascend Now — the premise that makes the Succession possible

Everything above exists to answer one question, the question the whole game is the joke of: **why now? Why can a nobody become a god *now*, when the gods themselves are dead?** The answer is the cosmology's punchline, and it is grim, and it is funny, and it lands harder for being both.

**The gods died and left the doors unlocked.** Apotheosis — the act of pushing a force to a pure pole until a being *crosses over* into the divine — was always possible; it is how the Titans became Titans and the Olympians became Olympians. But for most of history the thrones were *occupied*, the doors *guarded*, the ladder *crowded* — to ascend, you had to fight your way past a living, vigilant Pantheon that did not want successors and was very good at preventing them. The deicide-promotion was real but *throttled*: maybe one nobody an age made it through.

Then the Pantheon **died** — killed, walked-out, or hollowed into husks — and the throttle broke. The seats stand empty. The doors stand unlocked. The force that used to be *guarded* by jealous living gods now just *soaks the ground*, leaking out of every corpse-region, birthing creatures, free for anyone with the old knack to catch, remake, and ride up the ladder. **The Succession is not a new rule. It is the old rule with the bouncers gone.** The cosmos is in the one window it has ever had where ambition is *unthrottled* — where the gap between "nobody" and "god" is just a climb, a Lab, a willingness to do the unthinkable, and the nerve to sit in a chair you've watched eat everyone who sat in it before.

This is why **Threshold's betting pool exists** — the city has seen ten thousand of you walk in with a starter and a plan, because the unthrottled window means *everyone* tries now. It's why the **High Table** can run the Succession as "the greatest table ever opened" — the throttle breaking turned a once-an-age tragedy into a *season*, with brackets. It's why the **Concord** is frantic to re-lock the doors *lawfully* (refill the thrones, restore the throttle, make ascension a vetted civil service again) and why the **Unbound** are frantic to tear the ladder out by the root (kill the Primordials, end the cycle, so no nobody ever has to become this again). The two great factional poles are just two responses to the same terrifying fact: **the doors are open and they will not stay open forever, and what you do in this window decides what the next age is built on.**

And the final turn, the one the game saves for Act 5: **the window is *you*.** When you ascend, you don't just take a throne — you *become a door again.* Your snapshotted champion seeds into the next age as the **wall** the next nobody climbs, the bouncer the next aspirant has to beat. Apotheosis → the Succession. You climb a graveyard of winners to reach an empty seat, you sit, the chair eats the person and keeps the power, and *you become the next corpse the next nobody robs.* The cosmology makes the loop inevitable, and the game makes you feel every rung of it, and somewhere a load-screen line for the next run reads *"the gods are mostly dead — and one of them used to be you."*

> *The Echo of a God-Maker, "First-Among-Nobodies" (a previous run's ghost):* "The doors are open. That's all 'now' means. The gods aren't *gone* — they're just not *guarding* anymore. You're not special, climber. You're *early*. You got here in the window. ...So did I. So will whoever robs *your* corpse. Climb anyway. We all do. It's the one thing the universe can't stop wanting."

# PART TWO — THE INTER-REGION WEB

*The map is not eleven islands. It is one body — a graveyard of gods whose corpses lean on each other, whose factions traded and warred and grudged across the corpse-borders for an age, whose deaths rippled sideways into their neighbors. Eighteen connection notes, each binding a pair or cluster of the 11 regions by **trade route · old war · alliance/grudge · shared history · migration · or god-fall ripple.** Read together, they make the world feel *politically and mythically connected* — the way a real map feels: every border is a story two regions tell differently.*

> **Region roster (for reference):** §H Threshold (hub) · §1 The Verdant Glut (Eros / Bloomwardens+Revel) · §2 The Mournmarch (Thanatos / Pale Court) · §3 Forgefell (Cosmos+Gaia / Iron Guild) · §4 The Storm Vault (Ouranos / High Table) · §5 The Sunder (Chaos / Unbound+Revel) · §6 Titanfall (Gaia / Stoneblooded) · §7 The Tideless (Ouranos+Gaia / Deep Choir) · §8 The Astral Tier (Cosmos / Concord) · §9 The Maw Beneath (Corruption / Devourer+Unbound's deep reach) · §10 The Hollow Atelier (player-authored / God-Maker).

## CN-01 · The Verdant Glut ⇄ The Mournmarch — *the harvest-and-the-grave, the world's oldest grudge-trade*

The warmest region and the greyest share the world's longest border and its bitterest moral feud — and, infuriatingly to both, its busiest trade route. **Demeter (Eros+Gaia, the harvest)** lies down in the Glut's soil; **Hades (Thanatos+Gaia, here Aidaneus)** descended under the Mournmarch — and *both share the Gaia secondary,* which is why their corpse-lands grade into one another along the **Rotline**, a contested seam where the Glut's overripe fecundity meets the Mournmarch's patient subtraction and the two forces *compost together* into the richest, most-fought-over soil in the world.

- **Old war / grudge:** the **Bloomwardens** (life is sacred; never butcher) and the **Pale Court** (death is power no one's redeemed) are *rival factions by design* — the Court banks the Glut's prodigious dead, the Wardens grieve every corpse the Court repossesses. Their feud is doctrinal: the Court despises the Wardens not for kindness but for **waste** (all that grief, all those funerals, all that power left to rot unredeemed); the Wardens see the Court as grave-robbers who price a soul.
- **Trade route:** despite the grudge, the **Rotline is the single most lucrative trade-corridor on the map.** The Court *buys Bloomwarden creatures, legally and itemized,* for soul-tech (this is canon: a Pale Court collector "bought" three of the Weeping Gardener Hob Thornsoft's beasts, "all legal, all itemized" — he names them faster now, so they have a name before the collector arrives). The Wardens, doomed and underfunded, *need the drachma.* The trade is a quiet horror both sides have systematized into etiquette.
- **God-fall ripple:** kill **Demeter** in Act 3 and the Glut's growth runs feral and *starving* — and the starving fecundity floods *down the Rotline into the Mournmarch*, where it tries to grow over the grey wastes, raising "Gravebloom" and "Pallbloom" hybrids (canon Pale Court reanimant-flora) and forcing the Court into a panicked harvest. One god's fall is the neighbor's invasion. (Conversely, kill **Aidaneus** and the Mournmarch's dead stop staying dead — and they *wander upslope into the Glut*, where the over-eager soil tries to compost the walking corpses and gets indigestion.)

## CN-02 · The Verdant Glut ⇄ The Sunder — *the contested Revel, one party with two doors*

This is the most *intimate* connection on the map, because a single faction straddles it: **the Revel** (Chaos/Tainted) is native to **both** the deep Verdant Glut *and* the Sunder, and **Dionysus (Chaos+Eros)** — whose heir is Bakchanyr the Revel-Lord — *bleeds across the border in person,* his force seeping from the Sunder into the Glut's deep fringe. (Canon-ratified: the Verdant Glut is a *contested region*, the Bloomwardens' front against the Revel by design.)

- **Shared history:** the Revel and the Bloomwardens were **the same faith once.** Both hold that *life is sacred* — the Wardens conclude "therefore tend and protect it," the Revel concludes "therefore *enjoy it until it screams.*" The schism (detailed in the Glut's `[Ink →]` history tree) is the oldest religious split in the world: a single creed of reverence-for-life that fractured along the Eros⇄Chaos seam into mercy and excess.
- **Migration / creep:** the **Sweet Rot** (the Revel's deep festival under the Sunder) is where Glut-creatures *migrate to be "improved"* — Old Garran the Pruner on the Bloomwarden/Revel seam offers the first taste of mutation "to make it stronger, so it lives longer," a Bloomwarden justification for a Revel act. The Glut's gentle fringe-beasts that wander Sunder-ward come back *changed*, the Tumblebaby→Riotmummer→Bigtopper carnival-line being the visible creep.
- **Grudge inside the alliance:** Brother Cask (the Vintner) genuinely *loves* Greenmother Saoirse — and it's mutual and heartbroken. They are not enemies who hate each other; they are **family who chose opposite answers** and watch each other's regions with grief. Cask thinks Saoirse's mercy is a slow suicide; Saoirse thinks Cask's joy is a fast one. Both are right.

## CN-03 · Forgefell ⇄ The Astral Tier — *the Order axis: build the gods vs. file them*

The two most *structured* regions — **Forgefell (Cosmos+Gaia, the dead Forge)** and **The Astral Tier (Cosmos, the crystal citadel)** — share the **Cosmos primary** and are therefore the world's two great schools of *order*, which makes them natural allies and bitter rivals over a single question: **what is order *for*?**

- **Alliance (cold, transactional):** the **Concord licenses Iron Guild constructs** — the Sigilframe→Wardloom→Sanctwall Colossus line are "lawful constructs the Concord licenses" (canon, Astral Tier inhabitants). The Guild builds; the Concord *certifies, files, and permits.* The **Glass Road** runs constructs up from Forgefell's foundries to the Astral Tier's law-courts for inspection and stamping. It is the most paperwork-heavy trade route in the world and both factions are smug about it.
- **The schism:** the Concord believes order should **bind what exists** (seal the wild, file the covenant, refill the thrones lawfully). The Iron Guild believes order should **build what *should* exist** (the born gods were under-engineered; *make* better ones). The Concord finds the Guild's "build a god from scratch" *terrifyingly unfiled*; the Guild finds the Concord's "vet and seat the born ones" *sentimentally attached to legacy systems.* (Auditor Klemm of the Guild and the Concord's Assessor are, canonically, the same job — invasive helpful inspection — pointed at each other.)
- **God-fall ripple:** **Hephaestus** and **Athena** were *colleagues* — the Forge that armed the law, the law that justified the Forge. When Hephaestus fell at the workbench, the Astral Tier *lost its armorer*, which is part of why the Concord crystallized into pure bureaucracy: with no Forge to make its enforcement *physical*, the law became all writ and no sword. (This is the secret behind the Concord's frantic licensing of Guild constructs — they need a weapon and won't admit it.)

## CN-04 · The Storm Vault ⇄ everywhere — *the High Table's spokes: access is the trade*

The **Storm Vault (Ouranos, the sky-king's hung corpse)** is the only region connected to *all* the others, because the **High Table** doesn't hold ground — it holds **access**, and access reaches everywhere. The Vault is the map's *hub-above-the-hub*: where Threshold is the neutral *table*, the Storm Vault is the neutral *clearinghouse* that floats above all of it and charges admission.

- **The spoke-routes (the Marker network):** every other faction's tech can be *borrowed* through a High Table contract without joining — a "marker" that greases a region's door. The Table "greases the door" to a god's region for a long-marker (canon, High Table quest hook). Functionally, the Storm Vault has a **lightning-bridge** (literal and contractual) to every region: the Glass Road's stamps, the Rotline's bounties, the Sunder's "registered exceptions," the Tideless's deep-secret brokerage — all clear through the Table.
- **Old war:** the Table fought no one and *funded everyone*, which is its own kind of war. The myth is that **Zeus (Ouranos+Cosmos, the sky-sovereign)** ran the original "rules between gods" — and when he fell/hung, the **High Table inherited the *referee* role, not the *king* role**, because the brokers understood that selling neutrality outlasts wearing a crown. (The Concord covets the referee role too — they want to *rule* the rules; the Table just wants to *sell* them. This is the quiet cold war of CN-12.)
- **Migration:** sky-mobility is the Vault's gate, so **the Vault is where every region's flyers congregate** — the Skyrend Wyverns, the Augur Birds, anything gust-riding. Aspirants who can't reach the high islands stay grounded and resentful; the Vault literally sorts the world by *altitude*, which is Ouranos's whole argument made into a customs queue.

## CN-05 · Titanfall ⇄ The Tideless — *the Old Weight, above and below the waterline*

These two regions are bound by a **shared corpse**: the **dead sea-Titan** (the "Old Weight of the dead sea-Titan" that grinds in the Tideless's Pressure-Trench, canon) is geologically continuous with the **buried land-Titans of Titanfall** (Tartaron, Krathonar, Mordathun). Both regions are **Gaia-heavy** (Titanfall is pure Gaia; the Tideless is Ouranos+Gaia) and both are cultures of *the deep and the old* — the Stoneblooded who tend the corpse-mountains, the Deep Choir who commune with the corpse-sea.

- **Shared history / the Sundering of the Old Weight:** the Stoneblooded and the Deep Choir were **one people**, the oldest culture in the world, who lived on the Titan-corpses before the Pantheon fell. When the sea-Titan died and the waters *settled*, the coastal clans went under and *kept going* (becoming the Choir, changed by pressure and eldritch company); the highland clans stayed dry and *kept remembering* (becoming the Stoneblooded). They are **estranged siblings** — the ones who drowned and the ones who didn't — and they still trade Gaia-bloodline lore across the tideline, warily.
- **Trade route (the Tideline Exchange):** the Stoneblooded breed the sturdiest *land* Gaia lines; the Choir keep the *abyssal* Gaia/Ouranos leviathans. Where Titanfall's scree meets the Tideless's flat slate shore, the two exchange husbandry-lore — the only place the Choir's "communion" and the Stoneblooded's "patience" sit at the same table. (Both distrust the *fast* factions equally, which gives them common cause.)
- **God-fall ripple:** **Poseidon (Ouranos+Gaia)** *sank rather than fell* (canon: "he sank, there's a difference") — and his sinking is what *settled* the Tideless and, the Stoneblooded believe, **set the land-Titans' strata *grinding*** in sympathy. The two regions share earthquakes: when the Tideless's trench shifts, Titanfall's quake-tiles fire, because they are leaning on the same dead bone. The Listener (Titanfall hermit who hears the buried Titan dreaming) and Choir-Master Ude (who sings to drowned Poseidon) are, unknowingly, listening to *two ends of the same corpse.*

---

## CN-06 · The Sunder ⇄ The Astral Tier — *the great war: the wall and the salt*

The deepest *ideological* war on the map, and the cleanest opposed-force border: **The Sunder (Chaos)** and **The Astral Tier (Cosmos)** are the **Cosmos⇄Chaos axis incarnate** — the Concord and the Unbound are canonical rivals, each the other's mirror and great enemy. This is the war the cosmology is *about*: order against unmaking, the line that holds the world's edges vs. the force that frays them.

- **The war is geographic and literal:** the Astral Tier's Cosmos is described as "the ward that holds the world's edges from fraying into the **Sunder**" — meaning the Concord's seals are *physically the wall against the Sunder's chaos*, and the border between them is a **front line** where Concord sealwrights maintain the world's structural integrity against the Sunder's anti-coherence. Every seal Brother Recant smuggles you past is a brick out of that wall.
- **The two answers to the doors-are-open problem:** the Concord would **refill every throne and re-lock the doors** (restore the throttle, make ascension lawful); the Unbound would **tear the ladder out by the root** (kill the Primordials, end the cycle). They are not arguing about *who* should be god — they are arguing about whether there should be gods *at all*, and the Astral Tier/Sunder border is where that argument has a body count.
- **Defectors flow one way:** the Unbound "don't recruit the faithful — we collect the disillusioned" (canon, Nael). And the Astral Tier *manufactures* the disillusioned — Brother Recant (Concord jurist losing his faith), Sub-Velleth Heshcourt (true-believer with a crack), Brother Forsworn (former Concord, now in the Sunder's Unchurch). **The Astral Tier is the Unbound's best recruiting ground**, because nothing radicalizes like watching order be *cruel and consistent.* The migration is one-directional and the Concord knows it and files it under "attrition."

---

## CN-07 · The Mournmarch → The Maw Beneath — *the floor under the floor: where endings curdle*

A **vertical** connection, and the most important new one: **The Maw Beneath sits *directly below* the Mournmarch** (canon: "it sits below the Mournmarch — Thanatos is endings; the Maw is what endings *become* when they curdle"). The Mournmarch is the *lip* of the underworld; the Maw is the **sump beneath even that**, where spent force sinks past the barrows and rots into living appetite (the Rot).

- **Shared history / the Court's open secret:** the **Pale Court refuses to *name* the Maw** ("the Rot, which the Pale Court refuses to call anything at all," canon). This is the Court's deepest hypocrisy: they file *everything*, every soul a line-item — except the one place where the dead *stop being filable*, dissolving into undifferentiated hunger. The Maw is the Court's *failure state* made into geography: proof that their "nothing is lost, only owed" is a lie, because the Rot eats the ledger.
- **Migration (downward, one-way):** reanimants and souls that *default* hard enough, or rot past redemption, **fall from the Mournmarch into the Maw** — Cinderlich Boneheap, Honeyrot Bridegloam are canon "bridge-fauna between the death-region and the corruption-region." Brother Quill, Defaulted (the Court accountant who fell behind on his own soul-debt) is one bad season from *literally* falling down there.
- **God-fall ripple:** the Maw has **no god** — it is the corruption pole, would-be ruled by the ascension-form *The Devourer.* But it is *fed* by every death above it. Kill enough Olympians (Act 3), spill enough force, and the **Maw rises** — the Rot, better-fed, climbs toward the Mournmarch's barrows, and Warden Last's single Concord seal at the topmost gate becomes the last line holding the corruption-floor from flooding *up* into the world. The Maw is the drain the whole graveyard empties into, and the drain is starting to *back up.*

---

## CN-08 · The Tideless ⇄ The Maw Beneath — *the deep trench and the deeper rot*

The Maw is also reachable *downward from the Tideless* — the Pressure-Trench bottoms out near the Rot (canon: the Maw is "past the Tideless's deepest trench"). Two regions of *descent-as-the-gate*, where pressure/depth is the wall, braided at the bottom.

- **Shared theme / the temptation curve:** both the Tideless's communion-deeps and the Maw's Sump run the **same mechanic** — descend, gain power, lose yourself by degrees (the Choir's hymn-corruption; the Maw's Rot-purity-drain). The Tideless is the *seductive* version (the deep *loves* you, kindly, into changing); the Maw is the *indifferent* version (the Rot doesn't love you, doesn't hate you, would dissolve a god and a child identically). They are the corruption-pole's gentle and total faces.
- **Grudge / the Choir's denial:** the **Deep Choir won't descend to the Maw.** They commune with what *sleeps* (drowned gods, leviathans, the dead-but-intact) — but the Rot is what's *past* sleeping, past remembering, past being a *thing to commune with.* The Choir's whole faith is "the drowned remember everything"; the Maw is **where memory dissolves**, and that terrifies the Choir more than it terrifies anyone, because it's the one thing their reverence can't hold. (The Listener-in-the-Reeds, the Choir defector, fled *because* she learned the deep eventually leads *there.*)
- **Migration:** leviathans that die in the trench, and Choir-communicants who go *too deep*, slide into the Maw and become **soul-amalgams** (Manyface Sigilswarm, the Rot wearing several faces). The Tideless's drowned dead and the Maw's rotting dead are a continuum: the Choir keeps the ones that stayed *whole*, the Maw keeps the ones that didn't.

---

## CN-09 · Forgefell ⇄ The Sunder — *the build and the splice: who gets to make a god wrong*

A sharp **rivalry between two schools of *making*** — **Forgefell's Iron Guild** (build to spec; ethics are a tolerance) and **The Sunder's Unbound/Revel** (splice the taboo; ethics are a Concord tax). Both *make new beings the born gods never sanctioned;* they differ on *shame.*

- **The trade-and-grudge:** the Iron Guild "whispers about cross-force fusion. The Unbound *teach* it. The difference is shame" (canon, Vorr Saltbroke). The Guild makes its taboo creations *to spec, in secret, with documentation*; the Unbound make them *out in the open, gleefully.* Heretic-Vendor Quill Saltbroke (Sunder) explicitly undercuts the Guild: "The Iron Guild charges *triple* and makes you feel *dirty.* We charge *fair*." There is a real **black-market pipeline** running opposed-force catalysts from the Sunder's Open Heresy market to Guild builders who want the goods without the Guild knowing.
- **Old war / the Within-Spec schism:** the Guild's "Within Spec" contest (build an abomination that *doesn't* tip over the line) and the Revel's "Beautiful Mistake" contest (build one that *does*) are **explicit evil twins** (canon). The two regions hold mirror-image competitions, and a builder's choice of *which contest to enter* is a grid-statement: Order/Tainted (Guild, controlled transgression) vs. Chaos/Tainted (Revel, joyful transgression).
- **God-fall ripple:** **Hephaestus** (the Forge, order-made-physical) and the Sunder's **Eris/Dionysus** (chaos, rule-breaking) are *cosmically opposed* — the Forge builds *to a plan*, the Sunder unmakes the *idea* of a plan. When Hephaestus fell, the discipline of *making-with-rules* lost its patron, and the Sunder's *making-without-rules* gained ground — Reclaimer Oslo Coil (the Forgefell freelancer "climbing the Architect cell beside you") represents the Guild's anxiety that without Hephaestus, the difference between a Guild build and a Sunder splice is *only paperwork.*

## CN-10 · The Astral Tier ⇄ The Maw Beneath — *the sealed door: order's last and most futile war*

The **Concord seals the ways down to the Maw** (canon: "The Concord seals the ways down — it is the one thing every lawful instinct screams to contain"). This binds the most *ordered* region to the most *corrupt* one across a single sealed gate, and it's the Concord's most desperate and most futile front.

- **The war of the seal:** **Warden Last** — "a lone Concord seal-keeper stationed at the topmost gate" of the Maw, who has guarded the way down so long he's forgotten what surface life he's protecting — is a *Concord* asset, the Astral Tier's reach into the abyss. His single seal "holding, perfectly, against an entire ocean of Rot" is the image of the whole Concord/Maw relationship: order's law, *correct and consistent and doomed*, against a force that doesn't even know it's being resisted. The Rot will win. The seal does not care. Neither does Warden Last.
- **Why the Concord can't just file it:** the Maw is the one thing the Concord's "to be recorded is to be controlled" *cannot* touch — you can't file a thing that dissolves filing. So the Concord does the only thing it can: **seals it, denies it, and stations a man to die slowly at the door.** This is the Concord's tragedy in miniature — the law that believes order is the only mercy that scales, confronted with the one place where order *cannot* scale, and choosing to hold the line anyway because holding the line is all it knows how to do.
- **Ripple:** if the Maw rises (CN-07), the **first thing it eats is the Concord's authority** — every seal that fails is proof the law doesn't hold, and the Astral Tier's whole legitimacy rests on the claim that it *does.* The Maw is the Concord's nightmare not because it's powerful but because it's *unfileable*, and an unfileable thing is, to the Concord, the end of the world.

## CN-11 · Threshold ⇄ all eight force-regions — *the neutral table and its eight gates*

**Threshold** (the hub, the last mortal city, built on the one patch where no god fell) is connected to all eight force-regions by the **eight region-gates** — the metroidvania spine, each gate sealed until story/rank/faction/traversal conditions are met. Threshold is the *only* ground the dead gods never claimed, which is why it's the one place that ages normally and the one place trade can happen.

- **The Standstill (the truce that binds the map):** all Nine factions keep **embassies** in Threshold under "an old truce nobody likes and nobody breaks" — the **Standstill**, whose one rule is *no godhood within the walls.* This is the political keystone of the entire world: Threshold works as a market *because* it's neutral, and it's neutral *because* every faction needs a place to trade creatures, parts, and contracts without someone ascending in the foyer. The eight gates are spokes; the Standstill is the hub that lets the spokes coexist.
- **Migration / the funnel:** **every aspirant passes through Threshold** (it's where you start; it's where the Foundling Pens choose you). The city is the *demographic funnel* of the whole Succession — ten thousand nobodies a generation, walking in with a starter, fanning out through the eight gates by *alignment* (you court a faction in Act 0, and the world opens toward their region first). Threshold sorts the climbers and ships them out.
- **The hub's secret:** Threshold is built "in the lee of a dead god's ribcage" — and the running gag (Drunk Prophet Lallo) is that the city stands on **Demeter's elbow.** The hub is neutral *ground* but not *no* god's ground — it's the seam *between* the grieving regions, the one spot where no god fell *whole*, which is a very different thing from no god being there at all. The Foundling Pens' starters are the *baby forms* of every region's adult lines, gathered in one place: Threshold is where the map's whole roster begins as hatchlings before scattering home.

---

## CN-12 · The Storm Vault ⇄ The Astral Tier — *the two who would rule the rules: the cold war of the referees*

The map's quietest and most consequential cold war: **the High Table (Storm Vault, Ouranos)** and **the Concord (Astral Tier, Cosmos)** are the two factions that both want to **govern the Succession** — and they hate each other with the specific, bloodless loathing of two bureaucracies claiming the same jurisdiction.

- **The jurisdiction war:** the High Table *sells* neutrality (a referee who picks a side has nothing to sell); the Concord *enforces* law (a referee who won't pick a side is a coward). The Table runs the markers, bounties, and brackets that let aspirants compete *without rules beyond the contract*; the Concord wants ascension *vetted, covenanted, civil-serviced.* Both claim to keep "the rules between god-makers" — but the Table's rules are *deals* and the Concord's rules are *law*, and each thinks the other is a fraud.
- **Shared mythic root:** both inherited from sky-and-law gods who were *colleagues* — **Zeus (Ouranos+Cosmos, the original rule-keeper)** and **Athena (Cosmos+Ouranos, ordered wisdom)** share *both* forces, just weighted differently. The Table got Zeus's *referee* function (the spread, the access); the Concord got Athena's *jurist* function (the writ, the seal). They are two halves of a single dead office, each insisting it's the *whole* office.
- **The cold-war moves:** the Concord "refuses to file" the Hollow Atelier and the Maw (things outside its schema); the Table *happily brokers* both (a registered exception, a salvage marker) — every time the Table monetizes something the Concord won't sanction, it's a move in the war. They never fight openly (the Standstill, and mutual need); they undercut, out-file, and quietly poach each other's aspirants forever.

---

## CN-13 · Titanfall ⇄ The Sunder — *the slowest grudge: the clans and the mutators*

The **deepest faction-grudge that isn't a designed rivalry**: the **Stoneblooded** (Titanfall — breed, don't splice; endure) "distrust the Revel (who mutate what should be bred) above all others" (canon). Titanfall and the Sunder are the two ends of the *husbandry⇄mutation* argument — patience vs. transformation, the bred line vs. the spliced abomination.

- **The grudge:** to the Stoneblooded, the Sunder's whole project is *cheating* — "the Lab is cheating, the way a flood is cheating: powerful, undeniable, and gone the moment the rain stops." A recessive gene coaxed up over six generations is *real* in a way no mutation ever will be. The Revel's "make it stronger with one little mutagen" is, to a clan elder, the exact obscenity their entire culture exists to refuse. They would "no more splice a creature against its nature than salt their own fields."
- **Migration / the exile pipeline:** the grudge has a *body* — **Clanless R999 Driftblood**, exiled from the Stoneblooded "for taking the fast path" (using the Lab), drifts Titanfall's outer scree, and his kind *drift toward the Sunder*, where no one judges a shortcut. The Sunder is where exiled Stoneblooded go to stop being slow — Titanfall *produces* the disillusioned the Unbound/Revel *collect*, the Gaia-mirror of how the Astral Tier feeds the Sunder (CN-06).
- **Shared corpse, opposite reading:** both regions sit on *Titan*-era foundations (Titanfall's buried Titans; the Sunder's reality-warp is what Chaos pooled into where no stable Titan-corpse held it). The Stoneblooded read the Old Weight as *something to endure and honor*; the Sunder reads the same deep instability as *something to ride and exploit.* Same bones, opposite faiths.

---

## CN-14 · The Verdant Glut ⇄ Forgefell — *the rendered garden: the Bloomwardens' two-front war*

The **Bloomwardens are rivaled by the Iron Guild *and* the Pale Court** (canon) — meaning the warmest faction is hemmed by *two* regions at once: the Mournmarch (CN-01, the bankers of their dead) and **Forgefell (the renderers of their living).** This is the structural tragedy of the Glut: the soft creed surrounded by the two creeds that treat its creatures as *materials.*

- **The grudge:** the Iron Guild renders creatures "into parts" at Bohm's sacrifice-pits (he hums to them first — "the only mercy in the building, and it's a real one"). To the Bloomwardens, who weep at a beetle's funeral, the Forge's "your beloved bred creature is a legacy system" is as obscene as the Court's repossessions. The Guild thinks the Wardens are *sentimentally attached to legacy systems*; the Wardens think the Guild renders souls and files the screaming.
- **Trade route (the Render Road):** despite it, there's a grim trade — the Glut's *prodigious* biomass (it grows over the dead, fast) makes it a **supplier of organic stock** to Forgefell's pits, the way it supplies dead to the Court. A "healed" creature the Wardens release that becomes *aggressively* over-healthy is exactly the kind of high-Vitality material the Guild wants for grafts. The Wardens hate this trade most of all, because it's their *mercy* that grows the bodies the Forge buys.
- **The clamp:** geographically, the Glut (Eros) and Forgefell (Cosmos+Gaia) aren't adjacent — but the **Render Road runs through Threshold's market**, where the Guild's scrapwrights and the Court's collectors both shop for what the Glut produces. The Bloomwardens' two great enemies meet *over the Wardens' own harvest*, in the neutral city, and the Wardens can't stop it without breaking the Standstill.

---

## CN-15 · The Mournmarch ⇄ Forgefell — *the reanimant and the construct: two ways to cheat death's labor*

A **technical rivalry** between the two factions that *rebuild beings from dead material*: the **Pale Court** (reanimate the dead) and the **Iron Guild** (build constructs, "refurbishment with a soul-core"). They are competitors in the same dark market — *making a servant out of what's left* — and they sneer at each other's methods.

- **The competing crafts:** the Court *reanimates* (Cinderlich Boneheap, Pallbloom Revenant — the dead, brought back *grateful*, on terms). The Guild *builds* (Cogframe→Wardengine→Bulwark Colossus — constructs, possibly soul-cored). Journeyman Cael Brassknuckle's line — "it's not 'reanimation,' it's *refurbishment with a soul-core* — totally different department" — is the Guild *defensively distinguishing itself from the Court*, which tells you the two crafts are uncomfortably close.
- **The trade (the Soul-Core Exchange):** the Guild *needs soul-cores* for its better constructs; the Court *has souls*, filed and inventoried. There is a quiet pipeline of **Court soul-deposits sold to Guild builders** — the Court liquidates a defaulted soul, the Guild bolts it into a chassis. Both regions get what they want; the soul gets to scream in a new and more durable form. (Slag-Master Bohm and Bone-Sexton Halryn Coldmercy are the same trade — making a body grateful to serve — pointed at different raw material.)
- **Shared force (Gaia/Thanatos seam):** Forgefell is Cosmos+**Gaia**; the Mournmarch is **Thanatos**+Gaia — they share the *Gaia* heaviness (mass, the grave, the unkillable solidity). The Guild's constructs and the Court's reanimants are both *heavy, patient, hard to put down* — the Gaia secondary expressing as "this servant will outlast you." They are two Gaia-grounded death-industries, competing for the same corpses.

---

## CN-16 · The Sunder ⇄ The Maw Beneath — *the Unbound's descent: from rule-breaking to root-killing*

The **Unbound straddle both** — native to the Sunder (Chaos, rule-bending), but their *deepest reach* is the Maw Beneath, "the trailhead to the Primordials and the Unmaking" (canon: the Unbound "descend it on purpose, because the Unmaking ends here"). This is the single most important *path* on the map: the Sunder is where the Unbound *break the rules*, the Maw is where they go to **end the rules forever.**

- **The continuum:** the Sunder's creed ("burn every throne, let no god rule again") *culminates* in the Maw. The Sunder is the philosophy; the Maw is the *implementation* — the only road to killing the Primordials runs *down*, through the Rot, to the bottom of everything where "the cycle itself can be unmade." Nael's Unbound and the Hollow Choirmaster's deepest-delved are the same faction at two depths: the surface refusal and the abyssal one.
- **The grid-slide made geographic:** the Unbound "slide Free Wild ⇄ Devourer" (Chaos/Pure → Chaos/Corrupt) depending on *your* heart — and this slide is **literally the descent from the Sunder to the Maw.** The Sunder is where you can be Free Wild (liberation, the benign Chaos pole); the Maw is where you become the Devourer (annihilation, the corrupt one). To go down is to slide corrupt. The geography *is* the morality axis.
- **The two endgame doors share a trailhead:** the **Devourer ascension** (take the Maw's throne, become the world's hunger) and the **Unmaking** (descend past the world, kill the Primordials, end the cycle) are *both* reached from the Maw's bottom — "The Mouth at the Bottom." The single bleakest choice in the game (become the hunger / end the cycle) is set at the one point where the Unbound's two possible souls diverge. Same staircase down, opposite things at the bottom.

---

## CN-17 · The Hollow Atelier ⇄ Forgefell — *the dream the Guild can't reach: build vs. be-built-by*

The **Hollow Atelier** (the player-authored demiplane, the God-Maker's domain) is bound to **Forgefell** by a single covetous longing: the Atelier is "the Architect's dream made real" — the thing **Foundress Magna Ironwright** "has preached her whole life and never once achieved: a world you *build* instead of inherit." The Iron Guild *covets* the Atelier and *cannot hold it.*

- **The grudge-of-envy:** Forgefell is a dead *organic* god's foundry — the Guild *inherited* Hephaestus's workbench; it builds *in* a corpse it didn't make. The Hollow Atelier is *authored from scratch* by the player's own craft — the build-don't-be-born creed *fully realized*, with no inherited corpse under it. This is why the Guild can only get a *note* into the Atelier (canon: Magna's note slid under a door that leads nowhere — "You built a world. I never could. I'm not jealous. (I'm a little jealous.)"). The Atelier is the Guild's *theology come true in someone else's hands*, and Magna's envy is the most honest emotion in the world.
- **Shared roster, opposite source:** both regions are full of **constructs** — but Forgefell's are *Hephaestus's* leftovers (automatons still marching dead loops) while the Atelier's are *yours* (the foundry-class colossi you built, standing as load-bearing pillars of the wings they accreted). The Atelier proves the Guild's creed *works* — you really can build a god from scratch — which is exactly why the Guild can never be allowed in: it would have to admit a *nobody* did what its Foundress couldn't.
- **The structural mirror:** Forgefell is the *inherited* workshop (a dead god's, claimed by a faction); the Atelier is the *authored* workshop (no god's, claimed by no one but you). The Glass Road runs Guild constructs out to the Concord for filing; the Atelier files with *no one* — the Concord "refuses to file it (there is no form for a region with one citizen and no god)." It is the one making-place outside every institution that wants to govern making.

## CN-18 · The Hollow Atelier ⇄ Threshold ⇄ the Maw Beneath — *the three thresholds: front door, drafting table, and drain*

A **cluster note** binding the map's three "non-god-corpse" regions into a single thematic spine — the three places defined *not* by a dead god but by a *relationship to the Succession*: Threshold (where it begins), the Hollow Atelier (where you author an alternative to it), the Maw Beneath (where it can be ended).

- **The structural triangle:** **Threshold** is the world's gentle, hand-authored *front door* — neutral, shared, where every nobody starts (CN-11). The **Hollow Atelier** is the world's hardest-won, *player*-authored *back room* — yours alone, manifesting only past high craft-mastery, "the structural inverse of Threshold." The **Maw Beneath** is the world's *floor and drain* — sealed, dangerous, where force rots and the cycle can be unmade (CN-07). Threshold is *neutral ground that ages normally*; the Maw is *corruption-ground that dissolves*; the Atelier is *un-rendered ground that resolves only where you've earned it.* Three different answers to "what is ground, when it isn't a dead god's body?"
- **The shared NPCs (the through-line):** the Atelier **reuses Threshold's Lab NPCs** — Surgeon-Lab-Tech Veil and the gutter-rat tutor Quink "follow your craft there once the Atelier manifests" (canon). This is the only NPC-migration across the map: the people who taught you to build in the *front door* are the only ones who can stand in the *back room.* (Quink, who always said the Lab could become a *place*, is "somehow already here" — the tutorial tutor vindicated.) Threshold and the Atelier are the start and end of the *maker's* path; the same hands bracket both.
- **The three endgames, three regions:** the map's three *terminal* relationships to the Succession each have a home — **ASCEND** (the 9 thrones) happens at the Primordial peak above the Astral Tier; **GOD-MAKER** (refuse the throne, build gods for others) is the Hollow Atelier; **THE UNMAKING** (end the cycle) is the Maw's bottom. Threshold is where all three roads *begin*, the Atelier and the Maw are where the two *refusals* end. The whole map is a single sentence: *you start at the door, and you finish by becoming a wall, a forge, or a silence.*

# PART THREE — FACTION ENTANGLEMENTS

*The 9 factions are not a list of standing-tracks. They are a *web of obligation* — who funds whom, who hates whom, the marriages of convenience, the betrayals being planned, the cold wars conducted entirely through paperwork and price. The canon establishes each faction's **one named rival**; this section maps the *rest* — the entanglements the standing-system implies but doesn't spell out. Twenty entanglement notes, organized as: the **canon rivalries** (the spine), the **funding & supply webs** (who pays whom), the **secret dealings** (the trades nobody admits), and the **cold wars** (the bloodless jurisdiction fights).*

> **The Nine (canonical, per `factions_npcs.md`):** Concord (Order/Pure, Astral Tier, Velleth) · Iron Guild (Order/Tainted, Forgefell, Magna) · Pale Court (Order/Corrupt, Mournmarch, Wessel) · High Table (Balanced, Storm Vault, Indra Vael) · Stoneblooded (Balanced/Pure, Titanfall, Ostrega) · Bloomwardens (Pure, Verdant Glut, Saoirse) · Revel (Chaos/Tainted, Glut/Sunder, Cask) · Unbound (Chaos/any, Sunder, Nael) · Deep Choir (Balanced/Corrupt, Tideless, Vourl).
>
> **The grid as a map of who-hates-whom:** the morality grid (Order⇄Chaos × Purity⇄Corruption) *is* the faction-relationship engine. Factions on the **same row or column** are competitors (they want the same thing slightly differently); factions on **opposite corners** are mortal enemies (they want incompatible worlds); factions sharing an **edge** are uneasy allies. Aligning with one *cools* its same-axis competitors and *enrages* its opposite-corner enemy. The four canon rivalries are the four sharpest opposite-corner / shared-edge tensions.

## THE CANON RIVALRIES (the spine — four declared feuds)

### FE-01 · Concord ⇄ Unbound — *the war over whether there should be gods at all*
**The defining feud of the game.** Order/Pure (refill every throne, lock the doors lawfully) vs. Chaos/any (burn every throne, tear the ladder out). They are **opposite corners on the Order axis** and the cleanest Cosmos⇄Chaos opposition. The Concord would make ascension a vetted civil service; the Unbound would make ascension *impossible forever* (the Unmaking). This is not a turf war — it's an **existential disagreement about the cosmos**, and it's the one rivalry that can't be brokered, bought off, or balanced. Velleth files the Unbound under "irregularities to be regularized"; Nael pities the Concord as "a trap that's caught itself." Courting one turns the other *fully, permanently hostile* (canon: the Unmaking quest turns the Concord hostile for good). **The Astral Tier physically walls the Sunder out** (CN-06); the Unbound recruit the Concord's disillusioned (CN-06). Every other rivalry is *commerce.* This one is *theology.*

### FE-02 · Iron Guild ⇄ Bloomwardens — *the materials and the mercy*
Order/Tainted (souls are materials; build better gods) vs. Pure (life is sacred; never butcher). The Guild treats creatures as *stock* — "your beloved bred creature is a legacy system"; the Wardens weep at a beetle's funeral. They are the **Tainted⇄Pure opposition on the Order side**, and their feud is about *the worth of a soul.* The Guild finds the Wardens sentimentally inefficient (all that grief, all those mended creatures that die anyway); the Wardens find the Guild a soul-renderer with good documentation. Yet they **trade** (the Render Road, CN-14): the Wardens' prodigious biomass feeds the Guild's pits, and the Wardens hate that their own mercy grows the bodies the Forge buys. Winning the Guild's "Within Spec" contest *earns a Bloomwarden grudge* (canon); going Iron Guild cools Saoirse "while you still live."

### FE-03 · Pale Court ⇄ Bloomwardens — *the bank and the funeral*
Order/Corrupt (death is power no one's redeemed; bank every soul) vs. Pure (grief is sacred; let the dead rest). The Court repossesses the Wardens' dead (legally, itemized); the Wardens grieve every soul the Court prices. They are the **Corrupt⇄Pure opposition**, and the feud is about *what you owe the dead.* (Note: the Bloomwardens are rivaled by *both* the Pale Court and the Iron Guild — the warm faction is clamped between the two creeds that monetize its creatures, CN-14.) The Court despises the Wardens for **waste**; the Wardens despise the Court for **commerce in grief.** The Rotline (CN-01) is their shared border and their busiest trade route — the grudge and the trade are the same relationship.

### FE-04 · Stoneblooded ⇄ Revel — *the bred line and the glorious mutation*
Balanced/Pure (breed, don't splice; endure) vs. Chaos/Tainted (mutate everything; change for its own sake). The clans "distrust the Revel above all others" — the Revel mutates what should be bred, which is, to the oldest culture in the world, the exact obscenity it exists to refuse. They are the **husbandry⇄mutation axis** (CN-13). The grudge is *generational and total*: a recessive gene coaxed up over six generations vs. a creature changed by one mugagen. The Revel finds the clans *boringly slow* ("how brave, how *boring*"); the clans find the Revel *flood-cheaters* whose work is "gone the moment the rain stops." Going Revel (the "Beautiful Mistake" abomination) *turns the Stoneblooded cold* (canon).

## THE FUNDING & SUPPLY WEBS (who pays whom — the money map)

### FE-05 · The High Table funds *everyone* — *the house takes a cut of the apocalypse*
The Chairwoman **Indra Vael's** High Table is the financial spine of the whole web. It is "the one neutral power in a world of zealots," and it funds *all eight* other factions *indirectly* by running the markers, bounties, and brackets that move drachma, essence, and ichor between them. **The Table funds whoever's playing** — it backs aspirants of every grid, posts bounties co-signed by the Court, greases doors for every region. Its business model is *the Succession itself as a casino*, and a casino funds every game at its tables. The Table's only enemy is *anyone who would end the game* (the Unbound's Unmaking is "the one thing that genuinely frightens her, because it closes the book"). So the Table quietly funds **anti-Unbound stability** everywhere — it is the secret patron of the *status quo.*

### FE-06 · Pale Court → Iron Guild — *the soul-core pipeline*
The Court *has souls* (filed, inventoried, defaulted); the Guild *needs soul-cores* for its better constructs (CN-15). There is a quiet, lucrative pipeline: **the Court liquidates a defaulted soul-deposit, the Guild bolts it into a chassis.** Both are Order-row factions (Order/Corrupt and Order/Tainted) — *adjacent on the grid*, so uneasy allies rather than enemies. They share a worldview ("a being is a resource") and differ only on *form* (reanimate the dead vs. build the new). The pipeline is the most stable inter-faction trade on the map because both sides find it *reasonable*, which is exactly what makes it horrifying.

### FE-07 · Bloomwardens → Pale Court & Iron Guild — *the unwilling supplier*
The Glut's biomass is so prodigious that the Wardens are, against their will, the **organic-stock supplier to both their enemies** (the Render Road and the Rotline, CN-01/CN-14). They don't *choose* this — the Glut grows over the dead faster than anywhere, producing the corpses the Court banks and the high-Vitality bodies the Guild grafts. The Wardens are funded *by being harvested*: the drachma they need to keep the fringe survivable comes partly from selling what they can't stop the Glut from over-producing. It's the supply web's cruelest knot — **the kindest faction bankrolled by its own harvest**, and it knows it, and it tends anyway.

### FE-08 · Deep Choir → the deep-secret market — *paying in pieces of yourself*
The **Deep Choir** funds itself by **brokering deep-secrets** — drowned knowledge, sunk gods, the Thanatos/Ouranos cross-force lore the surface forgot (the Rememberer Sloane Saltlung's trade). But the Choir's "currency" is *communion*, which costs the buyer *pieces of themselves* (the hymn-corruption). The Choir doesn't deal in drachma so much as in *transformation* — you pay for the deep's secrets by *becoming a little more the deep's.* This makes the Choir the one faction that *can't* be funded conventionally (you can't bribe it; you can only *deepen*), which is why it sits apart from the Table's money-web — the Choir runs on a different economy entirely, and the Table finds it *unbankable* and unsettling.

### FE-09 · Concord licenses the Iron Guild — *the certification racket*
The Concord *licenses* Guild constructs (the Glass Road, CN-03) — the Guild builds, the Concord stamps. This is a **funding relationship dressed as regulation**: the Concord charges (in compliance, in covenant, in fees) for the legitimacy only it can grant, and the Guild pays because a *certified* construct is a *sellable* one. Both Order-row factions, adjacent on the grid, uneasy allies. The racket benefits both and *galls* both — the Guild resents needing the Concord's stamp (it built a god; why does it need a *form*?); the Concord resents needing the Guild's product (it makes law; why does it need a *weapon*?). They are co-dependent and mutually contemptuous, which is the most stable kind of bureaucratic marriage.

## THE SECRET DEALINGS (the trades nobody admits)

### FE-10 · Iron Guild ⇄ Unbound/Revel — *the shame-laundered catalyst pipeline*
The Guild publicly *condemns* opposed-force fusion (it has forms for the screaming, but it builds *to spec, in secret*). The Unbound/Revel *sell* opposed-force catalysts openly and cheaply, undercutting the Guild "by three times and the shame" (CN-09). The secret dealing: **Guild builders quietly buy Sunder catalysts** through the Open Heresy market and Threshold fences, getting the taboo goods *without the Guild's certification process knowing.* The Guild's official line is "we'd never"; the Guild's *actual* supply chain runs partly through the very heretics it disavows. (Auntie Sull, the Threshold black-market fence selling god-organs "no honest vendor will touch," is a node in this — Guild money flows to her, off the books.)

### FE-11 · Pale Court ⇄ High Table — *the posthumous bounty co-signing*
The Court and the Table **co-sign bounties** (canon: "Letters of Marque, Posthumous" is a High Table Bounty *co-signed by the Pale Court*). The secret layer: the Court uses the Table's bounty-network to **collect on soul-debts it can't legally pursue itself** — when a rival god-maker dies owing the Court, the Court can't always repossess across faction lines, so it *launders the debt into a High Table bounty*, and the Table sends an aspirant to "settle the estate." The Table gets its cut; the Court gets its soul back; the aspirant does the dirty work thinking it's a clean contract. Two Order-adjacent / Balanced factions, quietly converting *grief* into *gambling action.*

### FE-12 · The Revel ⇄ everyone's aspirants — *the first catalyst is always free*
The Revel's secret dealing isn't with factions — it's with *their members.* "The catalyst's on the house, first one always is. Second one's the real price and it isn't drachma" (canon, Brother Vint). The Revel **deals to the disillusioned of every faction**, the way a dealer works a party: the Concord jurist losing faith, the exiled Stoneblood, the over-disciplined Guild builder — the Revel offers each one *the glorious mutation*, free, the first time. This makes the Revel a **solvent that dissolves every other faction's loyalty from the inside.** It doesn't recruit by argument; it recruits by *appetite*, and appetite is universal. (The Unbound collect the disillusioned by *cold reason*; the Revel collects them by *warm temptation* — the same prey, two seductions.)

### FE-13 · Deep Choir ⇄ Concord — *the sealed-god cold trade*
The Choir and the Concord *publicly clash* over **gods that drowned rather than died** — the Choir wants them *heard*, the Concord wants them *sealed* (canon, "What Sank Instead of Fell"). The secret dealing underneath: there are sunk gods *both* factions would rather stayed *unbothered*, and they have an **unspoken non-aggression pact** over the most dangerous ones — the Choir won't sing to it if the Concord won't seal it (sealing might *wake* it; singing might *answer* it; both are bad). It's a cold trade conducted entirely in *what neither side does.* The Listener-in-the-Reeds (Choir defector) and Brother Recant (Concord whistleblower) are the two people who know about these pacts and are both being quietly silenced for it.

### FE-14 · Stoneblooded ⇄ Bloomwardens — *the quiet husbandry alliance*
Two **Pure-aligned** factions (Balanced/Pure and Pure) — *not* rivals, and quietly allied through shared method: both practice *husbandry over slaughter*, both bond rather than butcher, both believe a creature raised kind is worth more than one taken by force. The Stoneblooded's "outlast their fear" and the Bloomwardens' "love's a method, not a mood" are the same doctrine in different climates (stone-patience vs. green-mercy). They **trade gentle-husbandry lore** — the only two factions that would weep at a creature's funeral and mean it. It's not a formal alliance (the Stoneblooded distrust everyone's hurry, the Wardens are doomed and underfunded), but it's the **warmest faction-to-faction relationship on the map** — two losing creeds keeping each other's methods alive. (Tame-Warden Cuff Stillblood and Hearthward Ona Bramblekind would recognize each other instantly.)

## THE COLD WARS (the bloodless jurisdiction fights)

### FE-15 · High Table ⇄ Concord — *the referees' cold war*
The two factions that both claim to **govern the Succession** (CN-12) — the Table *sells* neutrality, the Concord *enforces* law. They never fight openly (the Standstill, mutual need) but conduct a permanent cold war of **out-filing and under-cutting**: every time the Table brokers something the Concord won't sanction (the Maw, the Atelier, an abomination "registered exception"), it's a move; every time the Concord files a covenant the Table can't broker around, it's a counter. They inherited two halves of Zeus-and-Athena's dead office and each insists it's the whole office. The aspirant is the battleground — both poach, both "intrigue," both keep a file on you. It's the **most consequential cold war** because whoever wins decides whether the next age's ascensions are *deals* or *laws.*

### FE-16 · Iron Guild ⇄ Pale Court — *the cold war of method (who cheats death better)*
Both Order-row factions *rebuild beings from dead material* (CN-15) and both *trade* (the soul-core pipeline, FE-06) — but they're in a quiet cold war over **whose craft is superior.** The Guild's "refurbishment with a soul-core — totally different department" is a *defensive sneer*; the Court's reanimants and the Guild's constructs compete for the same corpses and the same buyers. It's a craftsman's rivalry — mutual respect laced with contempt, each sure its method (build vs. reanimate) is the *honest* one. They cooperate (the pipeline) *and* compete (the market) simultaneously, which is the definition of a cold war between people who are basically the same.

### FE-17 · Concord ⇄ Deep Choir — *the order-against-the-deep cold war*
The Concord (Order/Pure) and the Deep Choir (Balanced/Corrupt) are **canon rivals** — but theirs is a *cold* war, not the hot existential one the Unbound get. The Concord's whole project is "to be recorded is to be controlled"; the Choir traffics in the *unrecorded* (drowned secrets, sunk gods, things the surface buried). The Concord wants the deep *sealed and filed*; the Choir wants it *heard and remembered.* They fight over **jurisdiction of the forgotten** — the Concord claims the right to seal what's dangerous, the Choir claims the right to commune with what's sunk (CN-13, FE-13). It's bloodless because direct confrontation is bad for both (waking the deep), so they wage it through *seals placed and seals broken*, through Warden Last's gates and the Choir's patient tides.

### FE-18 · The Unbound ⇄ the Revel — *the philosopher and the hedonist on a sinking ship*
Both **Chaos-aligned**, sharing the Sunder — *not* enemies, but *not* allies either: "a philosopher and a hedonist share a sinking ship, arguing about whether to bail" (canon). The Unbound want to *end* the cycle (cold, lucid, the Unmaking); the Revel want to *enjoy the wreckage* (warm, decadent, the glorious mutation). They share a region, a force (Chaos), and a contempt for the thrones — but they slide opposite directions (the Unbound toward *meaning the ending*, the Revel toward *not thinking about it*). Their cold war is a **theological argument conducted at a party**: Nael thinks the Revel's joy is a *distraction* from the necessary work; Cask thinks Nael's Unmaking is *the most boring possible use of freedom.* They tolerate each other because the alternative (fighting over the one lawless region they both need) would cost them both the Sunder.

## THE GRID-MAP OF THE NINE (the relationship engine, summarized)

> A single readout of who-stands-where, so the entanglements above resolve to the morality grid the whole game runs on:

| | **Pure** | **(Balanced purity)** | **Corrupt** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order** | **Concord** (Lawgiver) | — | **Iron Guild** (Architect, Tainted) · **Pale Court** (Iron Throne) |
| **Balanced** | **Stoneblooded** (Warden) | **High Table** (Broker) | **Deep Choir** (Plaguelord) |
| **Chaos** | *(Free Wild — Unbound's Pure pole)* | — | **Revel** (Reveler) · **Unbound** (slides Free Wild ⇄ Devourer) |
| **Bloomwardens** | (Warden→Lawgiver edge, Pure) | | |

**Reading the engine:**
- **Opposite corners = mortal enemies:** Concord (Order/Pure) ⇄ Unbound (Chaos/Corrupt-capable) is the sharpest — FE-01, the theology war. Concord ⇄ Revel (Chaos/Tainted) is the next.
- **Shared row, different purity = competitors who trade:** the whole **Order row** (Concord / Iron Guild / Pale Court) is a cluster of factions that *agree beings are resources* and differ only on *how* — hence the soul-core pipeline (FE-06), the certification racket (FE-09), the cold war of method (FE-16). They cooperate *and* despise each other.
- **Shared column (Pure) = quiet allies:** Bloomwardens / Stoneblooded (FE-14) — the warmest relationship, two losing creeds.
- **Balanced center = the broker:** the High Table (FE-05) funds everyone and commits to no one, because *balance is its product.*
- **Chaos row = the Sunder's odd couple:** Unbound / Revel (FE-18) — share a region and a force, slide opposite ways.
- **The Bloomwardens are clamped:** Pure, but *bordered by the entire Order-Corrupt cluster* that wants their creatures as materials (FE-02, FE-03, FE-07) — the structural reason the kind faction is the doomed one.

> **Faction-entanglement — count: 18 numbered entanglements** (FE-01 through FE-18) — 4 canon rivalries, 5 funding/supply webs, 5 secret dealings, 4 cold wars — plus the grid-relationship engine that generates them. Every faction appears in at least three entanglements; the web has no isolated nodes.

# PART FOUR — EXPANDED BRANCHED QUEST-OPTION TREES

*The `story_quests.md` spine names each quest's branches in a line or two. This section takes **six load-bearing quests** — one per act, spanning the whole Apotheosis→Succession arc — and writes their branch-trees **out in depth**: every major choice, the divergent paths, the **long-tail consequences** that echo across acts, and **how branches re-converge or fork the ending.** The morality-grid divergence is shown *concretely* — not "Pure tilt" but exactly which flag sets, which faction warms, which door at the Empty Seat opens or bolts shut.*

> **How to read a tree.** Each quest gets: the **stakes** (why this choice matters to the spine), a **branch map** (the forks as a tree), each branch's **immediate gate** (the mechanic it opens, per canon) and **long-tail** (what it changes 1–3 acts later), and a **convergence/divergence note** (does the river rejoin or split toward a specific ending). Flags use the canon axes: **Order⇄Chaos**, **Purity⇄Corruption**, plus the named threshold-flags (`UNMAKING`, `GOD-MAKER`, `betrayal-payoff`, `sticky-rival-state`, `Succession-fragment`).

## QT-1 · Q0.3 "The Mark" — *the grid's first seed (Act 0 inciting incident)*

**Stakes.** This is where the morality grid *starts recording.* The Primordial-tint brand you survive is the cosmology's "ambition made literal" (PART ONE §6) graft onto your own body — and what you do with it is the first concrete coordinate the whole run reads back at the Empty Seat. It looks like a tutorial choice. It is the **first clause of your eventual Reliquary file.**

**Branch map:**
```
THE MARK (survive the godling's brand)
├─ SEAL IT (Pure) ─────────── Concord ward-sigil; the mark sleeps
│     └─ later: "ambition removes it" — the seal breaks when you first push hard
├─ FEED IT (Corrupt) ──────── let the Primordial-tint spread; the Lab gains a hidden recipe slot
│     └─ later: the brand climbs your marrow; abominations come to obey sooner
└─ BARGAIN IT (Balanced) ──── let the High Table appraise it for a marker advance
      └─ later: the Table holds a lien on your *body* — a marker you can default on
```

- **SEAL IT — Pure / Order-leaning.** *Immediate gate:* a Concord ward-sigil sleeps the mark; sets your first **Purity** tick and a faint Concord-warmth. *Long-tail:* the seal is *load-bearing foreshadowing* — Vael's line ("the seal will hold until ambition removes it; ambition always removes it") is a **literal flag**: the first time you trip a hard Apotheosis or taboo Lab op in Act 2, the seal *cracks*, and the crack is a scripted beat (the Concord "cools if you've spliced"). Choosing Pure here makes the *eventual* corruption — if you go there — land as a *betrayal of a sealed promise*, not a neutral drift. Re-converges with everyone by Act 1 (the seal is dormant, not destiny).
- **FEED IT — Corrupt / Lab-favoring.** *Immediate gate:* unlocks a **hidden recipe slot** in the Lab (the brand is itself a Primordial-tint ingredient); sets the first **Corruption** tick. *Long-tail:* this is the run's **earliest down-payment on the Devourer / corrupt-Apotheosis path** — the brand "climbing your marrow" means by Act 2, *abominations begin to obey you sooner* (canon: feeding corruption makes them biddable), and by Act 3 the corrupt deicide-harvest is *easier* because you've been a corruption-vessel since Act 0. Feeding the mark is the first step down the staircase that ends at the Maw's Mouth (CN-16).
- **BARGAIN IT — Balanced / High Table.** *Immediate gate:* a **marker advance** (early drachma/essence) and the High Table standing track (Stranger). *Long-tail:* the Table now holds **a lien on your body**, not just your contracts — a unique *betrayal-payoff* hook: if you default on a High Table marker later (Act 4's "Marker on a God"), the Accountant comes for the *mark itself*, a recurring rival with a claim on your literal divinity. Bargaining keeps your grid uncommitted (the Broker's whole appeal) but mortgages your apotheosis to the house.

**Convergence/divergence:** all three re-converge into Act 1 (the mark is dormant or banked), but each plants a *different* seed that the game **re-reads at the Empty Seat** — SEAL → a Pure run's seal-break is a tragedy; FEED → a Corrupt run's Devourer was always coming; BARGAIN → a Broker run's body was always the Table's collateral. The tutorial choice is the ending's first sentence.

## QT-2 · Q1.4 "The Reliquary of Winners" — *the Succession truth (Act 1 climax)*

**Stakes.** The hinge of the whole game's *meaning.* You learn the cosmology's obscenity (PART ONE §4): every god was a nobody who climbed this ladder; the Pantheon is a graveyard of previous you's. Your *reaction* to this truth is the branch — and it sets the **emotional thesis** the rest of the run argues. This is also where the quest-giver itself forks by your Act-0/Act-1 alignment (Concord archivist / Pale Court mortician / drunk Reveler delivers the same truth in three different keys).

**Branch map:**
```
THE RELIQUARY (read the record of the ascended; survive what you learn)
├─ ACCEPT AS LAW (Order) ───── "the ladder is legitimate; I'll climb it correctly"
│     └─ Concord↑ → routes toward LAWGIVER / ARCHITECT
├─ ACCEPT AS PERMISSION (Corrupt) ── "if they were nobodies, nothing is sacred"
│     └─ Pale Court / Unbound↑ → routes toward IRON THRONE / DEVOURER
└─ LAUGH AT IT (Chaos) ─────── "the whole pantheon is a cosmic joke; I want the punchline"
      └─ Revel / Unbound↑ → routes toward REVELER / FREE WILD
```

- **ACCEPT AS LAW — Order.** *The thesis:* the Succession is a *system*, and systems can be climbed *correctly.* *Gate:* Concord standing↑, opens the lawful-ascension reading of every later quest. *Long-tail:* this is the **Lawgiver/Architect on-ramp** — you'll later kill the *chaotic* gods to "seat law" (Q3.1 Order branch), accept patrons *honestly* (Q3.2 Pure), and arrive at the Empty Seat with the *9-throne ASCEND door fully lit* and the *Unmaking door dark.* Accepting-as-law means you believe the graveyard is a *legitimate institution*, and you intend to join its management.
- **ACCEPT AS PERMISSION — Corrupt.** *The thesis:* if the awe was never earned, nothing is owed — *take everything.* *Gate:* Pale Court / Unbound standing↑. *Long-tail:* the **Iron Throne / Devourer on-ramp** — deicide becomes *harvest* (Q3.1 Corrupt: the god-organ is the best Lab stock in the game), patrons become *future betrayals* (Q3.2 Corrupt: accept-intending-to-harvest sets the betrayal-payoff flag), and the Empty Seat's *Devourer* door brightens. This branch reads the Reliquary as the Sunder's Heretic-Vendor does: "the world's looking at *you* instead of building another throne — good."
- **LAUGH AT IT — Chaos.** *The thesis:* it's all a joke, and the only honest response is to *want the punchline.* *Gate:* Revel / Unbound standing↑. *Long-tail:* the **Reveler / Free Wild on-ramp** — you'll free gods rather than seat or harvest them (Q3.1 Chaos), refuse all patrons (Q3.2 Chaos/Unmaking, which begins lighting the `UNMAKING` flag), and arrive split between the joyful-Chaos (Reveler) and the cycle-ending (Unmaking) doors. Laughing is the *first* thing that points at the Unmaking, because to laugh at the ladder is the first step toward *kicking it over.*
- **The 4th-wall crack (rare, canon):** the optional *"...So I'm speedrunning someone's tragedy"* line — Pellos's *"yes, and someone will speedrun yours"* — is the **thematic skeleton key** that all three branches share. Whichever reaction you pick, the game *knows* you've understood the loop, and Act 5 pays it off (you become the tragedy being speedrun).

**The `[Ink →]` deep vein (long-tail bomb):** reading a specific Successor's file **foreshadows the exact Act-5 world-boss you'll become** — the Lawgiver Twice-Crowned (DG-001), the Gardener of Wildfire (DG-004), the Grudgekeeper of the Burnt Court (DG-024). The branch you pick *biases which file you're drawn to read*, and the file you read *is a preview of your own Reliquary entry.* Order-readers gravitate to the Lawgiver's file; Chaos-readers to the Gardener's; Corrupt-readers to the Grudgekeeper's — and each is **literally the grid-god they're routing toward.** The quest secretly shows you your ending in Act 1 and lets you not notice.

**Convergence/divergence:** the river *forks here for real* — the three reactions seed three different grid-corners, and they **do not fully re-converge** (an Order thesis and a Chaos thesis arrive at *different doors* in Act 4). This is the quest where the run stops being "anyone's story" and becomes *yours.*

## QT-3 · Q2.4 "First Light" — *the first Apotheosis (the Succession TRIGGER)*

**Stakes.** The signature spine *fires for the first time.* You push your own creature to a pure force-pole and it **ascends into a minor god that walks out of your roster and reshapes a region in your name** — and the game **seeds the first Dead-God snapshot fragment** (the async-invasion wall begins building, wearing your face). This is the most mechanically consequential quest in the middle game: it's the first proof the graveyard has a fresh plot with your handwriting on it.

**Branch map — the branch is *which pole you crown*:**
```
FIRST LIGHT (push a creature to a pure pole; complete the Apotheosis ritual)
├─ EROS pole (Pure) ────────── the godling BLOOMS its region (life floods)
│     └─ seeds toward WARDEN / REVELER Dead-God; region heals-wild
├─ COSMOS pole (Pure/Order) ── the godling ORDERS its region (law crystallizes)
│     └─ seeds toward LAWGIVER / ARCHITECT Dead-God; region locks-wild
├─ THANATOS pole (Corrupt) ─── the godling WITHERS its region (subtraction spreads)
│     └─ seeds toward IRON THRONE / PLAGUELORD Dead-God; region rots-wild
└─ CHAOS pole (Corrupt/Chaos)─ the godling UNMAKES its region (rules come loose)
      └─ seeds toward DEVOURER / FREE WILD Dead-God; region warps-wild
```

- **EROS — Pure.** *Immediate:* the new godling *blooms* — its home region (likely the Verdant Glut) floods with over-fertility; the Bloomwardens reel as a *new Eros godling* over-grows their fringe. *Long-tail:* this is the **first Succession-fragment keyed to a Pure pole** — your Act-5 Dead-God will be a *Warden* (the living-wall, DG-009) or *Reveler* (Gardener of Wildfire, DG-004, if Chaos-tinged). The region "heals-wild": new heal-tiles, gentler-but-relentless spawns. The Apotheosis liturgy you crown it with (the `[Ink →]` tree) seeds the *epitaph* a future player reads.
- **COSMOS — Pure/Order.** *Immediate:* the godling *orders* — crystal-geometry spreads, the region locks into rigid law; the Concord *recognizes* it (a lawful godling, filable). *Long-tail:* seeds toward the **Lawgiver/Architect Dead-God** (DG-001 / Architect-form) — the wall a future climber breaks against will *edit their turn order* and *enforce the queue* (the Architect kit). The region "locks-wild": seal-gates, law-tiles. This is the most *Concord-pleasing* Apotheosis and bolts you further toward the lawful ASCEND door.
- **THANATOS — Corrupt.** *Immediate:* the godling *withers* — the region's force runs to subtraction, spawns rot, the Pale Court *warms* (a death-godling is an *asset*). *Long-tail:* seeds toward the **Iron Throne / Plaguelord Dead-God** (Reliquary-Frame "Stillwater" DG-023, or Engine of the Long Goodbye DG-005) — your eventual wall *revive-locks* and *retires the wounded.* The region "rots-wild": soul-drain mist, grave-fog. Pairs with a Corrupt Reliquary reading (QT-2) to make the Devourer's staircase steeper.
- **CHAOS — Corrupt/Chaos.** *Immediate:* the godling *unmakes* — the region's rules come loose (a fresh patch of Sunder-style warp); the Revel throws a festival, the Unbound take notice. *Long-tail:* seeds toward the **Devourer / Free Wild Dead-God** (Thunderfool DG-022 for the joyful pole, a hunger-titled entry for the corrupt one) — the wall a future climber faces will *scatter their turns* and *blow bigger the longer it waits.* The region "warps-wild": inverted force-advantage tiles, shifting layout. The first Apotheosis that points at *either* Chaos ending.

**The convergence that *is* the spine:** *all four poles converge on the same engine event* — **the Succession goes live in your save.** No matter which pole, Vael's notarization is the same ("it stopped being yours at the threshold; they always do"), and Thessaly's congratulation-that-is-a-condolence is the same. The *branch* is which Dead-God *kind* you seed; the *convergence* is that **you have now started becoming the wall.** This is the quest where the game's thesis (every triumph is a small horror) stops being told and starts being *felt* — you watch a thing you built become a god and forget you, and the High Table opens your Reliquary file *in front of you.*

**Cross-act echo:** the godling you make here **returns in Q3.3 "The Throne-Turned"** (the Pantheon seizes it and turns it against you) and again in **Q4.3 "What You Built"** (it fights beside you / as a boss / its core in your drawer, by its Q3.3 fate). First Light is the *first* domino in a three-act chain that ends at the Empty Seat — the single most *consequential* mid-game choice for the endgame's emotional accounting.

## QT-4 · Q3.1 "First Blood on the Thrones" — *the first deicide & domain-claim (Act 3)*

**Stakes.** You do the unthinkable the cosmology has been building toward (PART ONE §4): you **kill an Olympian** — a god who was once a nobody exactly like you — and *take its office*, inheriting a fraction of its force and its region's allegiance. The deicide-promotion mechanism, fired by your hand. This is where "the climb stops being a sport and becomes a war you started by being good at a game."

**Branch map — two axes cross here (which god × how you kill):**
```
FIRST BLOOD (defeat a chosen Olympian; claim its domain)
│  WHICH GOD (Order⇄Chaos):
│  ├─ kill a CHAOS god (Ares/Dionysus/Hermes) → seat LAW in its region [Order]
│  └─ kill a LAWFUL god (Athena/Zeus/Apollo) → FREE its region [Chaos]
│  HOW (Purity⇄Corruption):
│  ├─ CLEAN kill (no harvest) → region HEALS into its new state [Pure]
│  └─ HARVEST the god-organ → region ROTS; best Lab core in the game [Corrupt]
└─ (the two axes combine into four flavors of the same deicide)
```

- **The Order axis (which god you choose):** an **Order** player hunts the *chaotic* gods (Ares=Chaos/Thanatos, Dionysus=Chaos/Eros, Hermes=Ouranos/Chaos) and *seats law* in the vacuum — deicide as "correcting a filing error with extreme prejudice" (the Concord's framing). A **Chaos** player hunts the *lawful* gods (Athena=Cosmos/Ouranos, Zeus=Ouranos/Cosmos, Apollo=Ouranos/Eros) and *frees* the region — "make the sky a little more honest; the honest sky is on fire" (the Unbound's framing). *The god you kill is a grid-statement about which kind of world you're building.*
- **The Purity axis (how you kill):** a **Pure** deicide is *clean* — no harvest, the region *heals* into its post-god state (deicide as *euthanasia*: the gods are suffering on those thrones, and you're releasing them). A **Corrupt** deicide is a *harvest* — you cut the **god-organ** (the best Lab core in the game), and the region *rots* (deicide as *butchery*). The same kill, two moral registers — and the region *visibly reshapes differently* (heal-wild vs. rot-wild) based on which.
- **The four flavors and their long-tails:**
  - *Order + Pure* (kill a Chaos god cleanly, seat lawful order) → hard route toward **LAWGIVER**; Concord crowns you in Act 4.
  - *Order + Corrupt* (kill a Chaos god, harvest it, seat a *machine* order) → toward **IRON THRONE**; Pale Court + Iron Guild warm.
  - *Chaos + Pure* (kill a lawful god, free its region gently) → toward **FREE WILD**; lights the `UNMAKING` flag if paired with refusing patrons (Q3.2).
  - *Chaos + Corrupt* (kill a lawful god, harvest it, let the region warp-rot) → toward **DEVOURER**; the staircase to the Maw's Mouth (CN-16).

**The `[Ink →]` death-dialogue (the long-tail lore-bomb):** each dying Olympian tells you *how it climbed* and *how you'll fall* (canon: the deepest lore-vein in the act). This is the **cosmology's §4 delivered by its victims** — the god, dying, confirms it was a nobody once and that "whoever's coming for *you*" is too. Every deicide is the Reliquary truth (QT-2) *spoken aloud by the corpse*, and it maps the god to its faction's grief-response (kill a god, and its faction either martyrs it against you or, if it hated that god, *thanks* you).

**Convergence/divergence:** this quest **doesn't converge** — it's the act where "the grid is mostly locked; the act is about *committing.*" Each flavor digs your coordinate's gravity well deeper. The *only* shared convergence is structural: **every deicide thins the sky toward the threshold that wakes the Primordials** (Q3.4), regardless of flavor. You're all climbing the same staircase to the same peak; you just leave different kinds of corpses on the way up.

## QT-5 · Q4.4 "The Empty Seat" — *THE CHOICE (the three doors; Act 4 climax)*

**Stakes.** *Everything resolves here.* The whole run — every grid tick from "The Mark" (QT-1) forward, every faction rite, every god spared or eaten, every Apotheosis pole — has written **which gods you *can* become**, and the Empty Seat reads it all back and stages the verdict. This is the deepest `[Ink →]` tree in the game: the seat speaks plainly (the funny-grim mask half-off) and offers the three doors. This is where the **9 endings + 2 refusals** are authored in full.

**Branch map — the three doors:**
```
THE EMPTY SEAT (make the run-defining Choice)
├─ ASCEND (the 9) ─────────── SIT. Become your grid-god.
│     │  "The chair eats the person and keeps the power."
│     │  WHICH of the 9 = your locked grid coordinate:
│     │    Order:    Lawgiver (Pure) · Architect (Tainted) · Iron Throne (Corrupt)
│     │    Balanced: Warden (Pure) · Broker (Tainted) · Plaguelord (Corrupt)
│     │    Chaos:    Free Wild (Pure) · Reveler (Tainted) · Devourer (Corrupt)
│     └─ GATE: snapshots champion+pantheon into a DEAD GOD → seeds Act 5 (async-invasion)
├─ THE GOD-MAKER ──────────── REFUSE. Descend. Rule a pantheon of gods YOU built.
│     │  (requires no special flag — refusal is always available; the Hollow Atelier
│     │   is its home; the Architect-creed fully realized, but MORTAL)
│     └─ GATE: mortal-pantheon epilogue; NO Dead-God seed; you persist as a HUMAN power
└─ THE UNMAKING (true/secret) ── if you KILLED the Primordials (Q4.1 + `UNMAKING` flag):
      │  end the Succession itself; walk out; no more gods, ever.
      └─ GATE: TRUE ENDING; the cycle stops; save flagged "the run that ended the cycle"
```

- **ASCEND (the 9) — the door your grid *earned*.** You don't *choose* which of the nine; **your accumulated coordinate chose for you** (the Purity⇄Corruption axis sets the *flavor*, Order⇄Chaos sets the *shape* — canon). A Concord-Sworn, Pure, lawful-deicide run arrives with *only the Lawgiver lit*; a corruption-fed, harvest-everything, Devourer-poled run arrives with *only the Devourer.* Most runs light one or two adjacent gods. *Gate:* the chair eats the person; you become a **specific Book05 Dead-God** (Lawgiver→DG-001, Reveler→DG-004 Gardener of Wildfire, Devourer→a hunger-titled entry, etc.) and **seed the async-invasion** — Act 5 begins, and you become the wall. *This is the door the cosmology says is "natural" (the unthrottled window, PART ONE §6) and the door the game has spent five acts showing you is a tragedy.*
- **THE GOD-MAKER — the door of *refusal-by-pride*.** You *refuse the seat*, descend, and **rule a pantheon of gods you *built*** rather than become one (the Hollow Atelier's terminal offer, CN-17). *Gate:* the **mortal-pantheon epilogue** — *no* Dead-God seed; you persist as a **human recurring power**, sending your built gods as the next age's bosses while you, the puppet-master, "outlive every divine thing you made." *The Throne's line nails it:* "You'd rather *own* gods than *be* one. Crueler. More honest. ...a mortal who never let the chair eat him." This is the **Architect's creed taken past the Iron Guild's nerve** — Magna preached it; you *did* it (FE-17/CN-17, her envious note). The loneliest, most *human* ending: you win by staying the nobody.
- **THE UNMAKING — the door of *refusal-by-mercy* (true/secret).** Only available if you **killed the Primordials** (Q4.1 Unmaking path) *and* fed the `UNMAKING` flag across the run (refused all patrons in Q3.2, unmade your godling in Q3.3, laughed at the Reliquary in QT-2, took the Salt Path with the Unbound). *Gate:* the **true ending** — you "take the legs off the chair," end the Succession itself, walk out of a world that will *never make another god.* The cycle stops with you. *The Throne, exhausted:* "It's the kindest thing anyone's ever tried to do here. It's also the loneliest. ...Do it. *Please.* I'm so tired of being a chair." The save is flagged *the run that ended the cycle*; NG+ begins in a world that must make its first new god from scratch.

**The convergence-then-permanent-fork:** Acts 0–3 *converged* every run on this single room (everyone reaches the Empty Seat). Here the river **forks permanently and finally** — ASCEND seeds Act 5 (you become a wall); GOD-MAKER routes to a mortal-power epilogue (you become a forge); UNMAKING ends the cycle (you become a silence). The three doors are the cosmology's three answers to the doors-being-open (PART ONE §6): *join the institution* (ASCEND), *build a rival institution* (GOD-MAKER), or *demolish the institution* (UNMAKING). **Every flag from QT-1 onward is cashed here** — this is the quest the whole run was secretly always about.

**The `[Ink →]` verdict-tree:** the seat reads back your *entire* grid history and stages all nine ascended selves + the two refusals, each with its own crowning epilogue. The cruelty (canon, on-tone): the game "shows you both [who you meant to be and who you turned out to be] and does not let you pretend they're the same." A player who *meant* to be a Lawgiver but harvested too many gods arrives to find the *Iron Throne* lit instead — the verdict is the gap between intent and accumulation, made into your god.

## QT-6 · Q5.3 "The Wall's Choice" — *the inversion fork (how the loop re-converges; Act 5)*

**Stakes.** The signature spine pays out *fully* and *inverts.* If you ASCENDED, you are now the **Dead God** — the wall the whole game taught you to break, manned by *you* — and a new nobody is climbing toward you. The grid stops measuring *your* morality and starts measuring **what kind of obstacle you choose to be.** This is where the loop either *re-converges* (the cycle rolls on, your name carved one layer deeper) or *forks open* (you break your own link). It's the funny-grim heart fully open: *you are the horror now.*

**Branch map (set after Q5.2, where you fought the first invader as the Dead God):**
```
THE WALL'S CHOICE (climber bloodied at your feet, or you unseated)
├─ CRUSH (Corrupt/Order) ──── kill the climber; the seat stays cold
│     └─ terminal state: ETERNAL WALL — NG+ keeps invading you; the loop rolls on,
│        your name carved deeper; the next nobody already enters Threshold under your shadow
├─ STEP ASIDE (Pure/Chaos) ── let them past; become the Dead God who ABDICATES
│     └─ terminal state: ABDICATED — your Dead God becomes a one-time secret ALLY
│        for future climbers; the chain continues, gentler
└─ BREAK THE CHAIN (Unmaking echo) ── shatter your own petrification; end YOUR link
      └─ terminal state: CHAIN BROKEN — your link removed from the cycle;
         a late, costly, partial Unmaking from the DEFENDER'S side; unique NG+ flag
```

- **CRUSH — Corrupt/Order (enforce the loop).** *You kill the climber.* The seat stays yours; the age keeps your name; the Succession rolls on. *The mechanic:* terminal state **Eternal Wall** — your save's Dead God stays live in the invasion pool, NG+ keeps sending climbers, you keep being the boss. *The grid read:* a *Corrupt* wall crushes to *feed* (the seat is hungry); an *Order* wall crushes to *enforce the rules* (only the worthy pass, and this one wasn't). *The image:* "Somewhere a *new* nobody's catching a rat in an undercroft, looking up at me without knowing it's me. ...It's the only company a wall ever gets." This is the loop **re-converging** — the cosmology's §6 inevitability, made into your choice to *uphold* it.
- **STEP ASIDE — Pure/Chaos (subvert the loop gently).** *You let them past.* You become the rare Dead God who *abdicates*; they ascend, you go quiet. *The mechanic:* terminal state **Abdicated** — your Dead God becomes a *one-time secret ally* for future climbers (a wall that, once, chose to help). *The grid read:* a *Pure* wall steps aside to *protect* the next climber from the chair's hunger (mercy from the other end); a *Chaos* wall steps aside to *break the rule* that walls must crush. *The image:* "Be a kinder god than I was, if the chair lets you. It won't. But *try.* That's the most a wall can give the thing that climbs it: a single, useless, *try.*" The loop continues but *bends* — the first crack of warmth in the cycle.
- **BREAK THE CHAIN — Unmaking echo (a personal exit from the loop).** *You shatter your own petrification.* You cease to be a wall — a late, costly, *partial* Unmaking from the defender's side (the only echo of the true ending available to an Ascender). *The mechanic:* terminal state **Chain Broken** — your link is removed from the cycle; a unique NG+ flag; "one less wall, one less tragedy with a name on it." *The grid read:* this is the *Free Wild / Unmaking instinct* surfacing *after* you already took a throne — the realization, too late and yet not, that you don't have to be the next headstone someone climbs over. *The image:* "It's not the Unmaking. It's just *me,* leaving the board on purpose. ...Strange. It feels like the first free thing I've done since the undercroft." The loop **forks open** for *your* link specifically — the cosmology's §6 inevitability, refused at the last possible moment by the very wall it made.

**The re-convergence (the loop made explicit — Q5.4):** all three terminal states feed the game's final image and the **NG+ bridge text** — *about you.* Eternal Wall → a new nobody walks into Threshold under your dead-god shadow (the loop rolls on); Abdicated → your Dead God recurs as a *secret ally* (the loop, gentler); Chain Broken → NG+ begins with your link *missing* (the loop, frayed). And the GOD-MAKER and UNMAKING runs (which skipped the Act-5 invasion) get their own codas — the mortal-power who outlives his gods, the silence of a world that finally lets people stay people. **Every ending re-converges on the same closing sentence: a graveyard of winners, a fresh plot, and someone walking toward it with the old knack** — except the Unmaking, where no one is walking, and the silence is the answer.

**The cross-run payoff (the spine, complete):** the branch you choose here writes the **Reliquary entry a future player reads in *their* Act 1** (the `[Ink →]` chain-state tree). The loop QT-2 told you about (you'll speedrun someone's tragedy; someone will speedrun yours) is now *literally implemented*: your QT-6 choice is the *first clause of the next nobody's QT-1*. Apotheosis → the Succession, paid in full, with you as both the climber who started and the wall that finishes — and the choice of *what kind of wall* is the last, strangest expression of every grid-tick you set since "The Mark."
